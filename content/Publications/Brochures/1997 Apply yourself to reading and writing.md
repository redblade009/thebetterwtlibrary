# FOR THE TEACHER

It is vital to motivate the student. From the first session, the teacher should show the benefits of being able to read. Tell the students that if they apply themselves, they will be able to read the Bible, read the words to songs from our songbook, read stories to their children, participate more in Christian meetings, and generally improve their lives.

Tell them that they are not expected to read all the writing in the boxes. The small writing is for the teacher.

Be flexible; adjust the material in this brochure to the level of your student. This brochure can be used in teaching a class of students or individual students. If a blackboard is available, it is fine to use it; otherwise, the student can learn directly from this publication.

To make progress, the student should receive instruction for several hours each week. Homework should be assigned regularly.

Each student should have a copy of this brochure. The student will also need paper and a pen or a pencil.

Be positive, patient, encouraging, and always generous with commendation.

For more information, see pages 62-4.

## TEACHING ADULTS TO READ

Many of those who enroll in our literacy classes are adults. While the following guidelines apply to all learners, they are particularly important in teaching adults.

1. Do not be overly demanding, and do not overwhelm the student with too much material in one session. This can cause the student to become discouraged and to stop attending classes.

2. Be consistently encouraging and positive. Reading skills are developed in small, sequential steps. Students should find satisfaction in their progress.

3. Encourage the students to apply what they are learning as quickly as possible. Teachers can help them to read answers or scriptures at Christian meetings or to read a scripture in the field ministry. This will powerfully show the practical value of the literacy class.

4. Do not waste time on side issues. Adults are busy people. Make the most of classroom sessions to teach essentials.

5. Always be respectful of the students, according them the dignity they deserve. Do not embarrass or belittle them.

6. Ideally, students should do several hours of homework for every hour of formal instruction. The teacher may invite others to assist students with their homework.

7. Be alert to individual problems. Some students may be unable to read small print because they need glasses. Others may be hard of hearing.

# TEACHING ADULTS TO WRITE

1. Students should learn the manuscript alphabet before attempting cursive writing. Manuscript writing is easier to learn and easier to do. Also the letters more closely resemble the letters in this and other literature.
2. You can find a chart of both alphabets on the outside cover of this brochure. In learning to write letters of the alphabet, students can place a piece of paper over the letter they are practicing and then trace it. They should trace the letter several times before trying to copy it without tracing.
3. Progress at reading is often faster than progress at writing. Do not delay new reading lessons if the students are not able to perform the writing homework. On the other hand, remember that new letters are learned more easily if the student practices writing them.
4. Although adult students may be able to perform intricate tasks with their hands, writing with pen or pencil may be a difficult and frustrating experience. Do not insist on perfectly formed letters.

Just as in the Theocratic Ministry School, both baptized and unbaptized publishers may enroll in literacy classes.

If the elders decide it is necessary to create one or more classes for reading and writing, they should assign a capable person to conduct them. The elders need not feel obliged to do this extra work themselves, unless they have the time to do so or there is no one else to take on this responsibility. The elders have much to do in directly caring for the spiritual interests of the congregation.

Normally brothers should assume the responsibility of teaching. However, if no brother is able to conduct the class, a capable sister may be assigned to do so. If it is necessary to create several classes and there are no capable brothers to look after them, the elders might ask a sister to conduct a class of sisters and/or a class of unbaptized boys and girls. Since the sister replaces a brother in this teaching capacity, she should wear a head covering.

It would be beneficial, where possible, if the classes could function two or three times a week so that the students can make rapid progress. Between lessons, the student will be assigned homework, which the instructor will correct at the beginning of the next lesson.

Lessons should be held at a time convenient to the majority of the students. Some divide the classes into small groups and give lessons before the Congregation Book Study. Others separate the classes and teach during the Theocratic Ministry School. The time and place of congregation literacy classes is decided by the body of elders in the congregation.
