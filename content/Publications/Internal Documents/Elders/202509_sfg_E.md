## CONGREGATION SERVICE COMMITTEE

10. The Congregation Service Committee consists of the coordinator of the body of elders, the secretary, and the service overseer. Its decisions should harmonize with the thinking of the body of elders. The service committee uses good judgment in determining when it would be wise to confer with the other elders. (Prov. 15:22) The coordinator of the body of elders serves as the chairman of the meetings of the service committee. If a member of the service committee is absent, another elder may substitute. ## COORDINATOR OF THE BODY OF ELDERS

11. The circuit overseer appoints the coordinator of the body of elders. The body of elders may assign one or two other elders to assist him. The coordinator of the body of elders must be less than 80 years of age. He should be a good organizer and diligently care for his responsibilities. (Rom. 12:8) He is not the coordinator of the congregation. He should maintain a modest view of his assignment, humbly appreciating his fellow elders and welcoming their advice.—Prov. 15:22; Matt. 23:8.

# SECRETARY

13. The body of elders determines who will serve as the secretary. The body of elders may assign another elder to assist him. The secretary should have good organizational ability and should not be a procrastinator. (Rom. 12:11) He should have the ability to write in a way that is clear and understandable.

14. The secretary oversees the work of the accounts servant and convention matters. (See _Instructions for Congregation Accounting_ \[S-27].) He ensures that any legal and financial responsibilities the congregation has are handled in a timely manner. If necessary, the body of elders may assign a qualified ministerial servant to assist with tasks such as the compiling, posting, and submitting of the congregation's field service activity to the branch office. For a list of responsibilities handled by the secretary, see 'Secretary' in the Index.

# SERVICE OVERSEER

15. The body of elders determines who will serve as the service overseer. The body of elders may assign another elder to assist him. The service overseer should take a keen interest in the publishers' share in the ministry and their effectiveness. He should be enthusiastic about the ministry and motivate others to have a full share. (Rom 12:11) He should be skillful in various aspects of the work, capable of training others, and alert to do so.

16. The service overseer oversees the work of the literature and territory servants. This includes ensuring that those who are deaf, blind, or have low vision are able to request literature in their preferred format. (See _Literature Request and Inventory Guidelines_ \[S-56].) He organizes witnessing on holidays and during special campaigns.

17. He visits all field service groups at least once each year. During the visit, he conducts the meeting for field service and works with the group in the ministry. He also discusses with the group overseer and assistant how each publisher is doing in the field ministry and the effectiveness of the group's field service arrangements. For a list of responsibilities handled by the service overseer, see 'Service overseer' in the Index.

## FIELD SERVICE GROUP OVERSEER

18. The body of elders determines the number of field service groups and who will serve as group overseers and assistants. (See 1:21.) The Congregation Service Committee assigns publishers to field service groups. Group overseers and assistants should be alert, caring shepherds who strive to maintain regular contact with those in their group and who are zealous in the field ministry. (Prov. 27:23; Isa. 32:2) The other elders and ministerial servants in the group should be alert to assist them in caring for their responsibilities. Whether serving as group overseers or not, all elders should sense their personal responsibility to shepherd the congregation.—Eph. 4:16; od chap. 5 pars. 29-34.

19. The group overseer is responsible for the following (see also “Field service groups” in the Index):

(1) Taking the lead when the group meets for field service, especially on weekends. Periodically, he may assign his assistant or another brother to conduct such meetings. If he cannot be present, he makes sure that the group will be cared for by his assistant or another brother.—See 3:3.
(2) Arranging to work regularly in the ministry with each publisher in the group to provide encouragement and training in various aspects of the Kingdom-preaching and teaching work.—Luke 8:1; see 3:1.
(3) Reviewing periodically with his assistant the activity of those in his group to determine their strengths and weaknesses in the ministry.
(4) Giving ongoing attention to newly baptized publishers.—See 2:8.
(5) Assisting and training exemplary brothers in the group to reach out and qualify for congregation responsibilities.
(6) Helping to collect the monthly field service reports. When the secretary informs him of missing reports, he immediately follows through. If someone did not participate in field service for an entire month, he offers appropriate assistance.

1. The primary responsibility of elders is to “shepherd the flock of God” under their care. (1 Pet. 5:2, 3; John 21:15-17) The following Bible principles can help elders fulfill this responsibility:

(1) Elders should take a loving and active interest in the spiritual, emotional, and physical needs of others. (Prov. 27:23; Jas. 1:27; 2:15, 16) This requires that elders maintain regular contact with each family in the congregation. (Acts 20:28) However, elders should not meddle in personal matters.—1 Thess. 4:11.

(2) Whether he serves as a group overseer or not, each elder should sense his personal responsibility to shepherd the congregation.—Eph. 4:16.

(3) The objective of shepherding is to impart a spiritual gift that is faith-strengthening and to provide needed commendation and encouragement. (Rom. 1:11, 12) This can be done by giving well-prepared Scriptural talks, by conversing with publishers before and after the meetings and while sharing in the ministry, and by making shepherding calls.

(4) As shepherds, elders are responsible for giving counsel when it is needed. Reviewing the February 2022 issue of _The Watchtower_, pages 14-19, can help elders give counsel in an appealing way.—Prov. 27:9.

# SHEPHERDING CALLS

2. Elders and qualified ministerial servants who join the elders in making shepherding calls should keep the following points in mind:

(1) **Determine Whom to Take Along:** If you expect to discuss a confidential or serious matter, two elders should make the visit. Otherwise, a qualified ministerial servant may join an elder, with the elder taking the lead.—See 1:21.
(2) **Make an Appointment:** If there is a serious problem to be discussed, use good judgment in deciding whether the publisher should be informed of this before the visit.
(3) **Prepare:** Prayerfully consider the needs and circumstances of the individual or family. What Scriptural encouragement and information in the publications will be most beneficial? (Heb. 12:12, 13) When making the visit, be flexible, since the need may not be what you had anticipated.
(4) **Duration of the Call:** Keep to the agreed time. If necessary, another visit can be arranged.—Eccl. 3:1; Matt. 5:37.
(5) **Pray With the Individual or Family:** In your prayer, mention them by name and any adversity or trial they may be dealing with.—Phil. 4:6, 7; Col. 4:12.

3. Elders can help ministerial servants to develop the qualities of a shepherd by taking them along on shepherding calls when appropriate. (1 Tim. 3:1) Before making the shepherding call, the elder should discuss with the ministerial servant how he hopes to handle the call. He could ask the ministerial servant to prepare an encouraging Scriptural point or a faith-strengthening experience that would fit the needs of the individual or family. He could also ask the ministerial servant to conclude the visit with prayer. After the visit, he should review with the ministerial servant how the visit went, providing commendation and suggestions as needed.

# ASSISTING THOSE WITH MARITAL PROBLEMS

4. If both mates are Christians, it is usually best to meet with them together. However, if only one is present, the elders should discuss only what that one can do to improve the situation. Because elders cannot know everything that occurs in a marriage, they should avoid taking sides.—Prov. 18:13.

5. If a Christian is contemplating separation or divorce, the elders should direct his attention to the Scriptures and Christian publications. (1 Cor. 7:10, 11; _w18.12_ pp. 10-14; _lff_ endnote 4) The elders should explain that a legal divorce of itself does not free an individual to remarry. (Matt. 19:9) Elders should not encourage separation or divorce; neither should they forbid such. These are personal matters. (Gal. 6:7) However, a Christian's decisions in this area may disqualify him from being viewed as exemplary.—See 1:4, 20; 5:5.

# ASSISTING THOSE STRUGGLING TO BREAK FREE FROM VIEWING PORNOGRAPHY

6. Elders need to provide ongoing shepherding to a Christian who is struggling to break free from viewing pornography. The frequency and nature of the shepherding visits may depend on the extent of the person's involvement with pornography in the past. (See A:15.) Every effort should be made to help the Christian establish a daily routine of prayer, personal study, and wholesome meditation. (Phil. 4:8) When dealing with a married person, the elders should encourage him to reveal the matter to his wife. If the wife is a believer, the elders should provide spiritual assistance and comfort to her. While we refer to the husband as the one viewing pornography, the same direction would apply if the wife is the one viewing pornography.

## ASSISTING VICTIMS OF CHILD ABUSE

16. Ongoing spiritual shepherding is especially important for victims of child sexual abuse and their families. (Isa. 32:1, 2; Eph. 4:32) The following Bible principles can help elders assist victims of child abuse:

(1) Listen patiently and empathetically. (1 Cor. 13:4) Do not interrupt to give advice or to correct the person's thinking.
(2) "Speak consolingly" from God's Word. (1 Thess. 5:14; Prov. 12:25) Although asking a few tactful questions may help the victim to express herself, avoid probing unnecessarily or repeatedly into the details of the abuse.
