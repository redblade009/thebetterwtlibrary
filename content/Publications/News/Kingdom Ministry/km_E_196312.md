## Never Fail Our God in This Time of the End

¹ With the fast-approaching end of this system of things facing us, there are compelling reasons for never failing our God, who has commanded that the good news be preached in this time of the end. For others to benefit fully from the good news, we know that they need God's Word in understandable form. Certainly we have that in the New World Translation, the English edition, which we are offering for \$1, and the Christian Greek Scriptures, available in Spanish, French, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese and German, which we may offer for 50c, along with a booklet.

² Now that we have the New World Translation in these many languages, we eagerly want to make the most of our opportunities. Are there people who read Spanish, Italian, German or one of the other languages in our territory? Then we should have on hand the Bible in their languages. Jehovah has blessed us with these translations; let us use them. Past experience has shown that it is not difficult to place the Bible, especially if we give the householder an example of the clear rendering of the New World Translation by using the leaflet "Renderings of Scriptures Compared." Placements go up tremendously when we offer the Bible at every appropriate opportunity, to fellow employees, neighbors, salespeople, yes, to whomever we meet.

³ If we use a deluxe edition of the Bible in giving our sermon at the door, some householders might expect a similar copy for $1; so we may find it advantageous to use the regular edition when demonstrating the Bible. On the other hand, some persons may want the deluxe edition for $2.50. When you meet people who have the New World Translation, offer them a copy of the Babylon book for 75c. Whether placing a Bible or a Babylon book, we will leave them a booklet and try to start a study in it.

## ARE YOU ANSWERING THE CALL TO PIONEER?

This supplement should be kept because each of the five parts will be considered separately on the service meeting during the months of December 1963 through April 1964. It will be conducted by an enthusiastic brother, one qualified to handle this material. It will be conducted like a Watchtower study, with each paragraph being read after considering the questions on the paragraph. Study the material and come prepared for a lively, informative discussion.

¹ During the 1963 service year there were, on an average, 280,052 publishers preaching the Kingdom message every month in the United States. This was 12,616 more ministers in the field every month than during the previous year. Truly, as Jesus said, "the harvest is great." As great as it is—and it is marvelous in our eyes—there is a vast multitude not being reached regularly with the message. In the United States there are still some counties that were last worked before 1958, and 782 counties still not assigned to congregations. Some congregations cover their territory once a year or less and many other congregations have territory that needs more regular coverage. In addition to this, many congregations need mature servant leadership. Are you seriously considering answering the appeal, "Step over into Macedonia and help us?"—Matt. 9:37; Acts 16:9.

² In view of these needs the Society has regularly sent out a call for more pioneers. As you consider the above facts you will see that they point to the great work that needs to be done before Armageddon strikes in execution of divine judgment. Many more persons are to be reached with the message so they may take flight to safety. Does your desire to reach these people move you to respond to the call to pioneer? In Jesus' day a similar situa-

tion existed. "On seeing the crowds he felt pity for them, because they were skinned and thrown about like sheep without a shepherd." To be sure, there are many persons in the world, the field, that are "skinned and thrown about" and need shepherds to guide them. We too feel tender affection for them and see the need for more full-time "workers," more pioneers.—Matt. 9:36.

³ The call is so urgent that Jesus said: "Beg the Master of the harvest to send out workers into his harvest." (Matt. 9:38) The Master is thus implored to stimulate the desire in the hearts of many to respond to the plea and become "workers" in the harvest. By the term "workers" is meant persons who would be so concerned that they would be anxious to pitch in to get the harvest work done before unfavorable weather sets in. Each day that goes by, the time left to accomplish this work is reduced. Energetic persons with a vision of the need are required to be at work "urgently," as Paul admonished at 2 Timothy 4:2. The tremendous task at hand demands willing workers!

⁴ Individually, then, we have a responsibility to examine ourselves. Are you holding back from the pioneer service when you could serve in this capacity? Have you personally analyzed your circumstances? If you are in position to pioneer, what is preventing you from answering the call? It may be necessary for you to put up a hard fight for the faith in order to overcome the materialistic philosophy of life peculiar to this generation, which philosophy prevents some from being spiritually-minded.

⁵ Unquestionably, there are many in the New World society who can and should give more serious consideration to the call to pioneer. To

these we say: Do not delay! Take positive action toward answering the call for more pioneers. Do not take the negative approach and say, 'One does not have to pioneer to get into God's new order.' Actually, each person is judged on the basis of what he can do, and Jehovah expects one to increase or give more attention to the Kingdom interests to the extent that he can. (For further information on this please see the Kingdom Ministry supplement for December, 1962.) So why rationalize and miss out on the joys, privileges and responsibilities that can be yours if you are in position to pioneer? We urge you to give this matter thorough consideration.

⁶ Those of you who cannot become regular pioneers, make wise use of your time and energies. Jehovah is aware of what you are able to do. Perhaps you cannot respond to the call to become a regular pioneer now, but you can enjoy the vacation pioneer service. Whatever your circumstances, keep in mind the importance and urgency of preaching. The dedicated Christian will endeavor to share fully in the harvesting work. The call is urgent. Will you be one of these willing workers who can and will increase his privileges of service by responding to the Master's call? Happy are those who respond to the call to pioneer.—Ezek. 3:17; 1 Cor. 9:16.

# New Theocratic Ministry School Schedule

¹ A few adjustments in the theocratic ministry school schedule for 1964 are planned to give us all a more active part. Each week all of us will be assigned a portion of the Bible to read. We will do this at home. In due time we will read the entire Bible. Our home reading assignments are scheduled in the same Bible book that will be discussed that week in the school. We will concentrate more direct attention on the Bible itself through the use of our thrilling new book "All Scripture Is Inspired of God and Beneficial" (si).

² Each school session will be a symposium of five talks or demonstrations, all of them usually being based on the same Bible book. The first is the instruction talk. It will cover material designed to give us better background knowledge of the Bible book itself. The speaker should select a subject theme around which to develop his talk. He should amplify the material as necessary, doing any required research in the Bible and the Society's publications. He should concentrate particularly on material not assigned to students or, at the most, treat such portions in a more general way, leaving details to the student.

³ The first of the four student talks, or talk number two, will generally be simply a narration of some outstanding portion of the Bible book. The objective is to give the Bible account, not to explain its meaning or apply the principles embodied in it. This talk will replace the reading assignment.

⁴ The other student talks, numbers 3 to 5, will usually be more than mere summations of the assigned material. They will be explanatory or interpretive talks, making application of the scriptures to show how and why they are beneficial. Not all the portion assigned need be read or discussed. Whenever possible we will use those key texts that bear most directly on the subject theme assigned. The rest of the scripture assignment is given primarily for background so that the scriptures discussed will not be taken out of context. Neither should the talk be simply a discussion of different isolated texts from the assignment. We should get the central theme in mind and tie all parts of the talk together. In some cases the verses covered by two student talks will be the same, but, as indicated by the assigned themes, the talks will be given from completely different viewpoints.

⁵ In especially the last three student talks we want to have in

mind the particularly beneficial nature of the material assigned. Try to develop settings that will highlight the profitable use of these texts in everyday activity. This will be of more value to the rest of the school, since it will demonstrate application of the texts and how they can be used in a practical way.

⁶ The written review is scheduled to follow a completed study of a portion of the Bible rather than at regular intervals as heretofore. To prepare, complete your assigned home Bible reading and review the questions in the book "All Scripture Is Inspired of God and Beneficial." Material from both will be covered by the questions but only the Bible may be used while you are taking the review.

⁷ The new schedule should allow the school servant a minute or so to introduce the session effectively and perhaps another minute or so to conclude with a brief sum-up or to whet the appetites of all for the following week.

⁸ We will want to encourage all interested persons to join with us in this inspiring consideration of God's Word. Since our home Bible reading assignment is a vital new provision of the school, let us diligently keep up with our assignments, reading our Bible daily, rejoicing in the blessings we will receive.
