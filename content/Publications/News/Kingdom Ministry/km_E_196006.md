## Tend Our Shepherd's Flock Skillfully

¹ All dedicated Christians have the responsibility of finding and feeding the sheep of our Shepherd's flock. How we want to do this work skillfully! All publishers keenly desire to develop skill in teaching and preaching.

² As an aid to finding and feeding the sheep we have the Paradise book and the new sermon. What joy was ours when we received our first copy of the Paradise book! Remembering, then, our joyful enthusiasm, let us, in the same spirit, bring this Bible-study aid to all sheeplike persons in our territory.

³ What happy results from using the new sermon! A circuit servant writes: "I'm quite impressed with the results of placing the book in the people's hands early in the sermon. On one occasion I placed four books in five efforts and started studies at two of them." Another brother writes: "I feel the success with it is perhaps more effective than any sermon I have used heretofore. I gave the sermon not over 18 times and placed nine books and made arrangements to deliver three additional books." Another reports: "I found using the sermon as the Society suggested brought very good results. Placing the book in their hands was very effective. It captured their attention and held it. In one and a half hours I gave the sermon seven times, placed four books and had orders for two more. The sermon was easy to give; it has simplicity and yet it is practical, holds the attention and puts the point across regarding God's purpose for man."

⁴ With so many persons taking the Paradise book, how great is the number of potential Bible studies! How, then, can we show skillfulness in our ministry, that these placements may be turned into Bible studies? Keep an accurate house-to-house record. Never neglect to record a placement of the book. Follow through on not-at-homes. Reports

one circuit servant: "The service group during the week was in one territory calling on those who were not at home when we covered the entire territory. In addition to fifteen books being placed when covering it the first time, sixteen additional ones were placed when the not-at-homes were called on."

⁵ Since 3,250,000 copies of the Paradise book have been printed, we may encounter persons who already have the book. When the householder says, "I have that book," do you cope with the situation skillfully? Alertly take advantage of the opportunity to start a study, if one is not already being conducted. The simplified back-call sermon, described in "Your Service Meetings," is a great aid in starting studies. Use it not only on back-calls but also when you meet someone who already has the book.

⁶ Tactfulness is an evidence of our ministerial skill. Some householders

do not like to feel obligated; thus some potential Bible studies may be lost on the first call if too much effort is made to get a definite commitment for a regular study. If this is a problem with you, try this: Merely make a definite arrangement for the next visit. Without feeling obligated, the householder will be having a regular study.

⁷ Showing the skillfulness of undershepherds in tending to our Shepherd's flock is the fine increase in Bible studies. All publishers have done commendably. Consider the congregation publishers: In 1954 the average Bible study per publisher was .4. In 1955, 1956, 1957, it rose to .5. In 1958, it increased to .6; last year it rose to .7. Pioneers have progressed from 3.9 to 5.1, and special pioneers from 6.1 to 8.9 during the same period of time. May 1960 bring further progress toward our goal of one Bible study per congregation publisher!

## Teach New Ones How to Study

¹ Progress to maturity is directly affected by one's diligence in personal study. For that reason we should encourage those with whom we study to prepare their lesson in advance right from the start. Show them that the sincerity of their prayer to God for knowledge of his purposes is demonstrated by their earnestness in studying his Word.

² In most cases it is necessary to do more than encourage new ones to study; we must teach them how to do it. Show them how you mark your answers, selecting only key words and phrases. Stress the importance of looking up all scriptures cited and ascertaining their relation to what is in the paragraph. Encourage them to reason on the material in the lesson, seeking reasons for the statements made. Their understanding will be enlarged if they learn to think about the relationship of new points learned to truths already known. At the end of any study period they will find it helpful to review.

³ Their progress will be stimulated when they fully appreciate the reason for study. Point out that the acquiring of knowledge is not the only goal toward which they are striving, but that they should have in mind that Christians should be 'ready always to make a defense before everyone that demands a reason for the hope in them.' (1 Pet. 3:15) They should also be encouraged to analyze the material studied in relation to their own lives, asking themselves: What bearing might this have on personal decisions I might have to make? What does it call on me to do now?

⁴ When new ones ask Bible questions, alert publishers often seize this opportunity to help them learn how to study. Instead of simply giving the answer, they may take time to help the new one to find the answer. In this way they teach him how to use a concordance and the indexes in the Society's publications, and the benefit of the library at the Kingdom Hall.

⁵ It is important to have a regular schedule for personal study and then to stick to it. At first their schedule may involve simply setting aside a specific time to prepare for the home Bible study. Progressively encourage them to expand that program.

⁶ Never forget, too, that we need Jehovah's blessing when we study, and we should pray for it. Point this out to new ones. It will strengthen their heart appreciation of the truth and call forth Jehovah's blessing upon their efforts.

THE GOOD NEWS

## Preparation Needed to Teach from House to House

¹ When a new sermon is presented in _Kingdom Ministry_, each publisher should study it. Then at the service meeting the demonstration will help him to get a better grasp of it.

² After this, final preparation through careful consideration of the sermon should be made so that he thoroughly understands the theme and makes the sermon his own, something he can say clearly, sincerely and enthusiastically, at the same time making the sermon most appealing to the people. Their religion, way of life, objections commonly made, local conditions, seasons, and so forth, are factors demanding attention.

³ An outline helpful to preparation is: (1) Prepare to introduce the message in a manner fitted to the householder and leading to the first text. (2) On each scripture prepare three things: (a) lead up to the

text; (b) read it with force and meaning, emphasizing the theme; (c) apply the text by simple explanation or illustration. (3) Prepare, at definite places in the sermon, a question or remark to draw the householder into conversation. (4) Prepare what you will say to place the book and to start a Bible study.

⁴ As you approach each succeeding text, prepare definitely what to say so that the listener will be waiting, his expectation excited to hear how the next scripture answers a question raised in his mind. For example, after discussing Isaiah 55:11, you might say: "But the questions come up, How can it be possible? and, Who will be able to enjoy such a paradise? This is cleared up for us here." (Then read Psalm 37:10, 11.)

⁵ Continuing in this manner, complete your preparation for efficient teaching from house to house.

## No Place for Commercialism

It has come to the attention of the Society that some brothers have been taking advantage of their theocratic association to approach brothers and persons of good will to sell their products. As has been brought out in _The Watchtower_, it is entirely improper for congregation servants, pioneers or publishers to sell or make contacts for selling products at the Kingdom Hall, at book studies or at assemblies. Brothers should not use their position as a servant, a pioneer or as a publisher to call upon other brothers or persons of good will to sell their products. If a person wishes to engage in selling for a living, it is his personal business, but never should he misuse theocratic contacts and friendship as an outlet for the products he sells. This announcement is to answer the many inquiries received and to put all brothers on guard against these who would inject commercialism into the theocratic organization.
