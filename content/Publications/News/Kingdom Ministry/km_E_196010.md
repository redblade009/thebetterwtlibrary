# CONGREGATION ORGANIZATION

## Improving Service Meetings (Part 3)

¹ Short, well-rounded-out talks on the service meeting add variety and weight to the program. To be effective, each should have a Scriptural theme and should present reasonable and workable suggestions and admonition.

² If the points in a service meeting talk are outlined by the Society, the speaker should build his talk around these and tie the talk in with the theme of the meeting. If the talk is not outlined by the Society, the one assigned should first get clearly in mind the reason for the talk and the effect it should have on the congregation. If it is to deal with a congregation problem, ascertain the cause of the problem and how it can be overcome. Scriptures supporting the theme or covering the problem can be applied. The talk should not be abstract, but each publisher should see how he can apply the practical counsel in his own ministry.

³ A talk is made doubly effective with appropriate illustrations. If they are used to explain and clarify, the counsel and suggestions given will be remembered longer and applied. All this requires preparation. The one assigned cannot wait until the last minute to put a few thoughts on a piece of paper. The talk must be thought out thoroughly. He must observe the law of proportion, keeping in mind the amount of time allotted. The introduction should not be lengthy, but should lead right into the body. If the talk is to be

fifteen minutes in length, then prepare only fourteen minutes, just in case elaboration on one or two points takes a little more time. Taking more than the time assigned is unfair to others on the program as well as those in attendance.

⁴ In introducing the service meeting, the congregation servant need not handle the welcome and consideration of the text at every service meeting, but capable, enthusiastic brothers should always handle this in order to get the meeting started off right. Each brother handling succeeding parts should introduce the brother who follows him. There are a variety of ways this can be done. Avoid overuse of the phrase, "Now we will hear from," or, "We will turn the platform over to," and so forth. Use fifteen seconds to state very briefly what feature follows and introduce the brother by name. Each one should emphasize the theme. This will tie the whole meeting together.

⁵ Concluding announcements should be well thought out by the congregation servant, and it is usually preferable that he make these at each service meeting. Time is limited, so he should make the time count. Occasionally there are letters to be read or special arrangements to be discussed, not included in the outline, but careful planning will avoid going overtime if you adjust the program before the meeting starts. Talks on the service meeting, whether a few minutes or longer, should all be pointed and practical.

## Teaching at the Theocratic Ministry School

¹ The theocratic ministry school is provided by Jehovah to help us become effective teachers. Taking the lead as a teacher at this meeting is the school servant, who must always strive to help all to develop greater teaching ability.

² The servant should analyze the teaching ability of each student and constantly seek to improve it. When offering suggestions on overcoming weaknesses, he should be sure that the student understands the counsel given and appreciates its value. As opportunity affords, do more; offer private assistance.

³ Counsel should be designed to stimulate constant progress. However, when a student makes some improvement on a major weakness, it is not always advisable to pass on quickly to other weaknesses. This may slow down progress being made by focusing his attention elsewhere. In some cases it may do more good simply to commend the student for solid improvement, emphasize the value of the good quality he is developing and encourage him to continue to give it attention. The student will get a sense of achievement as he overcomes this weakness. If weaknesses are always highlighted no matter how well he does, he may get discouraged.

⁴ When counseling, bear in mind the type of talk being delivered. For example, in the first student talk, counsel on qualities and methods employed in talks given from the platform. On the other hand, counsel given on house-to-house sermons and back-call presentations should be designed to develop both speaking ability and the teaching techniques used in these features of the service, as discussed in Kingdom Ministry. Students should be encouraged to deliver these sermons just as they would in the field service. In fact, it would be beneficial for them to use their sermons in the field ministry, either from house to house or on back-calls, before and after giving them in the school. In this way the theocratic ministry school will provide the most practical instruction for the ministry.

⁵ It is good for the school servant to suggest that the students bring along their textbooks to the meeting. It may be helpful to mark important points in the books in preparation for the written review, as well as for use in the service. With both servant and students working to cultivate teaching ability, all can develop greater effectiveness in the ministry.—2 Tim. 2:2.
