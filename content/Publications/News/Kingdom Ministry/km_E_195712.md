# Your Service Meetings

## FIRST MEETING IN DECEMBER

Theme: Tell the Good News from Day to Day.

5 min: Welcome, text, comments.

8 min: Talk on 'Tell the Good News from Day to Day.' (See 'Watchtower,' 12/1/57, p. 719, and 12/15/56, p. 744.)

15 min: Question-and-answer coverage of the main article 'A Harvest for Willing Workers.'

22 min: Demonstration on 'Encourage Persons Studied with to Become Kingdom Ministers.'

CHAIRMAN (3 min.) Discuss two-fold purpose of conducting Bible studies: (1) to aid persons to 'come to an accurate knowledge of truth,' and (2) to aid them to become teachers of the good news. Some persons have studied for many months, but 'in view of the time' are not teachers. What can be done to help them?—1 Tim. 2: 4; Heb. 5: 12.

SCENE I (3 min.) A soliloquy. Bible study servant is examining study slips showing persons with whom studies have been conducted for a long time. One study has been conducted for nearly a year; 'Let God Be True' has been completed and study is well along in 'This Means Everlasting Life'. Still the person has not become a Kingdom minister. Bible study servant mulls on matter, wondering what might be holding back persons like this. As it is his responsibility, he plans to go to publisher and help in starting the new one as a Kingdom praiser in December.

SCENE II (4 min.) Servant contacts publisher. They discuss good-will person's knowledge. Publisher is pleased with present state of knowledge. Bible study servant asks whether publisher has invited good-will person to share in the ministry. Publisher says yes, but person did not respond to casual invitations; publisher continued the study, waiting for the time when the truth will move the person to share in the service. Bible study servant explains that all need encouragement to begin service, and that publisher can explain to potential new witness how he can share: with handbills, by reading a scripture and by sharing in house-to-house magazine service. Publisher sees wisdom of this, but is somewhat hesitant. Bible study servant offers to go along.

SCENE III (8 min.) At end of study Bible study servant comments on a scripture relating to service, perhaps one covered in the study. Tactfully he shows how all should share knowledge with others. He tells of many new ones going in service this month and invites good-will person to share in some feature of Kingdom ministry. Good-will person knows he should and plans to 'sometime.' Bible study servant suggests that the 'sometime' be the coming week. Definite arrangements are made. In answer to question, 'But what shall I say?' servant explains that after a few doors he can give invitations to the public meeting. Later new one may read a scripture and comment on it. House-to-house magazine work is mentioned as simple. Publisher is asked to demonstrate a magazine presentation. Householder

feels he can do that and tries. He is commended.

CHAIRMAN (4 min.) Do not hesitate to invite persons who have studied for a number of months to share in field service. New ones may report time if they say something at the door and share in the witness. Encourage publishers to analyze the conducting of their studies: Are they encouraging persons studied with to become Kingdom ministers?

10 min: Arrange locally. (Include comments on 'Awake!' Results' and text \[Matt. 5: 16] at bottom of page one.)

## THIRD MEETING IN DECEMBER

5 min: Welcome, text, comments.

20 min: Audience participation on 'How New Publishers Can Be Aided to Share in December Activity.'

CHAIRMAN (2 min.) Show how important it is to 'tell the good news from day to day.' To help new ones share the good news with others we ourselves must first appreciate the importance Scripturally of helping new ones to preach.

AUDIENCE PARTICIPATION (16 min.) This program has been divided into four main points. These will form the basis of the discussion, the audience having four minutes on each point to make comments and offer suggestions. To make certain that the program will be of practical value, as

well as spiritually edifying, the chairman may ask certain publishers to come prepared with a comment on a specific point. Comments should be brief and 'hit the nail on the head,' as it were, thus allowing for a number of brothers to comment on a single point. Following are the four points:

1. The importance Scripturally of helping new ones to preach.

2. How to aid good-will persons being studied with to see the importance of joining in the preaching work.

3. How publishers can help good-will persons overcome common problems that they have raised, thereby opening the door to service.

4. The ways in which new publishers can share in giving a witness. (Such as: Use of handbills, tracts to interested persons, magazine activity, reading a Scripture in the sermon and commenting on it and incidental witnessing.)

CHAIRMAN (2 min.) Put to use the suggestions so new publishers can be aided to share in December activity.

10 min: Hearing Results of Persons Being Studied with Aided in Service—a program of experiences conducted by the Bible study servant. (2 Tim. 2: 23-26; 4: 2; Titus 2: 1-10; Col. 4: 5, 6) The great service instruction, issued in Bible times, but to be carried out in this 'time of the end.'—Matt. 24: 14.

THE GOOD NEWS

## Simplicity and Practical Application

¹ When Jesus preached he did not use the language of the scholars and philosophers of his day. He spoke the language of the common people, using expressions familiar to them. Because he used simplicity of speech he could be understood by all. Our speech at the doors should have the same simplicity.

² Simplicity means clearness, unaffectedness and freedom from complexity. If you speak with simplicity you will not use theocratic terms in your sermons without proper explanation. Terms such as remnant, theocracy, Armageddon, great multitude, goats, other sheep, Jonadahs and so forth have no meaning to outsiders unless an explanation is given. If you use the term "battle of Armageddon," qualify it by saying, "that is, when God will destroy this present wicked world or system of things." Make sure each statement is understood before you make another one; then you will be speaking with simplicity. "Unless you through the tongue utter speech easily understood, how will it be known what is being spoken?" (1 Cor. 14:9) It takes a conscious effort to express oneself in terms that people can understand. That means we must be sensitive to the words and terms we use at the door, making sure they are clear to the householder. We should also have simplicity in our theme. It should be a simple point, which is clear, logical and easily grasped.

³ When Jesus preached he made frequent use of illustrations. "Then he told them many things by illustrations." (Matt. 13:3) He told about the sower that went out to sow seed; about the Kingdom being like a mustard grain, like yeast in dough and like a dragnet. He told many other things that were common to the people's experience. His simple and direct illustrations helped to impress on the minds of the people the truths he preached. We can make the same good use of illustrations.

⁴ Since people are generally interested in themselves, you will need to point out how the subject you are speaking about affects them and how they can benefit. If you are speaking about new-world blessings, ask if

they would like to live in a world where they and their children would never be sick. Then explain how they can. To people living in large cities it might be pointed out that it is unsafe to be on the streets at night, but under the rule of God's kingdom there will be no danger. There may be some local condition troubling a community that will lend itself well for making practical application of your sermon. Be alert in applying such things.

⁵ When Jesus spoke the people listened "with pleasure," not only because he spoke the truth but also because he spoke in a way they could understand. He used simplicity of speech with good illustrations, making practical application of what he said. Let us follow his example and improve the effectiveness of our sermons.
