### Check Sound Equipment Before Using

#### Needle Changing Essential

Transcription leaders and all persons operating sound equipment, which includes sound cars, phonographs and P.T.M.'s, should see to it that the turntable revolves at the proper speed. A careful check of this should be made by actual count for three minutes. This cannot be overstressed. In three minutes the transcription machines which are used to run the 16-inch discs should revolve 100 times, which is 33½ r.p.m. The phonographs should be set at a speed of 78 r.p.m. Please be careful in this regard and check your machines NOW and when used.

It is further very essential that the needles used on transcription machines and phonographs be changed after the running of each lecture. In other words, use a needle for only one side of a disc, whether the disc is 4½ minutes or 13½ minutes. Please keep this in mind, as some of the brethren have been negligent in this, and it is a reflection on the truth and the Kingdom which we are advertising, to reproduce these lectures in an unsatisfactory manner.

Many are inclined to be negligent because they think it is the Lord's business and he will overlook their mistakes. Remember, efficiency is very essential in the Lord's work. It would be well for each individual

listening to these lectures to remind the operator of any defect in the reproduction, so that the speed of the turntable might be checked and needles changed. Don't be careless with the Kingdom interests!

#### Do You Get Subscriptions?

The publishers who are carrying to the people the message of Jehovah's kingdom well know the necessity of continually receiving and assimilating Jehovah's gracious provision for their instruction and well-being through the Watchtower magazine. They cannot get along without the Watchtower themselves; why should they not give others to whom they witness the opportunity to obtain this most important periodical, the official publication of Jehovah's people on earth?

The publishers should present the Watchtower magazine to the people, and, to the same extent, The Golden Age. The definite contact with the organization, which is necessary to those of the great multitude, can be more firmly established by The Watchtower and The Golden Age coming regularly to the homes of all interested.

# Reports Are Very Necessary

## They Must Be Accurate

### Each Publisher Should Read This Carefully

#### Report Jonadabs

Our attention has been called to the fact that some of the service directors and sharpshooters are failing to include in their monthly reports the activities of Jonadabs who put in only a few hours a month in the service, claiming that if such activities were included the average hours per publisher for the group would be lowered. The instructions in regard to reporting field publishers are as follows: "Report the total number of different individual publishers taking part in the door-to-door service with the literature one or more hours during the month." In view of these instructions, if some Jonadabs put in one or a few more hours in the field service during the month, the service director or sharpshooter should be sure to count and report these as active field publishers for that month. This also applies to juveniles having a share in the witness work. It is true that at first the average hours per publisher for the group will be lowered somewhat, yet the activities of the older and more experienced publishers should tend to offset this. Jonadabs beginning in the service naturally do not put in full time the first month or two of service. However, after a short time Jonadabs contribute as much time to the field service as do a majority of those of the remnant, and this in the end materially aids the company's quota. Jonadabs are beginning to come, and every provision should be made to aid them to become and serve as field publishers of the Kingdom. Pioneers and auxiliaries in isolated territory having Jonadabs occasionally join them in field service and who have not enrolled with the Society for service under the sharpshooter arrangement should report those occasional activities separately to the Brooklyn office. Briefly report on a separate sheet of paper the number of books and booklets placed, number of publishers, hours of service, and obtainers. Send such Jonadabs reports along with your monthly report to the office.

The easiest way to use an auto radio or other radio set with the sound equipment is to place the microphone directly in front of the loud-speaker of the radio, pick up the program, and run it through the sound equipment the same as when one is speaking into the microphone. This is not satisfactory in every case, and should be experimented with to find out the results that can be obtained.

The other means of using a radio with the sound equipment is to establish direct electrical connection between the radio set and the sound equipment; but this is somewhat complicated and should be done by a competent radio mechanic. There are so many models of radios that do general advice can be given except that the most accessible place to connect onto the radio set is usually the wires going to the voice coil of the speaker. In most instances a matching transformer and a pad to reduce the volume will be required, the leads from the wires going to the voice coil being run through the transformer and pad and then through a plug which can be plugged into the microphone jack in the transcription machine, one lead to the end and the other lead to the ring on the plug, the outside sleeve of this three-wire plug not being used.

The centre of the input transformer in the transcription machine is grounded inside the machine. The ends of this transformer are connected to the jack to make contact with the tip and the ring of the three-wire plug. The outside sleeve connection of the plug will not be grounded, but this is connected to the e-volt inside the machine. The tip and ring will give a 200-ohm input.
