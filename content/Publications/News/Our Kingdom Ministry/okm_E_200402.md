## A Work Backed by God

¹ Among God's servants today, few possess outstanding education, wealth, or prominence in the present system of things. For that reason, some people dismiss our ministry as of little account. (Isa. 53:3) However, the Bible educational work that we do has brought comfort and hope to millions of people around the globe. How have ordinary people achieved such outstanding results? Only with God's backing. (Matt. 28: 19, 20; Acts 1:8) "God chose the weak things of the world," explained the apostle Paul, "that he might put the strong things to shame."—1 Cor. 1:26-29.

² The apostles and other first-century Christians were largely

"unlettered and ordinary." (Acts 4:13) Yet, they boldly pursued their commission to preach the good news, and Jehovah blessed their efforts. Despite obstacles and opposition, 'the word of Jehovah kept growing and prevailing in a mighty way.' Nothing could stop the work because it was backed by God. (Acts 5:38, 39; 19:20) The same has been true in modern times. Not even determined opposition by powerful rulers has been able to keep the good news from prevailing and growing.—Isa. 54:17.

³ All Credit to God: Does our favored position as God's ministers give us reason to boast in ourselves? Not at all. Referring to the Christian ministry, Paul

wrote: "We have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the power beyond what is normal may be God's and not that out of ourselves." (2 Cor. 4:7) Paul appreciated that he was able to carry out his ministry only in the strength that God supplied.—Eph. 6:19, 20; Phil. 4:13.

⁴ We likewise recognize that it is only because we have "obtained the help that is from God" that the preaching work is being accomplished. (Acts 26: 22) By such worldwide proclamation, Jehovah is using us in a mighty way to send a tremor through the nations—a portent of the judgmental shattering soon to come. (Hag. 2:7) How privileged we are to be "God's fellow workers" in the grand spiritual harvest!—1 Cor. 3:6-9.

## Regular Meeting Attendance —A Priority

¹ Christian families give priority to attending congregation meetings regularly. But the demands of life today can present a challenge. Are household duties, secular work, or schoolwork starting to encroach on time that has been set aside for the worship of Jehovah? Looking at things from Jehovah's standpoint will help us to maintain our priorities.—1 Sam. 24:6; 26:11.

² By gathering wood on the Sabbath, one Israelite man deliberately failed to consider Jehovah's point of view. He may have reasoned that he was providing for his family or that it was just a small thing. By means of the judgment rendered, however, Jehovah showed

that pursuing mundane activities during times designated for worship is a serious matter.—Num. 15:32-36.

³ Meeting the Challenge: For many it is a battle to keep secular work from interfering with meeting attendance. Some have met this challenge by speaking to their employer, trading shifts with workmates, seeking a more suitable job, or simplifying their life-style. Assuredly, such sacrifices in behalf of true worship are well-pleasing to God.—Heb. 13:16.

⁴ Schoolwork can also pose a challenge. "I do some of my schoolwork before the meetings and the rest after I come home," said one youth. If all assigned homework cannot be

completed on meeting nights, some parents have explained to teachers that attendance at Christian meetings is a priority for their family.

⁵ Cooperation and good planning are keys to accomplishing household chores in preparation for attending meetings on time as a family. (Prov. 20:18) Even younger children can be taught to be dressed and ready to leave for the meeting at a specified time. By their example, parents can impress upon their children the importance of meetings.—Prov. 20:7.

⁶ As the pressures of the present system of things increase, it is vital that we attend congregation meetings regularly. May we continue to view matters from Jehovah's standpoint and make regular meeting attendance a priority.—Heb. 10: 24, 25.

## We Can Do What Jehovah Asks

¹ Complying with Jehovah's laws and principles is the best way of life today, and it helps us to lay a fine foundation for an eternal future. (Ps. 19:7-11; 1 Tim. 6:19) However, Satan's world puts us under much stress. Our own imperfect flesh adds to the challenge. In trying to fulfill our Scriptural responsibilities, we may at times feel overwhelmed. (Ps. 40:12; 55:1-8) We may even wonder if we can do all that Jehovah asks of us. At such times, what can help us to maintain our spiritual balance?

² **Jehovah's Commandments Not Burdensome:** Jehovah never places unreasonable demands upon us. His commandments are not burdensome but are for our good. (Deut. 10:12, 13; 1 John 5:3) He takes into account our human frailties, "remembering that we are dust." (Ps. 103:13, 14) God mercifully accepts our best efforts to serve him, even if these are limited by our circumstances. (Lev. 5:7, 11; Mark 14:8) He invites us to throw our burdens on him, and he assures us that he will make it possible for us to remain faithful.—Ps. 55:22; 1 Cor. 10:13.

## What to Say About the Magazines

THE WATCHTOWER Feb. 15

"Many people are troubled by the bad things that have been done in the name of religion. Some feel that religion is at the root of mankind's problems. Have you ever wondered about this? \[Allow for response. Then read Revelation 18:24.] This magazine examines what the Bible says on this subject."

**Awake! Feb. 22**

"Many people delight in learning about animals, and some enjoy having them as pets. \[Allow for comment.] This magazine discusses some of the challenges of owning a pet. It also explains God's promise of a time when all animals will be at peace with one another and with mankind." Read Isaiah 11:6-9.

THE WATCHTOWER Mar. 1

"On one occasion, Jesus Christ was asked: 'Which commandment is first of all?' Notice his reply. \[Read Mark 12:29, 30.] Have you ever wondered what Jesus meant? \[Allow for response.] The article 'How We Show Our Love for God' examines the meaning of those famous words."

**Awake! Mar. 8**

"At one time, many people lived in fear of a nuclear war. Do you think nuclear war is likely today? \[Allow for response.] This magazine examines current developments that concern all of us. It also explains the Bible's promise of a world where fear will be a thing of the past." Read Zephaniah 3:13.
