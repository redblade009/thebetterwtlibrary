# BULLETIN for Jehovah's witnesses

AUGUST 1, 1933

## Kingdom Proclamation Period, Sept. 30-Oct. 8

### A Message to All of Jehovah's witnesses from Brother Rutherford

"Escape to the Kingdom" to Feature Campaign in Many Nations

#### Season to Be Holy unto the Lord

All Witnesses to Try to Put in More Time than Ever Before

Special Transcription Speeches Arouse Great Interest in Message of New Booklet

"God's typical people celebrated two periods of the year: one in the spring and one in the autumn season. These periods were 'holy unto the Lord'. Why should not God's anointed people specially observe during the year two days of joy, thanksgiving and praise unto Jehovah God? 'The joy of the Lord is your strength.' The Lord 'has given us an understanding of his word' and continues to graciously 'feed us upon food that is convenient'. The kingdom is here and progresses, and our business is to sing forth the praises of Jehovah God and, as his witnesses, to hear testimony to his name.'—The Watchtower, February 15, 1932.

September 30 to October 8 is to be the autumn "period of joyful thanksgiving, praise and service unto Jehovah God, a feast to the prosperous kingdom work", and therefore it is designated Kingdom Proclamation Period. To be sure, there are other service periods during the year in which the remnant delight to put forth a special effort to sing forth the Lord's praises, but, these being seasons of special feasting upon the goodness of Jehovah, it is the desire of the remnant to make each one of these spring and fall thanksgiving periods bigger and better than ever.

Brother Rutherford is so impressed with the importance of this fall period that he has addressed a special letter "to Jehovah's witnesses in all the earth", sending it to us from London, England, so that it may be received by Jehovah's witnesses in the August issue of the Bulletin, regardless of where they may be. All branch offices that publish their own Bulletin will carry a translation of this letter in their own particular language for the benefit of the workers in their land. You will be richly blessed as you read it and see more than ever the

importance of putting forth every possible effort to give thanks unto the Lord by actively engaging in proclaiming the Kingdom.

For a practical example of what would be pleasing and acceptable to Jehovah in these feast seasons, it is well to briefly consider what he required of his typical people. These experiences were recorded for our learning, upon whom the ends of the ages have come.

First: They were holy unto Jehovah.

Second: Every male member of Israel was bound to appear before Jehovah at the sanctuary at Jerusalem.

Third: There was a general occasion set apart from servile work.

Fourth: The first and last days of the feast were entirely devoted to the service of Jehovah.

This strongly indicates that the anointed and all who love Jehovah should wholly set themselves on his side and strive for the prosperity of Jerusalem, cutting off and laying aside all hindrances to the service work; wherever possible getting off some or all of the time from secular employment for this occasion, and especially setting aside the first and last Saturdays and Sundays of the campaign for the exclusive service of Jehovah.

If the Lord was pleased with and accepted this typical service of Israel, with greater reason we may expect him to be pleased with the whole-hearted participation of his people now in that which Israel's ceremonies typified.

Kingdom Proclamation Period being a season of feasting and rejoicing, Jehovah has provided his people with something special to make their hearts glad. That special course for the feast is to be a new 64-page booklet, Escape to the Kingdom. Fifty-two countries were able to use the Crisis booklet during the spring International Thanksgiving Period, and every effort is to be made to get this booklet published in even more languages and shipped in advance to all countries, so that all of the brethren can share in its distribution during Kingdom Proclamation Period.

# Rubber Cement for Pasting Placards

The remnant rejoice to know that the name of Jehovah is their strong tower! "There must be no divided affection. God preserves and brings into his house only those who love him and serve him in spirit and in truth. To dwell in the house of Jehovah God is the greatest favor that could possibly be bestowed upon any creature. To enjoy that favor one must earnestly and diligently seek after the Lord's house and faithfully comply with the divine requirements."—Preservation, page 215.

The transcription machines which are in the hands of the brethren are being put to good use in getting out the Kingdom message. The early reports indicate good attendance at the meetings. In fact, for 145 meetings held by the pioneers the total attendance has been 7,000, or an average of approximately 40 at each meeting. They have had many interesting experiences in holding these meetings, and a report of such in addition to the regular report card information is appreciated by us; for from these many valuable suggestions can be obtained. Thus, one brother has found that if rubber cement (the stainless kind used in art work) is used in pasting on the placards the announcements of place and time of meeting, the cement will rub right off when the paper is removed, leaving the poster next and clean. In leaving these posters in the store it is well to impress the storekeeper with the fact that you will call for them after the meeting and that you do not want them destroyed. If you don't do this they are likely to be thrown out.

Another brother writes:
"You may be interested to know of my experience at an unemployment camp near Jacksonville. We have been out several times with the machine. There are more than 500 men living at this state military camp. I called on the major and the captain in charge and asked permission to entertain the men for an hour with Judge Rutherford's lectures and a unusual program. I presented both with a Crisis, and they agreed to post a notice on the board. They put us on the platform for boxing beats. We tuned up to 70, and you could hear it all over the big camp. Had an audience around the ring of about 100. At the close they gathered around and asked questions. Practically all of them were opposed to religious of today. When we promised to return in two weeks' time, they asked, 'Why can't you come next week?' and so it was 'next week' until we made five trips. The electrician delighted to help us get electrical connections. He had been an assistant field manager for NBD, and, I later found out, was to put us out of the camp if anything was said that was not O.K.' The second trip he was glad to accept some booklets and offered to place the whole set of booklets in the camp library, where, he informed me, they were creating much interest. At the last two broadcasts the captain was present, expressing himself as pleased that the men were taking such interest in a program of that nature. We used two musical records to draw the crowd, and then a lecture with a brief introduction of the judge. These camps may be a place that some of the friends are overlooking. There are many fine fellows among them, and they advertise as they travel."
