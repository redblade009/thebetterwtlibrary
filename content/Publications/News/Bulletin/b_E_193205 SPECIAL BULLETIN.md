#### You Will Enjoy This Trip Around the World with Jehovah's witnesses

#### This Is the Lord's Doing 45 Countries Represented in the Report

Who would ever have thought, a few years ago, that the remnant would live on earth to see the day when two million, four hundred forty-seven thousand, eight hundred and forty testimonies concerning Jehovah and His kingdom would be given within a short period of eight days? Yet this is actually what has occurred. International Thanksgiving Testimony Period, from March 20 to 27, resulted in this glorious

shout of praise and thanksgiving lifted by the voices of 38,585 joyful witnesses of Jehovah who desired to show their thankfulness to Jehovah by singing forth his praises.

International Thanksgiving Testimony Period found those who love the Lord unitedly carrying on the work in accordance with the Lord's commands. The results themselves show that Jehovah does have a people in the earth who are serving as his witnesses, who see eye to eye, and who are working unitedly as one man in the same great cause. Those in the people class, whether located in the most isolated and remote sections of the earth or in the thickly populated sections of 'Christendom', were found singing in unison during this Thanksgiving Testimony Period. How truly the words of Isaiah 52: 8 apply to God's people

at this time: 'Thy watchmen shall lift up the voice; with the voice together shall they sing; for they shall see eye to eye, when the Lord shall bring again Zion.'

To get a world-wide outlook upon the activities during this particular period let us make a mental tour of the world and behold with what unity and real the ancient have responded to the call to service. Starting in the northwestern part of the continent of North America, we will proceed south through South America, taking in the West Indies en route, and then make a tour of Europe. From the British Isles we will move north to the Scandinavian countries and Iceland and from there southward through central and southern Europe and the Balkans, and then across the Mediterranean into Africa. From Africa we will look in upon Australia before going to Asia. Our last stop in our trip around the world will be in Hawaii.

Our journey starts in Alaska, where we find one pioneer valiantly facing all odds to have his part in this Thanksgiving Testimony Period. His efforts resulted in 120 persons receiving the testimony. From throughout Canada and Newfoundland reports were received from 1,637 workers, showing that 68,816 testimonies were delivered. In the United States more workers participated in the service during this period than at any other one time. The total of 13,540 workers places this country at the head of the list in number of workers, and the 611,381 testimonies which these gave puts a real responsibility upon the rulers of 'Christendom' who will not heed Jehovah's message of warning. Into Mexico we now proceed, where 5,401 persons heard the glad song song by 68 workers, and then continue south into Central America, our first stop being Guatemala. One pioneer reports 87 testimonies given in that country. Costa Rica is not far distant, where 26 witnesses testified to 886 persons. Panama reported 9 workers and 188 testimonies; and the Canal Zone had 8 workers, who gave 200 testimonies. Before going to South America we look over the large group of islands known as the West Indies. There we find 6 workers in the Bahamas, 12 in Cuba, and 2 pioneers in Porto Rico, delivering 92, 2,070 and 295 testimonies respectively. The branch in Trinidad reported 171 workers in Trinidad, Tobago, Grenada and Barbados, and a total of 6,563 testimonies. No report was received from the branch in Jamaica.

# 444 Pioneers Putting in Pioneer Time

## 1,285 Pioneers Putting in Auxiliary Time

### All Jehovah's witnesses Will Be Interested in These Figures

The _Watchtower_ of May 15, 1932, stated: 'JEHOVAH'S real temple was not set up until 1918, when the Lord Jesus came and the faithful sleeping saints were raised and brought into Zion, and the faithful ones remaining on the earth were also brought into the temple. From that time forward the praises of Jehovah must begin and must be sung throughout the nations even within the hearing of the present rulers of the earth. Such is the reason why the vigorous witness work began after 1918, and particularly from 1922 forward. Who, then, are the 'kings \[that] bring presents unto thee', as stated in this twenty-ninth verse of the Psalm? Surely not the kings of Satan's organization, because they do nothing of the kind. The words of the Psalm, therefore, must refer to the remnant, amounted to the kingdom with Christ Jesus. This conclusion is supported by Revelation 21:24, to wit: 'And the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it.' Members of the faithful remnant class are in the covenant for the kingdom and are now bringing presents unto the Lord, in this, that they present themselves living sacrifices to bring honor and praise to the name and to the kingdom of the Most High. No other kind of presents could be brought into the temple. 'Yes, and pay unto the Lord your God; let all that be found about him bring presents unto him that ought to be feared. He shall cut off the spirit of princes; he is terrible to the kings of the earth \[Istatute earth].'—Ps. 76:11,12.'

Jehovah's witnesses realize and appreciate the fact that they are being taught of Jehovah, and they desire greatly to follow the instructions He gives them through _The Watchtower_. The pioneer witnesses have been released from all secular work and duties, and are free to devote all their time and effort to Jehovah's service. These at all times are endeavoring to increase their usefulness in Jehovah's service, that they may better sing forth his praises. Now the plight of the people is such that it is a most opportune time to present to them the message of the kingdom. Also this is the most favorable season of the year in which to go from house to house witnessing. It is hoped that a mighty witness will be given during the summer months by 'kings bringing presents unto thee', to the honor and praise of Jehovah's name. It is to be expected that thus presenting themselves living sacrifices to Jehovah will require time and much effort. In fact, it will demand each one's keenest determination and whole-souled devotion to Jehovah, to the exclusion of all else. But Jehovah's witnesses have found the privilege of serving him so great as to be worth their best efforts, and so they are joyfully singing his praises as they fight for his kingdom.

To aid the pioneers in their work, the following facts are brought to their attention. During the month of March pioneers to the number of 1,725 engaged in the work. Of this number only 444 met the minimum time requirement of 25 hours each week in witnessing, and 1,285 pio-

nciers failed to do so and devoted less than that amount. It is surprising indeed to see the contrast in results accomplished by the 444 pioneers who did meet the minimum time requirement with those of the 1,285 who did not. The figures are:

Notice that the 444 pioneers devoted almost as much time as the 1,285 other pioneers did. Also that the 444 gave 227,884 testimonies, to compare with 270,013 testimonies given by the other 1,285 pioneers. Note, too, that the 444 pioneers devoted an average of 20.8 hours more per week in the witnessing than did the 1,285. In fact, on the average the 444 pioneers devoted two and a half times as much time each to the work as did the group of 1,285 other pioneers. This fact enabled them to place 90,102 books and booklets, while it required nearly three times as many other pioneers to place 22,489 books and booklets. The group of 444 placed literature in the hands of 60,541 persons, whereas the other 1,285 pioneers reached 70,736 persons. Thus it is to be plainly seen that about one-quarter of those on the pioneer list accomplished almost as much work in the field during March as did the remaining three-quarters of the pioneers enrolled.

These figures are not based upon the work of those placing the most literature, but the reports of the entire list of pioneers for March were gone over, and the work of every pioneer who devoted an average of 25 hours per week was included, and is shown in the tabulation 'By 444 pioneers'. The work of all the remaining 1,285 pioneers for the month of March is included in the other tabulation. 'By 1,285 pioneers.' These figures also show that if all the pioneers devoted as much time and effort to the witnessing as these 444 pioneers did, the placement of literature by pioneers for March would have been approximately doubled.

The purpose in calling this to the attention of the brethren is to invite, encourage and urge the pioneers to do their very utmost to devote as much time as possible to the work of house-to-house witnessing. The great results accomplished by the group of 444 pioneers show what can be done if each one will exert his best endeavors to put in his full time in the actual work of house-to-house witnessing. _The Watchtower_ for May 15 further states: 'The faithful remnant are near the end of their earthly pilgrimage. Soon these shall see the manifestation of Jehovah's power for the vindication of his name.' Therefore it behooves each and every pioneer to press on with greater determination and an increased desire to devote as much time as is possible to the work of witnessing from house to house, singing Jehovah's praises to the people.

Some will say, 'Well, there were things that hindered me from putting in as much time as these other brethren did.' If so, it is hoped that each one so hindered will make a greater effort to remove such hindrances as prevented him from put-

ting in pioneer hours in the field service. What these various obstacles were is immaterial and does not matter. What is important is the fact that the 1,285 pioneers allowed such to interfere with their activity to the extent of their devoting an average of only 13.5 hours per week to Jehovah's service.

It is suggested that each and every pioneer witness take to hear this matter of time spent in witnessing, and consider it seriously, and plan how he may increase the time spent in actually witnessing from house to house, and then do if Jehovah bears the burdens of his faithful sons, and each one should take advantage of the privilege of presenting this important matter to Jehovah in prayer. Thus doing, each pioneer may joyously feel that he is indeed 'presumably himself' a living sacrifice to bring honor and praise to the name and to the kingdom of the Most High.'

While these figures are cited from the records of the pioneers, it is a matter of concern to every one of Jehovah's witnesses, and each auxiliary, company worker, and sharp-wooder may have the privilege of increasing the time he spends in the field and thus increase the scope of the witness.

And now we take a trip north-eastward to Europe, stopping first in the British Isles, where 1,728 workers lifted their voices in praise and thanksgiving so that 283,441 heard their glad song. Across the North sea, in the Scandinavian countries, the Northern European office informs us that in Denmark 300 participated in the work and witnessed to 20,115 persons; in Norway there were 144 workers giving 10,274 testimonies; in Sweden, 486 workers giving 20,970; and in Finland, 455 giving 24,081. Latvia reported 27 workers and 1,231 testimonies; Lithuania, 31 workers and 2,108 testimonies; and Kolonia, 20 workers serving notice on 4,524. These Scandinavian countries certainly did well in their average testimonies per worker. Koen in Iceland the voice of one pioneer was heard witnessing to 284.

Our route now takes us south through Germany, and here we find 13,704 workers engaged in the service; only 6 less than in the United States, but they gave many more testimonies, as their final report shows 608,333 delivered. Surely that is a cause for rejoicing on the part of all. The Central European office, at Berne, reports in one total the work done in France, Belgium, Holland, Yugoslavia, Austria, Poland and Switzerland. Italy, Rumania and Algeria may also be included, as these countries are likewise under the supervision of that office. Throughout this territory they show 2,157 workers delivered 183,330 testimonies. In Czechoslovakia 34,324 people heard the message from 323 witnesses of Jehovah; and 9,000 in Greece from 250. The warning to 563 of the leaders and people was delivered by a little band of 18 in Portugal, and by the same number in Spain to 1,445.

From Europe we travel through Algeria (where some work is being done, but just what we cannot tell, as the detailed information has not been given by the branch) to Nigeria, which is just below the equator, in West Africa. Even here we find 86 workers were in the field during this period, delivering 8,540 testimonies. Still further south we come to North and South Rhodesia and Nyasaland, together with Cape Town and territory in that vicinity. The South African branch reports 141 workers, and 31,312 testimonies were the results in these places for this period.

Still continuing eastward, we hear of the voices of 743 which were lifted in praise during Thanksgiving Period, and 72,088 testimonies delivered, in Australia and New Zealand. The branch there reports that they had not at that time heard from the South Sea Islands workers. In Asia notice was served on the rulers and the comforting message given to the people by 106 workers, in India, Burma and Ceylon, and 3,458 testimonies delivered. Japan reports 75 workers and 27,009 testimonies. This probably also includes the work in Korea, as the work in this country is under its supervision. With a stop in Hawaii, where we find it participated in Thanksgiving Testimony Period, witnessing to 1,283, our trip around the world comes to an end.

And that is how 2,447,840 testimonies were delivered by 38,583 workers throughout the earth during the short period of eight days. Jesus foretold that the Kingdom gospel must be preached in all the world unto all nations as a witness before the final and would come. There is much yet to be done, and by the Lord's grace the remnant will press on with the seal peculiar to the Lord's house and joyfully respond to every opportunity of service that is afforded by the Lord through his organization.
