# 1930 A YEAR OF HONORING GOD'S NAME

2,173,271 Distribution

### Foreign-speaking Brethren and Sharpshooters Do Splendidly

Literature Placed in Over 1,000,000 Homes by Workers Average Hours per Week, 4.3

As we come to consider the past year's activity of the English and foreign-speaking classes and sharpshooters it is with thanksgiving to the Lord for the marvelous way in which he used his people to magnify his name throughout the earth.

The fiscal year just closing was one of continual depression, from a business standpoint. Abnormal weather conditions, resulting in crop failures in large areas of this country, and the oppression of the people by the prominent ones in Satan's organization were such as to make the giving of an effective witness to the Lord's glory impossible if we viewed it from a worldly standpoint. Yet despite all of these, which indicate the best efforts on the part of Satan to stop the work, the Lord used his people to give the most tremendous and widespread witness ever given on the earth.

As we make an analysis of the activities of the different parts of the Lord's organization we note that no one branch stands out prominently, but that each one, whether in large classes or of isolated sharpshooters or those who speak a foreign tongue, got practically the same results for the time one spent in the service. ## His Servant Gave a Real Witness

### Blessings Untold Are Those of the Anointed

The fiscal year of 1930 came to a close October 1. The report for the year's activities is most encouraging, as it reveals that those really in the truth in the United States have been making the kingdom interests the biggest thing in life and have been putting forth a determined effort to show forth the praises of Jehovah. That which is most gratifying to us is the way the friends have responded to the doing of the work which the Lord has commissioned them to do as his witnesses. As strenuous as the campaign was for the year 1929, during 1930 more workers were regularly in the field and gave more hours to the work. Instead of the 1,480 pioneer and auxiliary colporteurs and 3,000 class workers which were in the field weekly during 1929, the past year saw 1,757 colporteurs and 4,375 class workers. And now these friends did work! Almost half a million more hours, 445,893 to be exact, were given in singing forth the praises of Jehovah, or a total of 2,379,597 hours, as compared with 1,953,704 hours in 1929. That in itself is evidence of how the real kingdom interests are being taken away from those who have hid their one talent in the earth and given to the ten-talent class, for those who have a real vision of God's purpose are diligently seeking to increase their kingdom privileges.

And now, what about the total books and booklets placed? While the quota was not reached, an account of the unforeseen financial distress, which kept many from obtaining the literature, were childhood books and 10¢ booklets were placed during the past year than in any other year in the history of the church. The final totals showed

2,057,282 books, as compared with 1,915,003 during 1929; or an increase of 142,279. The 10¢ booklets placed numbered 2,020,124, as compared with 1,877,891 during 1929; or an increase of 742,233. Of course there was a big decrease in the number of 5¢ booklets placed, on account of there being no special effort made with these, and therefore the total literature placed during the year was less than that during 1929. Only 504,924 5¢ booklets were placed, as compared with the 2,050,998 during 1929. This was expected, but it seems to us really remarkable that with 1,546,074 more 5¢ booklets placed in 1929 than in 1930 this large lead should be so far overcome that the total literature distributed numbers 5,182,330, or only 601,502 less than during 1929. Since 5¢ booklets were excluded in figuring the quota for 1929, but were included in that for 1930, the results on the quota basis show a total increase for 1930 or 142,279 books and 1,247,157 booklets.

This tremendous witness was left in 2,392,443 homes which means that practically 10,000,000 people will be in direct contact with the literature which has been left in their homes during the past year. One might say "ten million" without really appreciating what a city this makes, but when it is considered that this represents almost one-twelfth of the people in the entire United States, or more than the entire population in the states of Arkansas, Oklahoma and Texas combined, one can better appreciate just what it means. Many more have received the witness over the radio. Surely there is great cause for rejoicing on the part of those who have the Lord to see how wonderfully he is having the witness given that he is Jehovah.

## Auxiliaries Have a Remarkable Year

### Peak Enrollment, 933

535,369 Books and Booklets Placed in 294,283 Hours

In the year 1921-1922 the auxiliary colporteur work was organized, during which period there was an average of 194 auxiliary colporteurs enrolled throughout the year. The next year saw the enrollment list increased to an average of 312. But it was not until 1928-1929 that the real privilege of being a colporteur was recognized by the friends in general. During that year the enrollment list took a decided jump to 633. We thought that that was splendid, but during the year which has just drawn to a close there has been another substantial gain, the average enrollment being 750, with a peak enrollment of 933 reached on July 16.

The faithfulness with which the auxiliary colporteurs are endeavoring to carry out their divine commission may be judged by the fact that out of this number there has been an average of 545 who have reported regularly each week, as compared with 408 during the previous year. These put in an average of 10.4 hours weekly, as compared with the 8.45 during 1929, or practically 2 hours more weekly. This may be accounted for by the fact that Sundays' time has been included during 1930, whereas previously the method of reckoning excluded Sundays. By putting forth this effort the auxiliary colporteurs were able to give a total of 294,283 hours in the witness work, as compared with 179,406 during 1929. Isn't that splendid? # TESTIMONY WEEK! READ AND RECORD

## 750,000 Testimonies Week the Best Ever

### Results Surpass Everything Previously

#### Done by Classes

#### This Gospel of the Kingdom Must Be Preached and Is Being Preached 391,433 Testimonies Given by 9,015 Workers

We have the report from the classes and sharpshooters for the 750,000 Testimonies Week, and it, like the yearly report, indicates the Lord's blessing on his people. As far as the amount of literature is concerned, this week exceeded September 1929, when business conditions were good and people generally were pretty prosperous and it was not so difficult to place the books. It exceeded even the 150,000 Hour Week of last July, which had one more day than the 750,000 Testimonies Week.

The total literature placed by all classes and sharpshooters was 40,251 bound books and 139,253 booklets, making a total of 170,466 pieces of literature. During 150,000 Hour Week we placed only 174,450 pieces, and during 1934 Week in September 1929 we placed only 166,950 pieces. There were 9,015 workers who participated in the drive, averaging 9.1 hours. The workers out during the 150,000-Hour Week put in one-half hour more per worker. However, they had an additional day in which to do this. Consequently, instead of its reflecting unfavorably toward the present drive, it reflects favorably for the worker during the 9-day period of the 750,000 Testimonies Week put in 81,974 hours, whereas, in July, for 10 days the workers put in only 83,377 hours. This indicates that as time goes on the Lord's people are appreciating more the necessity for using all their faculties to magnify the name of Jehovah. The Lord blessed the efforts of his people this fall most remarkably: in the July drive the workers were able to place literature in an average of one home every hour and a half, while in this fall drive they placed literature in a little better than one home an hour. The total testimonies given by the classes and sharpshooters were 391,433. In other words, during this drive almost 400,000 people received definite testimonies pertaining to the Lord's kingdom. This averaged 4.8 testimonies an hour. Of course, this work was done in urban territory and we cannot expect to maintain the same average throughout the year, because considerable work will have to be done in rural territory and when rurals are worked the average of 4.8 testimonies an hour will not be maintained. However, we believe that 4 testimonies an hour can be maintained, for the additional number of homes that can be called on in city territory will make up for the time that will be lost in reaching the homes in the rural districts.

The Light combinations went splendidly, considering the hard times and the tightness of money throughout the country. There were 14,684 two-book combinations placed, and practically all of these were the Light books 1 and 2. There were 30,844 Light books placed all together. However, some of the friends seemed to get the idea that the only booklet they were to take during this drive was the Prohibition booklet. Consequently, when the people did not accept the 95% combination

of Light they dropped down to the Prohibition booklet for 10¢, and in many instances placed only a 10¢ booklet when they should have placed 4 for 25¢ or 9 for 50¢. This resulted in a big increase in the single-booklet placements, when we might have had quite a number of booklet combinations instead. It should be kept in mind in the future that our booklet combinations are much more attractive and will be taken much more readily by the people than one booklet for 10¢. Two for 15¢ or 4 for 25¢ or 9 for 50¢ are much more attractive. Therefore the friends should get the habit of presenting them in combinations rather than simply. However, this is not intended to discourage anyone, but only to call attention to the possibility that we overlooked during this period; and knowing that each one wants to give the most effective witness possible, we are making the above recommendation.

Of the above group figures, the English classes distributed 31,714 bound books and 106,071 booklets, or a total of 157,785 pieces. There were 6,627 English workers in the field, putting in 60,719 hours, or an average of 9.2 hours per worker. These workers placed literature in 71,720 homes, averaging about 1.5 placements an hour; and they gave 289,859 testimonies, or 4.8 testimonies every hour, and averaged one placement to every 4 testimonies. In other words, they placed the kingdom message in its printed form in one out of every four

homes at which they were able to give a testimony.

The foreign classes placed 5,509 bound books and 26,959 booklets, making a total of 32,408 pieces. There were 2,143 workers participating, working 16,310 hours and averaging 7.6 hours per worker. These placed literature in 16,465 different homes, or a little better than one home an hour. In order to place this amount of literature they gave 82,387 testimonies, which averages 5.1 testimonies an hour. The foreign-speaking friends did better than the English-speaking friends as far as the number of testimonies per hour is concerned, but did not do so well from the standpoint of the number of testimonies required to place literature. The foreign friends were able to place literature in 1 out of every 5 homes where they testified, while the English friends were able to place literature in 1 out of every 4. However, this should be no cause for discouragement. The foreign-speaking friends are handicapped in many respects, in that they have difficulty in locating the people who speak their language and the amount of literature in many of the foreign languages is limited to a few books and booklets. In fact, taking all things into consideration, the foreign-speaking friends have much for which to be grateful to the Lord, because of the manner in which he used them to magnify his name in this drive.

## "10,000,000 Testimonies" Quota for 1931

### Report Testimonies Each Week

#### Details of Quota in December Bulletin

Looking for the Lord's guidance in our future activities, and seeing that he has so richly blessed the efforts put forth during Testimonies Week, it seems that it would be pleasing to him to make this year's quota on the testimonies basis rather than on books and booklets alone. It has been decided, therefore, to do this, setting up the quota 10,000,000 testimonies for the year which began October 1. From now on a careful record should be kept by all the workers of every testimony given, and every testimony given, as well as books and booklets placed, should be reported; for 10,000,000 is a big number. We will tell you more about this quota and the plans for the year in the next Bulletin.

Since the quota is based on testimonies, it is essential that all the class workers, sharpshooters, colporteurs and class service committees have a clear understanding as to just what constitutes a testimony. Judging by some of the reports for 750,000 Testimonies Week, some are still confused on the matter.

The special work which the anointed have been commissioned to do is to preach the Kingdom gospel as a witness. Therefore, each call where one is permitted to proceed for enough with his courses to any something about the kingdom should be counted as one testimony, regardless of the number who listen to it. To do this it is not necessary to give a long-ended discourse, but one should be able to get to this point within a few

minutes. More time may be needed, of course, to develop the interest sufficiently for placing the books. This is the purpose of the full caucus. But if the call is interrupted and the interview closed before mention of the kingdom has been made, it should not be counted as a testimony. The only exception to this general rule would be in the case where a person takes the literature before the worker reached this point. In such a case the leaving of the books or booklets with the party would be considered as completing the testimony.

Remember, then, a testimony is counted each time the worker says something about the kingdom. If two or more persons happen to listen to the one presentation, it would be counted as one testimony only. Even if these two or more persons take books or one takes several sets to be used as presents for others, it would be considered as only one testimony given.

Testimonies should be reported on the weekly report cards in the upper right-hand corner. For the colporteurs and sharpshooters there is ample space there on their cards. On the other cards report should be made in this corner between the "Language" line and the "Brothers...Sisters..." line.

# Watertower Radio Program to Be Extended

## Brother Rutherford's Lectures to Be Heard Each Week

### Over 100 Stations Already Arranged For

#### New Radio Slips Ready November 17 Advertising Programs

For many years much publicity has been given to Brother Rutherford's lectures, both in large public meetings and over the air. Millions have listened in to the big chain network of radio stations and to the weekly broadcasts of _The Watch Tower's Sunday_ morning hour. People have packed theaters, auditoriums and coliseums, and frequently made necessary the placing of amplifiers on the streets so that all the crowd unable to get in might hear also. Thus Brother Rutherford has become internationally known and many people are interested in hearing what he has to say. This being true, the Society has decided to take advantage of the public interest created by this large amount of advertising. To further the witness about God's kingdom in radio service, featuring a Bible lecture by Brother Rutherford once every week, will be extended to cover the entire United States.

In the past there have been thirty or more stations broadcasting his lectures, but new arrangements are made for broadcasting his talks over more than a hundred stations each week. This new arrangement will have two great advantages. First, the message of the truth will be broadcast to a far greater audience and more people will listen in; second, those that hear will be in a receptive mood to receive the canvasser, or the Lord's witness, when he or she calls at the door. Many people on earth today are seeking the truth, and when they hear it they will recognize it as the truth from the Lord. This will give the remnant a better opportunity to leave the books with them.

About a month ago a letter was sent to most of the classes, especially those that had a radio station in their own city, and these were asked to make an investigation in regard to broadcasting a fifteen-minute program every Sunday morning from 10:00 to 10:15 o'clock. Each program is to be an electrical transcription of Brother Rutherford's lecture. The fifteen-minute period will embrace an announcement introducing the speaker and will be followed by a thirteen-and-one-half-minute lecture, which in every instance will be given by Brother Rutherford. These lectures are clear, concise, and to the point, and will bring to the listeners the facts and the truth on the particular subject being discussed.

We have not had a response from all of the classes respecting this letter, and we should appreciate hearing from all of them, whether or not they can obtain a station for this broadcast. The arrangements made up to the present time are very wonderful, and the United States is covered from coast to coast. In the East, from 10:00 to 10:15 Eastern Standard Time there will be over forty stations on the air. The first lecture will be broadcast on December 7. Those who listen in in the Central states will have over thirty-five stations from which to choose. There will be over twenty stations in the Mountain and Pacific Time region sending forth the truth, beginning December 7.

This new arrangement for broadcasting the truth and preparing the field for the house-to-house witness

work has caused great enthusiasm amongst the friends. They are anxious to advertise this new radio feature of _The Watch Tower_, and the Society is arranging a special radio slip or folder, to be ready by the 17th of November. We hope that all the friends throughout the country will use these radio slips to advertise this WATERTOWER program. The slip will carry a list of all the stations broadcasting the program, and will be offered to the friends at 21:00 a thousand. As we can print these on our large presses in quantities of one million at a time, it will be possible for us to

supply the friends with them at this low price, even though they are going to be considerably larger than the ones used heretofore. No special slips will be printed by the Society for certain local sections. Our purpose is to advertise the WATERTOWER program, and not any individual stations. The pioneer colporteurs also can use these slips in their territory, and we are granting them a special rate, in accordance with our regular policy of hearing part of the pioneer burden. The Society is glad to do this so that the pioneer colporteurs also may have the use of them. Please order these slips when you order your books, so that we can ship them by freight.

## Colporteurs Again Do Big Things

### Testimony Week Filled Them with Enthusiasm

#### Pioneer and Auxiliary Report Most Interesting

Once more the colporteurs, pioneer and auxiliary, joyfully grasped the opportunity of demonstrating to the Lord that they have taken a positive stand on his side and desire to be right in the thick of every fight in which his name is involved. Although Satan's agents threatened a big counter attack during 750,000 Testimonies Week, we have heard of no workers encountering any of the representatives of the International Council of Churches out distributing their literature as they planned to do in their 'religious educational' week. The Lord said that his people would be willing in the day of his power; but apparently the Devil is not finding his people quite so willing.

The colporteurs seized upon the 'testimonies' idea with enthusiasm. Many of the pioneers did not have as much urban territory in which to work, so found it difficult to maintain the average of 5 testimonies an hour. They did very well, however, averaging 4.45. The auxiliaries averaged 4.8, which was possible for them on account of the large number of them who were located in urban territory, where they could make more calls.
