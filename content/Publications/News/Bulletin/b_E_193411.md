# SHALL THE NATIONS HOPE"

Sound Machines Bring Comfort

As the witness with the transcription lectures by means of the phonographs and transcription machines progresses, the field for this work is seen to widen continually and there appears to be no limit to the possibilities of this method in seeking out those of good will. From the field workers come many reports showing specifically an increase in placements of books and booklets where the phonograph has been used. Examples: "At our first meeting we had 14 present besides ourselves, and we placed 17 bound volumes in 15 minutes." "A lady read the testimony and said, 'I can't agree with Judge Rutherford, and I don't want any more of his books.' After listening to four lectures and asking a few questions her husband took Preparation and 7 booklets and a neighbor lady also took Preparation and 6 booklets. Similar occurrences happen almost every day."

The placement of literature at a meeting, however, is not the entire measure of the meeting's value, nor is such the end of the phonograph's use at any one place. Repeated meetings for the same group are most valuable, and where workers are in one territory indefinitely, as is the case with all territories except those of isolated pioneers, return meetings should be arranged for at every opportunity. At all meetings with the phonographs the people should be directed to the central transcription machine meetings in the territory, and also to the regular studies of the company. In this way the field

service with the literature, transcription machine work, studies, and phonograph service, work together, bringing those of the good will class close to the organization of the Lord's people. This close contact cannot be gained by a mere superficial covering of the territory and an indifferent attitude toward those manifesting interest. "It behooves each one of Jehovah's witnesses to aid others to understand these truths who have a desire to know and to serve Jehovah God."

The brethren in the United States have several thousand cars which they use in the field service, and every one of these cars should be equipped with a phonograph. Such would put a great many more phonographs in the work. Not only should these machines be part of the equipment available to all the workers, but they must be kept busy. Having the machine right with him, the worker can go ahead and run it on occasions when permission for a later meeting could not be obtained. "Some friends have machines which stand idle because the brethren go about asking the prospects if they want to hear a lecture."

Phonograph records are now in stock again, and your orders for these and phonographs can be filled promptly. Several orders for transcription machines are still being held, awaiting the manufacture of machines to ship. This work is being done as rapidly as other activities permit, and we want the brethren with such orders in to know they are not forgotten.

We have not found much opposition except in some cases when the dominion told us, 'This is our job.' We advised them to get out of the sinking ship before it is too late."—Pioneer, Northern British Columbia.

# BAHAMA ISLANDS

From a pioneer in the Bahama Islands: "The transcription machine reached Arthur's Town, and the message was appreciated by nearly all who heard. The people shout for joy, giving God thanks for the truth. One preacher is using the booklets in his church. He told me he saw the books contain nothing but the truth. He heard Brother Rutherford, and now he is asking God to forgive him for all the wrong he was preaching. Then he said Christ died for the truth and 'I will preach out of these books and let the people hear.' The lecture stirred the people, and World Recovery was meat in due season."
