# The Plainfield Meeting a Thriller

## The Lord's Protection Most Manifest

### Now for a Steady Downpour of Truth

According to the 1930 census, Plainfield, New Jersey, is a city with a population of 34,422; not so large, but its officials have been bitter in their opposition to the message of God's kingdom. On Sunday, July 30, their strong-arm squad attended in force the mass meeting which was held in their city for the purpose of hearing Brother Rutherford deliver a lecture on the subject "Why Religious Intolerance Is Practiced Now in This Country". Like the worthlessness of Basil Whom John called together to offer sacrifices unto Basil, they came decked in their robes of identification, handcuffs, ammunition belts, revolvers and sawed-off shotguns; in fact, armed to the teeth. There were two or three policemen stationed at each of the exits, besides others in plain clothes who were specially detailed at each exit and throughout the audience. These also carried guns. Even on the platform itself and behind the drop curtain armed guards were stationed, prepared to bans forth at the least prosecution. To the early arrival this armed guard was so large that it appeared that a policeman's convention, instead of a Bible lecture, was to be held.

An hour or more before the meeting time the people began to assemble for the lecture. The day was extremely hot, but many of the order-loving people of Plainfield and vitality who desire righteousness braved the heat and came early in order to get in. Many brethren had come also to participate in distributing the invitations to this special mass meeting. Seventeen of them had been seized by the police the previous day and held in jail to suffer as Christ suffered because of their faithfulness to Jehovah.

Suspicions of every act, the strong-arm squad, upon seeing one brother came in with a package, demanded to know what he had, but he refused to tell. Perceptibly the package was seized and torn open. Much to the chagrin of the officer, instead of finding a bomb or explosives, he found some copies of _The Golden Age_ which were afterwards given free to the people.

By three o'clock the big hall was packed and others had assembled in another theater connected by loud speaker extension. The audience hung on every word that Brother Rutherford had to say, as if spellbound. If the officials of Plainfield anticipated the stopping of the meeting by their display of arms, or if they expected that Brother Rutherford would put on the soft pedal, they were quickly disillusioned. Boldly and fearlessly, yet without sarcasm or ridicule or attacking any individual, the truth was presented to the people of Plainfield and the radio audience. Officialdom was given to understand what the Lord says will happen to them if they oppose Him and oppress, persecute and endeavor to suppress His witnesses. The strong-arm squad were seen paying close attention to the arguments presented.

At the close of his lecture Brother Rutherford offered free to every one present a copy of the special issue of _The Golden Age_ containing his open letter to the Catholic Press of America answering their attack on the radio stations in their effort to have his lectures put off the air. Brethren were stationed at the various exits who passed out these copies, and it was especially noteworthy

thy with what eagerness the people received them.

Following the meeting, the brethren who had been present assembled in a grove which had been used as the contact point for the distribution of the mass meeting announcements. Brother Rutherford had arranged with the police captain to release the brethren who were in jail, and the meeting in the park was delayed until these seventeen had been brought there from jail. Brother Rutherford then spoke to the brethren for a few minutes, outlining the big campaign to be made with this special issue of _The Golden Age_ and bringing to their attention words of encouragement from the Lord's Word. All went home rejecting greatly in the privilege which the Lord had granted to them that day of being his witnesses and having been present at this meeting at which the protection of the Lord, in spite of the strong-arm squad of Sarah's organization, was so clearly manifest.

And now, after such a thrilling meeting at which the Lord's protection and blessing was so clearly manifest, all the remnant throughout America can press the battle to the gate. This lecture sent to His people from the Lord will be put into _Golden Age_ form and will soon be ready. During the month of September all of the mounted should be prepared to distribute the first million edition of this _Golden Age_, and all the _Heaven and Purgatory_, _Crimes and Calamities_, _War or Peace_ and _Prohibition_ booklets that are now in stock. These booklets with the special _Golden Age_ will undoubtedly make the Catholics and their allies more tedious in their attack than before. However, the September campaign will just be a beginner and will get the brethren in good fighting action for the Kingdom Proclamation Period, in which _Escape to the Kingdom_, containing the "Holy Year" lecture, will be distributed far and wide. What a blessed privilege the Lord's people have now, to be engaged in a work that must be done speedily!

# The Demeanor of the Brethren

### Remember You Are Jehovah's Witness

All who participate in Jehovah's service as his witnesses should, of course, conduct themselves as he comes a representative of the Most High God. Never forget that you are a witness of Jehovah. You are not in any way to adopt the adversary's method of force, coercion, threatening or intimidation. With calm dignity and boldness tell the people the facts. If they refuse to hear or reject the facts presented, do not force yourself or your message upon them; walk on to the next house and present the testimony there. This is especially true in this special campaign. The truth and Jehovah's witnesses have been woefully misrepresented to the Catholic people, through their press and clergy. Many of them are going to be incensed against you because of their ignorance of the true facts of the case. Be patient, kind and considerate with them. Appeal to their justice. Tell them that you have not come for a controversy of any sort, that you simply wish to place the other side of the question before them and you wish to give them an opportunity to read Judge Rutherford's answer to all those charges. Try to make them see the fairness and justice in such a course. Never get into a controversy with them. That is what the enemy wants us to do now in order to have some excuse to bring down upon the mounted the local authorities and any other destructive force he may have in his command. Do not play into his hands. Avoid such a course. If you find people who are bitter and unreasonable and do not want the message, then calmly and quietly go on to the next house.

# Good Results with Transcription Machine

### Keep Them Busy

Reports coming to the office from the different brethren assigned to the work of operating the portable transcription machines indicate that in a number of cases these brethren do not really appreciate what comprises the real field for this type of work. Many of them seem to figure that all they have to do is to get a machine, set it up in some little, stuffy ball, do a little bit of local advertising, and then the people will come to the meeting.

Other brethren, realizing the need of forethought and planning for these campaigns, obtain splendid results. They realize that the last place in the world to put one of these machines, if they want a real audience, in this weather, is in a hot, stuffy ball. They go out in the open, into the parks, camping sites, picnic grounds, by lakes where the people assemble, and in summer cottage colons, and places such as these, where the people are and want to be. They feel at home and relax there and will gladly attend a meeting of this nature when it is arranged for at their convenience, and that is what we must do if we want success. In going after parks, if you cannot get permission from the park commissioner to use the public parks and playgrounds, don't lie down and quit then; go to the farmer across the street, or to any one convenient who has a suitable place for an assembly; scout around. There are many places in practically every community where meetings can be held.

These meetings can be held Saturday afternoon, Saturday evening, and three or four on Sunday, if proper effort is put forth and the territory is really scouted out by competent brethren. One brother, taking advantage of such openings during the month of July, put on 20 meetings and had a total attendance of 4,937, an average of almost 100 people at each meeting. These meetings were arranged by a pioneer in isolated territory. Another brother loaded his machine into his car and started out. He knew where there were a number of fruit pickers' camps along the highway. He planned in advance, got permission from the superintendent, and, starting with the first one, served them in a row, making five different places each Sunday, and repeated the route every Sunday until 24 of these meetings were held; at which 227 persons took literature, besides the great number who heard and did not obtain any literature.

These are simply illustrations of what may be done to push this work if the brethren are versatile and zealously using their kingdom interests to the praise of Jehovah.
