"Better and more books and booklets" is the slogan of the Watch Tower printing plant. We have tried our best to make these two new booklets the finest-looking and the most attractive we've ever published. We hope you will like them. They contain the very best reading matter, because it will bring to the people comfort and gladness of heart. It is because they contain the truth that we have endeavored to make covers that will attract attention and make people want the booklets. Of course, you yourself should get well acquainted with their contents before canvassing with them.

Brother Rutherford has made these two booklets _Judgment_ and _Opposition_, the best yet, and we expect a tremendous circulation. We are not going to tell you all about them, but here are some things of interest. On the cover of one appears the title, "Opposition. When will it end?" There is something to talk about right away. The people are suffering oppression. You can help them by placing in their hands booklets with the answer. As soon as one looks at it, it will make him want it. We believe these booklets will 'take big' with the public, and we hope that with the Lord's blessing, they will have the greatest circulation of any year booklets.

On the back half of the covers we have reproduced the five big sellers. The pictures of these five books, printed in four colors, can be used to great advantage in selling or taking orders for the set. The cover can also be used as a prospectus because it has such a splendid reproduction of our books.

### Directors Reporting

Colporteur Sales

Many of the directors still seem to have difficulty in reporting sales made by colporteurs. All sales made by auxiliary colporteurs should be reported to the director of the class the same as those of any other worker in the territory, and the director should include these sales in his weekly report to the Society, as well as on his yearly report at the end of the year. Auxiliary colporteurs are requested to make two reports weekly, one for the director and one for the Society. These reports by the auxiliary colporteur to the director should include all the sales they have made during the week, whether on Sunday or on weekdays. They should plan their work, however, so that what they do on Sunday is over and above their 10-hour weekly minimum requirement. Reports of colporteurs other than auxiliary colporteurs should not be included in the weekly report of the director. In fact, all colporteurs other than auxiliary are requested not to report to the director weekly, but to give him a report when the territory they are working in his assignment is completed, showing the number and kind of books sold, and in which town, that he might include this information in his yearly report covering the territory assignment of the class. In his weekly report the director should not report sales made by any colporteur except auxiliary colporteurs.

### Transportation Charges

What We Pay
What You Pay

The question has been raised in regard to expense of shipping books to classes and colporteurs. All shipments are made the cheapest way when we prepay the charges. If the one ordering desires the shipment made by mail or express when freight is cheaper, the difference between the cheapest way and that specified will be charged to his account.

In view of the fact that freight rates are based upon 100 pounds as a minimum charge, we ask that colporteurs and classes place their orders long enough in advance to make it possible for them to order at least this amount. The freight charges on shipments weighing over 100 pounds are figured proportionately at the 100-pound rate. To ascertain the weight of any shipment figure on the basis of 135 cloth-bound books or 500 booklets to the 100 pounds.

A close analysis of these figures reveals a number of interesting facts which will, we trust, be helpful to the service committees and the workers in planning their work for the remainder of the year. In considering the figures for January, the fact that the first twenty days of the month were devoted to the Peoples Friend drive makes that the most outstanding event of the month.

ANALYSIS OF JANUARY ACTIVITIES
In the February Bulletin we called attention to the fact that, due to the epidemic of influenza, we hardly expected that the Peoples Friend drive would come up to the quota set for this period. We are free to admit, however, that we did not expect that the decrease in the drive would come anywhere near the disappointing figures shown on the table above. Our quota for the drive was approximately one million booklets. Our sales for the period were 100,500, or about 800,000 short of what we expected. When we advised you in the January Bulletin that the March Bulletin would carry the results of the drive, we did not expect that our report would be so poor.

However, facts are facts, and no army can hope to be victorious if it is afraid to face the facts as they exist. The pertinent question that these figures bring up is, Why did we come so far short in the Peoples Friend drive in January? The first cause undoubtedly was the effect of the flu over a large portion of the United States, which retarded the activity of the workers in many of the classes. The second contributing cause was the intensely cold weather that existed in sections of the country where the weather is usually mild at this season of the year. The southwestern states, which usually have an ideal climate for our work during the winter months, had their worst winter in years, snow, sleet and ice tying up traffic and paralyzing business in general in some sections. The North and the Northwest were also tied up with similar conditions.

## CLASSES' RESPONSIBILITY IN SLEMP

While these facts undoubtedly played the major part in retarding our sales, there were other causes which resulted in so small a sale of the Peoples Friend booklet, and these are found amongst the classes and the workers themselves. During the October and November drive the average sales per hour per worker were 17 booklets. During the January drive the average was five booklets per hour, which seems to suggest that the real force of the message was not appreciated by the friends to the same extent as when the booklet first came out and that

consequently they did not put the same 'punch' into their canvass for the booklet and it did not be over' so well.

CLASSES NOT REPORTING
Another reason is that practicing one-third of all the organized classes in the country have not sent in reports for the month of January. We received reports from only 610 out of the 910 organized classes. There does not seem to be any real reason why this condition should exist, for regardless of how bad the weather is, a postcard can be dropped in the mailbox. Even if a class has been unable to do any work during the week, a report should be mailed to this office. Otherwise it is impossible for us to properly plan the work and cooperate with you in disposing of your stock and magnify the name of Jehovah. Therefore we again request that each service director do his part by seeing that a report card is forwarded every week.

CONTINUE WITH PEOPLES FRIEND
From this time forward, every combination of books sold should include a copy of The Peoples Friend and this booklet should be offered in every home where none of the other literature can be placed, so that this message will get into the hands of the people as quickly as possible.

## DELIVERANCE AND RECONCILIATION SALES WEEK

Considering the figures for the four months just ended, it will be noted that we are particularly weak in the distribution of Deliverance and Reconciliation. These are 12,000 and 10,000 short of the peak that Government has reached. Therefore an effort should be made to tighten up on the canvasses for these books. Examine your presentation at your workers' meeting. Discuss the proposition, and see if this difficulty can not be overcome so as to bring Deliverance and Reconciliation up to the same level as the other books.

As an indication of how the public appreciates Deliverance, we have before us a copy of a catalog issued by a book-publishing house wherein they quote "Deliverance, by Judge Rutherford—\$1.40". Of course this is entirely unauthorized by us, but it does illustrate the public's idea of the value of our books.

## Special Combination For This Week

As announced in the February 1 Watch Tower, the first IBSA Week for 1929 takes place May 19 to 26. Each service committee and worker should commence planning for this event right now. The drives should be arranged to cover the larger towns in your assignment that have not been worked during the winter months. The towns which you intend to canvass during that week should be chosen as soon as possible and captains appointed to look after the witness in each town. These captains should arrange to get well acquainted with the territory in advance. Figure out how many workers each captain will require and how the workers can be most advantageously distributed to avoid duplication of work and waiting around for books and territory. Also, the means for transporting the friends to these different towns should be considered, and whether interurban trolley, bus or train is to be used. Competent guides with a thorough knowledge of the territory should be appointed for each party, and depots for additional supplies arranged for in convenient locations which would be known to all the workers in that town. These can usually be arranged for in small places. Of course, if automobiles are used, they will constitute the depots for the literature and should be conveniently parked so as to be accessible to the workers. In this way many of the retarding influences and conditions that would hold up the work and waste considerable time may be largely eliminated before the drive commences.

MOST ATTRACTIVE COMBINATION
The particular combination to be used during May IBSA Week will be the five books. The Society has arranged to ship to each class whose reports indicate that they are disposing of these combinations in their territory a limited supply for IBSA Week. These combinations are being enclosed in a very attractive Flash-tone container which can be used to display the literature in a prominent place in the home, such as on a library table. This can also be placed in the bookcase, thus keeping the combination always intact. We believe that this container will add very materially to the attractiveness of the combination and be a big help in selling the entire combination at one time. People will rarely request that a combination packed in such a container be broken. We believe that the advantage derived from this method of presenting the books will be a big help in placing entire sets in the homes.
