## 41,902 Field Workers in the United States

### Look Ahead to 50,000 in 1940

It was a real joy to see the completed figures of the witness work in August, which month was designated the "Divine Mandate" Testimony Period. Undoubtedly all who desire to share in the divine mandate and the blessings of the Kingdom that will be theirs because of the stand they are now taking expressed their appreciation to the Lord by being publishers during the "Divine Mandate" Testimony Period. In the United States alone, 41,902 publishers reported as engaged in door-to-door witnessing. Publishers in Cuba and other places totaled 417, bringing the number of publishers reporting to the Brooklyn office up to 42,319. Last August there were 25,750 engaged in the field work in the United States. This shows an increase in 1939 of over 15,000 for this month alone. This news should make the hearts of all of Jehovah's witnesses and their companions rejoice, because surely they can see the great multitude taking their stand.

Distribution of the Salvation book got off to an excellent start, and the "THEOCRACY" Testimony Period just ahead should find as many publishers in the work and a greater distribution of books and booklets, due to the release of the Government and Peace booklet for distribution to the public. With the new year coming on, we look forward to the new quota of 50,000 publishers; which is a 25-percent increase over that for this year. When that is reached, we will raise it. Jehovah's rich blessing has been upon those persons who desire to follow and heed the instructions of Jehovah God through his Theocratic organization.

### Canada

An interesting report has just been received from Canada, also showing that they had a new peak of publishers for August, namely, 5,100. This is an increase of 1,898 publishers over the same month of last year.

# Keeping Track of Our Increase

## Each Company to Have Its Chart

The reports thus far received on the witness work accomplished by the companies during the fiscal year of 1939 just closed is very encouraging. The enthusiasm and zeal of the brethren is high, and we confidently expect and look forward to 1940 to be the greatest year ever to the honor and praise of Jehovah and his King Christ Jesus.

One particular fact noticeable last year is that the companies making the most rapid progress have been those whose servants are continually watching and reporting to the company on every phase of the witness work. The interest and zeal thus shown by the servants have fired the other publishers with similar enthusiasm, and the result has been that the company as a unit has wholeheartedly taken hold of every campaign and suggestion of the Society, with corresponding benefit to the people of their territory.

As one aid to all companies to be on the alert, the Society recommends that a large chart be made and conspicuously posted in your Kingdom hall showing the work done by the company during each month of 1939 with space beneath for comparing with it the work done during 1940. Below is set forth such a chart, which should be immediately made and kept up by each company. The chart sets forth the activities of a representative company during 1939. We suggest that the 1939 report be shown in red figures and the corresponding report for each month

of the new fiscal year be contrasted by being shown in black figures. Special consideration should be given monthly to three features of the work which are largely under the control of each company publisher. These are PUBLISHERS, HOURS, and BACK-CALLS. The brethren can get out in the field service every month and thus 'control' the number of publishers witnessing; they can put in time, the amount of which depends on the vision and zeal of the publisher; and they can make back-calls. The placement of literature and the sound attendance are beyond our control, though they are greatly affected by the other three features. Each publisher should make it his FIRST BUSINESS to get out in the field service, at the very least, once during every month, put in as much time as possible, and make back-calls; and the same servants and servants of the company should continually emphasize these features before the company. Keep always before you the quota set for 1940: 50,000 publishers, which is an increase of 25 percent over last year; 60 hours a month in the field, the quota outlined by Brother Rutherford in his letter of January 1, and of which we are still far short; and one back-call a week for each publisher. We hope to reach this quota by the end of 1940; and to do this each publisher must DAILY do what he can to further the interests of the Kingdom. Watch the chart, and make each month a greater one to the vindication of Jehovah's name and word.

### 100-Pound Shipments

A large majority of companies could order 100-pound shipments if the company servants and stockkeepers did a little planning in ordering. Please have in mind that it costs just as much for the Society to send a 75-pound shipment of books by freight as it does 100 pounds. To send several small orders a month means the Society spends more money to get the literature to you than if you would order one large shipment a month and make it 100 pounds or more. The Society does not encourage small companies to order 100 pounds, but they should place an order about once every two months for the supplies they require and at the same time make remittance for the literature placed during the previous period, so as to keep their accounts in good condition. Where there are pioneers in small companies, or even large companies, the pioneers can be asked to turn in their orders at a certain time, to be mailed to the Society in the same envelope, so that all of this literature can be shipped out as one shipment, thus taking advantage of the 100-pound freight rate. The literature can be distributed to the pioneers at the company headquarters when received. When these joint orders are shipped as one shipment the various packages are marked on the sealing tape as to whom they are for, thus making it unnecessary to open the carton to see what is in it. Co-operation on the part of company servants and groups of pioneers ordering 100-pound shipments will save the Society considerable money which can be used for other interests of the Kingdom.

### Do Not Ship Literature to Foreign Countries

Brethren having friends or relatives in other countries should not send literature to those countries. If you want these friends to get literature, order it through the Brooklyn office, and we in turn will forward your order for filling by the Branch in the country where you want the order delivered.

For example, if some Polish brethren in the United States want books sent to Polish people in France, send your order to the Brooklyn office with the proper remittance and the Brooklyn office will forward your order to the French office to fill. Government authorities have confiscated considerable literature sent from one country to another by the brethren. It is best to do this through the organization that the Lord has provided.

### Bibles

Many of the brethren throughout the country are obtaining Bibles from different sources to place with the literature. This is quite all right, and there is no objection to their doing so. However, care should be taken that these Bibles do not contain comments that are contrary to the truth and confusing to the people. Only Bibles should be used that have no comments in them beyond the marginal rendering and concordance.

### Discontinue Information Marches

Information marches throughout the country have given a good witness. But the witnesses will discontinue information marches for the present time. After October no more information marches are to be held by any of the companies until further advised by the Society.

The material you have for information marches can be stored until further notice.
