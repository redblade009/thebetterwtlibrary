## Around the World with Jehovah's Witnesses

### Taught Ones Teach Others

Upon being taught the truth appreciative persons delight to share with others the good things they have been taught. (Gal. 6:6, NW) This is taking place world-wide as more and more take teaching from Jehovah and in turn teach others.

A group of publishers in Colombia was surprised on witnessing in a small village to be greeted with: 'Oh, you talk just like our neighbor,' appreciating the truth 'the neighbor' was teaching others. In another area while a witness called at the front door preaching the new-world hope, a Pentecostal was going to the back door telling householders not to listen. In time the Pentecostal took teaching from Jehovah and is now privileged to teach others the truth. (127)

'I shall remain Catholic,' declared a woman who took a publication. Through a home Bible study she learned the truth and now is a publisher of the new world. (260, 261) And how long does it take to become a teacher? Not long, if you apply yourself. (90)

Having a burning desire to tell others what he had learned, a young man studying to become a priest met a witness and asked: 'Do you know what you are doing?' The witness knew what he was doing and convinced him. Now the young man is a pioneer. (137, 138)

'It's a mystery, my son,' was the priest's reply to a businessman's inquiry about the trinity. One of Jehovah's witnesses explained the 'mystery' and now the businessman looks forward to teaching others as a missionary. (105) Are you teaching others?

### Jehovah's Provision

Of all magazines published today, 'The Watchtower' stands in a class by itself. Why so? Because it is Jehovah's provision to guide those who love him to understand and keep informed of his glorious kingdom. No wonder then all of Jehovah's witnesses delight to take part in the 16th annual 'Watchtower' campaign.

By our utilizing three- to eight-minute sermons householders are assisted to know the value of 'The Watchtower.' However, some householders need no introduction to it, as was true of one in North Borneo. He asked if he could subscribe for 'the best magazine he had ever read,' without the witness even having a chance to present the offer. (244, 245)

In a remote part of Finnmark two missionaries were greeted by an old man: 'Do you really belong to my people?' He had read issues of 'The Watchtower' a neighbor subscribed for, understood the truth and shared the provision with others. (220, 221)

To discourage parishioners from listening to Jehovah's witnesses a priest in Egypt hung a copy of 'The Watchtower' on the door of the church. Did it discourage them? Not at all! In fact, it made some so curious they hunted up the congregation servant and are now having home Bible studies. (144)

Magazine Day activity is devoted primarily to house-to-house and store-to-store work. However, occasionally street witnessing is done and interesting experiences result. A man walked up to a publisher in Switzerland saying: 'I must have 'The Watchtower.'' The man had been a Catholic monk but, thanks to Jehovah's provisions, learned the truth and now shares it with others. (261)

## Presenting THE GOOD NEWS

### To Those Who Know of The Watchtower

During the last two months many favorably disposed persons have wisely subscribed for _The Watchtower_ on our invitation. Others, just passively interested at the time, took single copies. These will be somewhat acquainted with the magazine and when approached later some will say: 'Oh, yes, I know about _The Watchtower_.'

This opens an excellent opportunity to respond something like this: 'I'm happy to hear you know about _The Watchtower_. Undoubtedly you, like so many other people, enjoy the Scriptural comfort it offers. Its pages are filled with heart-cheering information of a new world of God's making now so close at hand. Jesus centered many illustrations around the new world for which he taught us to pray. One of them is found at Mark 4:14-20. There the Master said the word or truth was likened to seed that fell upon different types of soil. Some fell alongside the road where it could not grow; some upon rocky places where roots could not penetrate; some among the thorns only to be choked out by the cares of this world; and, most important, some fell upon soil that was right, took root, grew and bore fruit. We want to be of this last type of 'soil' and with this in view much

depends upon our study of God's Word, for Jesus said at John 17:3: \[Quote or read.] Inasmuch as this journal has aided so many to a clearer knowledge of the new world, I'm sure you would enjoy getting it regularly and benefiting too. For just one dollar you may have a year's subscription and these three booklets.'

With our current offer we meet some who say, 'I've subscribed for _The Watchtower_ before.' The good news may be presented to these individuals several ways. The new _Watchtower_ features may be pointed out, doctrinal or other questions cleared up, emphasis placed on the need for Bible knowledge, things of interest in the current magazine highlighted, etc. You might say: 'It certainly is a pleasure to talk to you. So many people today have little or no respect for God's teachings, neglecting almost completely his counsel to be an approved workman, as 2 Timothy 2:15 calls to our attention. \[Read or quote.] Having received _The Watchtower_ before, I'm sure you appreciate its value as a Bible study aid. The subscription is still only \$1, and, as you know, it will come to you twice a month. I'll be glad to serve you by taking your subscription now and leaving this issue with you. Soon the issues will be coming regularly to you through the mail.'

# YOUR SERVICE MEETING

## Preparing Three- to Eight-Minute Sermons

**SCENE I (3 min.)** After a morning of witnessing two brothers discuss their activity. Brother Alert enthusiastically relates to Brother Willing how much more effective his witnessing is by using three- to eight-minute sermons. It is like doing a new work. Willing quietly listens and then says he has not been giving a sermon, not knowing how it is to be done and does not feel capable. Alert suggests they spend a few minutes to see just how easy it is to develop a talk.

**SCENE II (12 min.)** Alert explains first that a theme is to be selected. Various themes may be used, such as "The New World," "The Importance of Bible Study," "The Kingdom, Man's Real Hope," "Christ's Purpose in Coming to Earth," etc. Then material is selected to support the theme and to give a witness. Willing interrupts to ask how this is done. Selecting "The New World" theme, Alert counsels that to be effective witness should have introduction, body and conclusion. In the introduction of your witness establish who you are and the purpose of your call. Then move to the body, mentioning troubled world conditions and need for a new world. Basic points could be: (1) old world cannot satisfy desire of man, (2) basis for hope in new world, (3) what is necessary to enter new world, and (4) need for "The Watchtower" in attaining hope. A supporting scripture or two woven into talk will increase effectiveness and add authority to witness. Matthew 24: 37 shows old-world conditions will pass away; John 3: 16 gives basis for new world; and Romans 12: 2 shows need for study. These simple basic points are noted and a mental outline formed to ensure directness at the door. In concluding witness restate the "Watchtower" offer. The various features of "The Watchtower" and its value in giving Scriptural help to understand God's purposes can be highlighted. Brother Willing now understands just what should be done, likes the idea and will try it himself. It is not as difficult as he had thought. He will develop several sermons for variety. In summary Alert reiterates need for thorough preparation to give a good witness, hold attention of householder, and arouse interest even though offer may not always be placed.

### Take Teaching

**SCENE I (4 min.)** Congregation servant, "Watchtower" study conductor and school servant discuss need of taking teaching from Jehovah. Do all associated with the congregation regularly attend meetings? Each one compares number attending meeting he oversees with number associated and adds appropriate comments. Why do some who could attend regularly fail to do so? Or prefer one meeting over another? At this time Brother Fair and congregation book study conductor enter Kingdom Hall. They decide to discuss the matter with Brother Fair, who does not attend regularly.

**SCENE II (12 min.)** Tactfully the matter of meeting attendance is approached. Fair replies he feels tired after work and needs some relaxation, so he cannot always get to ministry school regularly. School servant relates that teaching received directly from Bible is needed source of refreshment and prepares us to talk truth more fully to persons of good will. Congregation servant tactfully brings up matter of Fair sometimes leaving after

school. Fair explains he does not always stay since he always reads the "Informant" and likes to be home and retire early. On questioning him the congregation servant ascertains Fair does not retire very early even when leaving the meeting. He then points out that the service meeting is especially prepared to improve our ministry. House-to-house work must increase and we must more effectively minister to people desirous of fleeing to safety with the New World society.

Book study conductor then asks about attendance at his study. "Too busy these days," is Fair's reply. Conductor stresses advantage of group study and service. "Watchtower" conductor relates it is nice to have Fair attend "Watchtower" study after public talk. Fair says he does not always attend these meetings because he wants some time with his family and it is about the only chance he gets. Conductor reasons on importance of all in family attending lectures and "Watchtower" studies where chief instrument of "faithful and discreet slave" in ministering food is considered. "Watchtower" campaign demands that we know and appreciate its lifesaving message to help others. Fair is encouraged and expresses appreciation for counsel.

**SCENE III (4 min.)** After Fair leaves servants analyze and discuss: (1) Old-world system keeps publishers busy trying to make ends meet. (2) All must realize that while attending meetings and service keeps one busy, yet it is practical and best for Kingdom interests. (3) Meetings are for good of all, being God's provision to bless his people. (4) New-world discussion and association is relaxing as well as uplifting and should be enjoyed by all family members. (5) Servants must do their part by planning and conducting good, lively and instructive meetings, and publishers must do their part by regularly attending and encouraging others to do so.

# Engage in Pastoral Work with the Shepherd-Prince

## Start Organizing April Activity Now

$^{1}$ April will be a full, happy, busy month of theocratic activity for all who 'engage in pastoral work with the Shepherd-Prince.' (Ezek. 34: 23, 24) Memorial falls on Saturday the 17th, the new public talks start the 18th, all publishers should have engaged in house-to-house witnessing by the 18th. It is the final month of the _Watchtower_ campaign and each congregation will want to be able to say 'We made it!' for the 20 per cent increase. Briefly, that is our activity for April.

$^{2}$ Jehovah's people will arrange to attend and will invite persons of good will to attend the Memorial on Saturday and the public talk on Sunday. Additionally, special group witnessing will be arranged the 17th and 18th and encouragement will be given to all publishers and good-will persons to join in the activities. Between April 1 and 18 all should engage in house-to-house preaching. The total number who do is to be reported to the Society on the Memorial card. Congregation servants will plan a stimulating service meeting just before Memorial and outline local arrangements for one and all to

enjoy a grand week end of service.

$^{3}$ April brings to a close the _Watchtower_ campaign. By the end of the month will we in the United States have obtained 400,000 new subscriptions? It will be determined by preaching activity during the month. And during April assist all publishers to share in Kingdom service. With study conductors and ones privileged to train others diligently doing their part, and an invitation given to new ones to join, April will see a 20 per cent increase as all congregations will be able to write 'We made it!' on their report.

$^{4}$ By carrying out the above suggestions during April, it will be a full, happy, busy and blessed month for all.

## Subscription Renewals

$^{1}$ More subscription renewals are being received now than ever before. Chiefly, this is due to subscribers' being called upon and given an opportunity to renew their subscription before it expires. To promptly and efficiently handle these renewals and to take care of the thousands of new subscriptions pouring in daily, the Society's office will appreciate it if all comply with the following suggestions.

$^{2}$ EARLY RENEWALS: When submitting a renewal for a subscription that has not yet expired, fill out the subscription slip exactly as shown on the address label appearing on the magazine or wrapper. Mark the slip clearly as a 'renewal.'

$^{3}$ RENEWAL NOTICE: The Society inserts a renewal notice in the magazine when a subscription is about to expire. This slip should be used whenever possible. Attaching the address label from the magazine or the wrapper will be a help to the office.

$^{4}$ NEW SUBSCRIPTIONS: 'Insufficient Address,' 'No Such Post Office,' and 'Subscriber Unknown at This Address,' etc., are reasons magazines are returned, with great inconvenience to subscribers and added expense to the Society. So carefully fill in each subscription slip. Make certain the city or town in the subscriber's address is the post office town from which mail is received. Never abbreviate the name of the street or city and in larger cities always include zone numbers.

$^{5}$ If these suggestions are carefully followed, the net result will be time saved in handling renewal subscriptions, a minimum of complaints, and uninterrupted service assured to subscribers.

## CONGREGATION ORGANIZATION

### Service Meeting Preparation

$^{1}$ To unitedly 'preach the word' and continually improve ministerial activities, Jehovah's witnesses look forward to regularly attending and being edified from well-prepared service meetings. A great deal of thought, time, effort and co-operation are required for wholesome, instructive meetings to result.

$^{2}$ The congregation servant is responsible for planning instructive, well-balanced and interesting meetings. Upon receiving the _Informant_ he will study it thoroughly and then plan meetings for the entire month. The article dealing with ministerial work for the current month will be considered at the first meeting of the month, if at all possible, while the article dealing with the next month's activity will be considered at the last service meeting of the month. The _Yearbook_ and '_Make Sure of All Things_' will be considered in accord with instructions appearing in the January, 1954, _Informant_. Other _Informant_ articles and local ministerial matters to be considered may be worked into different weeks' programs where desired.

$^{3}$ To ensure coherence each meeting should be developed around a theme. Brothers preparing parts will have the theme in mind and work it into their part. The first meeting should highlight the current month's theme as outlined in the _Informant_, while succeeding program themes may be based upon the daily text or an appropriate Scriptural theme.

$^{4}$ Demonstrations, pantomimes, interviews, round-table discussions, quizzes, forums, dialogues, question-

question-and-answer discussions, service talks (using secondary _Watchtower_ articles, etc.) and other methods to present the material may be used. Generally a meeting will have three or four different parts, handled various ways for variety. Other servants may be consulted for program suggestions.

$^{5}$ It is a privilege to impart instructions to a congregation. Brothers will wisely accept each assignment and spend sufficient time preparing so proper edification will result. Every part should be rehearsed before it is presented and each should stay within his allotted time so the service meeting does not exceed one hour in length. While demonstrations, etc., may be lively, it should always be kept in mind that the purpose of a service meeting is to impart ministry instructions, not to entertain.

$^{6}$ Since everyone will want to contribute to meetings as opportunity affords, the next week's program should be announced at the end of each service meeting. Articles to be considered by discussion or other ways calling for congregation participation can thus be studied before the next meeting. Many will then be able to join in the discussion and contribute toward an instructive meeting.

$^{7}$ With the congregation servant planning instructive meetings, those assigned parts preparing properly and all associated attending regularly, the service meeting will provide instruction for all to more effectively 'preach the word' as Jehovah's ministers.
