## The London, England, International Assembly

To the great Wembley Stadium they came, thousands of them, from forty nations, eagerly, knowing that their days together would more than justify all the expenditure of effort, time and resources they made to be in London on the first five days of August. And why not? Had they not received through the organization of the new world society an invitation to back up by their presence a theocratic assembly? Important were previous Christian conventions in London; of outstanding importance they knew this one would be.

And it was! In its title is seen its theme: "The Clean Worship Assembly of Jehovah's Witnesses." Assurance, optimism, encouragement, these prevailed throughout the program. Practical were the many features relative to meetings, conduct, organization, ministry. Thrilling were the many reports from countries of Europe, Africa, Asia, the islands, the Americas. Devastating to demon religion was the resolving of the issues in the current fight to the finish between true and false worship.

# YOUR SERVICE MEETING

If new and irregular publishers are to make rapid progress and become regular and effective ministers, they must be instructed in what to say and how to say it, given an assignment and shown by example how to carry it out. This Jesus did for his new apostles and disciples. He told them where to go, told them what to say, and set them a proper example in the field. (Matt. 10: 5-7; Luke 4: 43; Luke 8: 1, NW) The following demonstration should show how to follow this Christian example now in helping our brothers become efficient and productive ministers.

**SCENE ONE:** Early Sunday morning a mature publisher calls for the one with whom he conducts a home Bible study to go in the field service. (4 minutes)

This newly interested one has not as yet engaged in the house-to-house work. Having this in mind, the mature publisher calls early in order that some time can be spent helping the new publisher prepare a testimony to use at the door during the morning's field service. Tactfully he helps him decide upon a point or two in the book that can be used in its presentation. (For example, the need people have for new world conditions described on pages 261-263 of the book "Let God Be True".) Thereafter they leave for the field to work in the mature publisher's territory.

**SCENE TWO:** Both publishers are in the field. (6 minutes)

Mature publisher suggests new publisher accompany him to door for first few calls. Mature publisher makes first presentation, leaving booklet with promise to call back, and then shows the new publisher how to use House to House Record, so he will know where to call back. Thereafter new publisher is encouraged to try the next door alone using presentation previously decided upon at home. He makes presentation, copying mature publisher's example, and, using points discussed earlier, he places book, and gets person's name and promises to call back. This scene should close with new publisher asking mature one to go with him when he makes the back-call.

**SCENE THREE:** Two weeks later. Both publishers are calling back on new publisher's book placement. (6 minutes)

Before approaching the door they decide to proceed on a definite course in their discussion. They choose to use the chapter "Who is Jehovah?" in the book "Let God Be True". When greeted by the householder, they are asked to step in for a few minutes. After a few friendly remarks, the conversation is directed to the book and it is found that the person has not read much of it but seemed to like it. Since the householder has no Bible question of his own, the new publisher raises the question as to who Jehovah is. This catches the interest of the householder. For the answer, they turn to pages 21 to 32 of the book. The householder makes mention of never having noticed the name Jehovah in the so-called "New Testament", and asks, "Why?" Paragraph 4 is discussed in answer. The mature publisher supports the argument with information found on pages 10-18 of the Foreword of the New World Translation. The back-call ends with the householder asking for a copy of the New World Translation, and arrangements are made to call back and further consider the book in a home Bible study.

## COMPANY ORGANIZATION

### The Stock Servant

$^{1}$ The company stock of books, booklets, Bibles and all other supplies except magazines, is under the supervision of the stock servant. He is to see that there is always sufficient literature on hand to supply the needs of the publishers for the gospel-preaching work.

$^{2}$ The stock servant will co-operate closely with the company servant in making out the company order for literature supplies. Frequent orders can be avoided by carefully keeping and checking the Progressive Inventory of stock. It is necessary to post this record only once a month. A separate sheet should be kept for each language.

$^{3}$ Stock received from the Society is to be opened and checked by the stock servant against the order and invoice received. If invoices do not check, the Society should be advised of the discrepancy by the company servant within five days after receipt of the shipment. An actual-count inventory is to be taken twice a year, September 1 and March 1; but only on the first day of the service year (September 1) will each stock servant fill out and return the inventory form supplied by the Society at such time.

$^{4}$ The stock servant should see to it that the stockroom is open approximately twenty minutes before

and after meetings for the benefit of publishers desiring literature and should be on time to care for the publishers' needs. At times it may be necessary for the stock servant to issue a small amount of literature to publishers on credit. However, additional credit should not be extended until payment has been made on the literature previously obtained on credit. Publisher's Order Blanks are used to keep a record of the movement of literature. Money taken in by the stock servant should be turned over to the accounts servant at least once a week.

$^{5}$ An estimate of literature needs should be made by the stock servant well in advance of campaigns. Likewise, he should consult with the pioneers on their literature needs when ordering. He will also co-operate with the accounts servant to keep a proper record of literature placed with pioneers and list the totals in duplicate on the Literature Credit Request form. The original, properly signed and accurately checked, will be mailed to the Society and proper credit will be given the company.

$^{6}$ With the stock servant faithfully discharging his responsibility in caring for the company stock and publishers' needs, the company organization will function smoothly and effectively to the accomplishment of Jehovah's will and pleasure.
