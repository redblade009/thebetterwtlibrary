# YOUR SERVICE MEETING

Circuit and district assemblies are means that the Lord is using to assemble his people for vital Kingdom instruction so necessary during these last days. A great deal of time, money, and effort are spent in arranging and preparing assemblies and, for publishers to derive full benefit, they must properly prepare to attend and conduct themselves fittingly while there. The following material emphasizes proper publisher preparation, consideration for the landlady and conduct at the assembly. To cover this material as outlined, twenty to twenty-five minutes should be devoted to it at a service meeting.

FIRST SCENE: (Part one) By pantomime illustrate the basic steps each publisher should take in preparing for an assembly.

Publisher has Room Request form and is in the process of filling it out. Care is taken to supply all requested information, such as full name and address, type of room requested and amount desired to be paid, date of arrival and length of stay, etc. Upon completion it is rechecked and mailed. Receiving the rooming assignment a few days later, time is taken to write the landlady and confirm date of arrival and intended time of arrival. Next comes personal organization. Proper clothing is selected, appropriate literature, book and magazine bags are put in order, duties requiring one's attention before leaving are cared for, room assignment card is thoughtfully put in with those things to be carried to the assembly, such as songbook, etc.

(Part two) Show arrival of publisher in assembly city at a reasonable hour on day before assembly.

Upon arrival the publisher, with suitcase and book bag, goes directly to assigned room, introduces himself in a friendly manner to the landlady, and is directed to his room. Showing consideration for the landlady and wanting to be fully rested so as to get the most from the next day's assembly schedule, the publisher retires for the night.

SECOND SCENE: Witnessing to the landlady. (Arrange as a demonstration.)

Many landladies are interested in Jehovah's witnesses, want to know what kind of work we are doing, what we believe, etc., and open their homes to Jehovah's witnesses with this in mind. Understanding this, on the following evening the alert publisher utilizes an opportunity to witness. A program is produced, future assembly activity is highlighted, particularly the public meeting, and an offer is made to have the landlady and her household attend assembly sessions with the publisher. Continuing, the witness tells of an experience had in the field service there in the assembly city. The scene will end with the publisher leaving with the landlady the particular booklet, magazine or book which covers the point of discussion raised in the experience related.

THIRD SCENE: Forum discussion covering Christian conduct at an assembly.

Two or three publishers should be assigned to cover the following points, and in their discussion they should point out the benefits all receive by adhering to these principles: 1. Be on time for and attend all sessions. 2. Show neighbor love by not talking and distracting others during the meetings. 3. Do not rudely leave sessions before being dismissed or while prayer is being said. 4. Visit before and after sessions (not during). 5. Volunteer for assembly work. 6. Have a part in field service; help others attend. Turn in good-will slips and subscriptions at assembly city; field service reports to home company. 7. Arrive at your rooms at a reasonable hour each evening.

# "That the Ministry Be Not Blamed"

# Personal Appearance Important

When a person calls at another's door, displays magazines on a street corner or stands upon a platform to deliver a talk, people reach conclusions about him before he even speaks. This is a natural tendency, and even the Scriptures note that "man looks upon the outward appearance". A poor appearance im-

presses men that the speaker and his message are not important or worthy of serious concern. Thus, one who is indifferent about his appearance and dress may unwittingly close many minds against the truth even while zealously engaging in field service. It is very difficult to remove these first impressions, however incorrect they may later prove to be.

It was with good reason, then,

that Isaiah instructed, "Be ye clean, that bear the vessels of the LORD." Baggy, unpressed trousers, grimy, wrinkled shirts or dresses, or unshined shoes are no aid to us as we bear the Kingdom message. Though not necessarily fancy or expensive, clothing should always be neat and clean, befitting the message we bear.

Personal cleanliness is likewise important. An ambassador of the Theocracy will not have unkempt hair, unshaven face or dirty hands. Such things nullify the good effects of the comforting words we speak.

By far the majority of the servants of God do observe the simple rules of cleanliness. But since people are prone to judge all by the one who may not, it is essential for all to be equally diligent in this important matter. We are called upon to reflect the light and glory of Jehovah. Neat appearance, clothes pressed, shoes shined reflect cleanliness and enlightenment and confidence. Pause a moment each time before going into the service and ascertain if these things have been taken care of. It is a good investment to be clean. It will make your work doubly effective, and you will be conforming to the apostle's counsel. "Giving no offence in any thing, that the ministry be not blamed." —2 Cor. 6:3.

# COMPANY ORGANIZATION

## The Company Committee

1 Sometimes it is necessary to settle matters in the company upon which no specific instructions have been given in Counsel on Theocratic Organization. These matters must be decided upon by the company committee representing the congregation. The company committee, comprised of the company servant, assistant company servant and the Bible study servant, has the responsibility of handling such matters in a systematic and orderly way. Wherever possible, the committee should be composed of three different publishers, and should be the most mature in the company and the logical ones for the company servant to consult when he needs advice or counsel on such matters.

2 The committee will consider and care for all incidental company matters such as the advisability of dividing the company into units, how much rent should be paid for halls, what halls should be rented, what should be done with excess money, how much money should be spent on legal matters approved by the Society, how to minister spiritual and physical aid to the poor and infirm in the congregation, when needed, and any other similar matters relative to company affairs and the service. It will also be the duty of the committee to make recommendations for servants to the Society, in accordance with paragraph 82 of Counsel on Theocratic Organization. All such recommendations should be signed by each member of the committee. The recommendations must be impartial and always for the best interests of the company. Publishers should not be recommended until they have published for one full year after immersion.—See Philippians 2:3,4; James 2:1-9; and 1 Timothy 3:2-10, NW.

3 It must be remembered that the company servant takes full oversight in the company and that the other members of the committee are to work closely with him in supervising the company's activities. Decisions made by the committee relative to company matters will be presented to the company when necessary by the company servant. The committee will meet only when necessary.

4 When difficulties arise in the company because of an individual's conduct and the congregation and persons of good will are affected, the Bible places the responsibility for handling such matters in the hands of the "older men", and such difficulties may be handled by the company committee or, if assigned, other mature brothers who are Scripturally qualified. If the company servant feels it advisable, he may select two mature brothers other than those on the company committee to handle these particular difficulties. This assigned committee, after hearing all the evidence and witnesses, can recommend whether the guilty individuals should be removed from their servants' positions, disfellowshipped, or just counseled and given a chance to prove their repentance and good intentions. If a disfellowshipped person has repented, and shown this by right works over a period of time, such committee can recommend his acceptance back into the congregation.

5 The company committee should always be willing to receive suggestions from the publishers and give them careful consideration, and should be at unity and in harmony so that it can carry out its duties to the honor and glory of Jehovah's name.
