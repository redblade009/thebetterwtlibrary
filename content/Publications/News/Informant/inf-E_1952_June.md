## Around the World with Jehovah's Witnesses

### Public Meetings

Christians today follow the example of Paul in making use of every opportunity to give a public witness. (Acts 17:16-34) Particularly should we be alert in this regard as we work the isolated territories.

We may not be able to obtain a public meeting place, but what of that? A small company in Austria held a public meeting in an isolated town, where, due to clergy opposition, only four attended. Unable to procure the hall for further meetings they held the next meeting out of doors. Eighty attended that meeting.—See 'Yearbook', page 84.

Nor should we feel that the effort will not be worth while just because we may not have much time to advertise the talk. In one isolated town in Nicaragua, two brothers had just two hours to advertise the public talk to be held in a home that evening. By the time the conclusion of the talk was given 125 were listening, the meeting having spilled over into the street. (183, 184) And for an example of results of a public meeting held in a home see report on El Salvador. (135)

Nor should we let opposition to our public meetings discourage us. If we press the battle to the gate we may expect the Lord to give us the victory as was the case in Costa Rica where a public meeting in a park was broken up by a priest-led mob. By not surrendering to the enemy the meeting was held the following week with police protection, and 150 heard the lecture. (114-116)

### Expressing Appreciation

Added privileges of service, such as witnessing in isolated territory, usually mean added burdens and even hardships. But dare we consider these burdens too great to be borne in view of what our brothers put up with in other parts of the world?—Job 23:12; Ps. 84:10.

Jehovah's witnesses in Thailand continue in the service during the rainy season even though, when at its height, they have to use oxcarts and small boats, walk barefoot through streets that are seas of mud and then wade knee-deep in water to reach homes. (235, 236) Working our isolated territory may also pose travel problems, muddy streets, etc., but surely that should not discourage us.

And although our brothers in Korea, due to war conditions, experience great difficulty in getting even the bare necessities of life, their chief concern, as our 'Yearbook' shows, is not for the material, but for the spiritual food, so that they can continue on in the work. (71, 72) And how the Lord is blessing that appreciation! ('The Watchtower', April 1, 1952) In caring for the isolated territory, sandwiches may have to take the place of Sunday dinners, but if we show the same appreciation that our brothers in Korea do, may we not expect similar blessings?

In Taiwan (Formosa) a newly interested person kept going ahead in Jehovah's service even though mobsters had damaged his farm and stolen his farm animals because he stopped worshiping idols. (167) It may be that a like intolerance and violence will greet our activity in isolated assignments. But that should not cause us to stop preaching, for how do we know but that there are some of the Lord's sheep scattered in among such goats?

# YOUR SERVICE MEETING

The prisoners living in unassigned territory urgently need a strong refuge. They need to forsake the failing and inadequate organizations of men. Undoubtedly a number of these will respond to the call to freedom and will eventually hail the King. The following demonstrations will visibly call to our attention the urgency and details of how we can aid these persons. Companies that have no isolated assignments may adapt these demonstrations to apply to their unworked territory or even to unworked personal territories held by publishers.

**Scene I: (3 minutes)** A man is talking to his wife about summer isolated territory campaign, which had been featured on company's last service meeting. He argues he can't see why publishers should put themselves out so much when the company can't get around its territory more than once a year now. Also, he has worked hard all year and when vacation time comes he wants to be on his own and rest. He'd like a vacation at the seashore, or to get off to himself away from people for a while. _Besides, his own personal territory could stand more working, and if he wants to put in a day or two of his vacation there he can do that. Wife goes to cook supper and he falls asleep and dreams._

# COMPANY ORGANIZATION

## Isolated Publisher

1 An isolated publisher is one who does not live in company territory and is unable to regularly associate with a company because of extreme distance. Isolated publishers are part of Jehovah's theocratic organization, sharing in its privileges and responsibilities. Therefore, the Society desires to assist them in every way possible to care for Kingdom interests in their locality.

2 Wherever practical, the Society will assign the territory in which an isolated publisher or group lives to the nearest company, even though it may be some distance away. This arrangement is made to encourage attendance at company meetings as often as possible so they might regularly receive beneficial counsel, help and encouragement for their advancement in the ministry. If necessary, other meetings, or at least a Watchtower study, can be established by the company for the benefit of these publishers. The company can also supply them with the Informant, mailing it if necessary, in addition to literature and other witnessing equipment. They should be kept informed of the circuit servant's visit, the circuit assembly and other company activities. Their field service reports are to be turned in to the company. Under this arrangement, they will be taken off the isolated publisher list and will be recognized as part of the company.

3 Where it is impractical for isolated publishers to associate with or report to a company, the Society will assist them directly, providing them with the Informant, literature at company rates and report cards which should be mailed directly to the Society at the end of each month, and will include the combined report of others if there is a group. Where there is a group, the most active

and capable publisher should be selected to communicate with the Society and the Society advised to that effect. Even though they are not organized as a company, they should have regular meetings and service arrangements. When visiting the nearest company, the circuit servant will, if possible, take a day or so to assist the isolated publishers, and make a report to the Society. He can ascertain the prospects of forming a company in that area and, if advisable, a company application with its recommendations can be sent to the Society for its consideration. The one recommended as company servant, to qualify, must have symbolized his dedication by water immersion, be able to set a proper example and be willing to carry out instructions. If no one is qualified to serve as company servant, then the group should continue the existing arrangement until a company can be organized.

4 Where a pioneer has developed an isolated group and believes that a company can be organized, he may fill out and submit a company application form to the Society's office. Should an isolated group feel able to function as a company, the most qualified publisher of the group may write and explain their situation to the Society's office, request a company application form, fill it out and then return it. Pioneer help should not be requested as a condition in order to be organized into a company.

5 As the Kingdom work continues to expand, many who are now isolated publishers will be organized into company groups, sharing the blessings of that arrangement, and contributing with greater effectiveness to the expansion of Jehovah's work to his praise.
