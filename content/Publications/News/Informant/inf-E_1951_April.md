### Literature Orders

Many companies are continually changing the address to which their literature orders are shipped. This causes much confusion, loss of time and delay in handling orders. Oftentimes the address of the Kingdom Hall is used, with the result that freight companies are unable to deliver the orders because no one is there to receive the shipment; or it is necessary to give two addresses, one for shipping and another for notification. For these reasons it is better to use _one address_ that can be used both for freight and mail. We would prefer that all orders be sent to the name and address of the company servant, for then we can use the address stencil that is in our files.

If you must have your shipments sent to someone other than the company servant, we suggest that the _name_ and _address_ of some other servant be selected who can conveniently receive _all_ orders. Then use this same address each time you order literature. Your co-operation on these points will facilitate handling your orders.

# YOUR SERVICE MEETING

As all features of Kingdom service increase in tempo, more and more good-will persons are being found and many new faces appear at the Kingdom Hall. These strangers, present for the first time, are not acquainted with God's people as a group and are observing very intently what goes on. Their first impression of the organization is usually a lasting one. For that reason, it is necessary to know how to treat these strangers when they come to our meetings. This demonstration should show how to treat them before, during and after the meeting.

**FIRST SCENE:** Two publishers are conversing just before the meeting is to begin. A person walks in the door and takes a seat in the back row, all by himself. The two publishers notice him.

This scene should demonstrate how a stranger should be greeted when he first enters the hall. The two publishers can discuss whether or not they are acquainted with him and one will decide that he will go and find out who the stranger is. He goes over to the stranger and introduces himself in a friendly manner and engages him in a conversation. The publisher then explains that this is the Kingdom Hall where Jehovah's witnesses meet to study God's Word together, and offers to show him around. Then he can show him the various features of the Kingdom Hall, such as the yeartext, the library, the stock counter where literature can be obtained, the advertising department, etc., explaining briefly the functions of each one. Then it will be time for the meeting to begin and they will sit down.

**SECOND SCENE:** The publisher is seated beside the stranger as the meeting progresses.

Since it is the service meeting, various publications will be studied, and, of course, the stranger has none of them. So as each one is studied, the publisher should offer to let the stranger look on with him. (That is one of the reasons why he sat beside the stranger, because he knew that the stranger would not be prepared for the meeting and would feel quite helpless without some assistance.) The stranger appreciates this very much because now he knows what is going on and he benefits from the program.

**THIRD SCENE:** The meeting is over and the publisher is again talking with the stranger. He proceeds to introduce him to the servants, procures an 'Informant' for him, and then introduces him to other publishers in the hall.

Those to whom the stranger is introduced should accept the introduction warmly and in a friendly manner. Finally, before the stranger leaves, the publisher arranges for him to return again, informs him of the times of other meetings and tells him of the programs for such meetings. Getting the stranger acquainted with the publishers and the meetings is a very important part of welcoming him.

**GENERAL POINTS:** It should be kept in mind that a friendly, receptive spirit should prevail throughout. The stranger should be made to feel right at home, however not made to feel too conspicuous, but readily accepted into our midst. The stranger will feel like returning again, and in an atmosphere such as this, he will begin to take in life-giving knowledge from the table of Jehovah. A stranger should be a stranger only once, and that once should be only on his first visit.

## Do Not Use U.S. Mailboxes

From time to time it has been called to the attention of the Society by Post Office officials that publishers, when in the witness work, leave handbills and other advertising material in mail- and letterboxes. This is illegal and contrary to postal regulations and is punishable by a fine of \$300. Mail- and letterboxes are for the use of mailmen, and only for matter on which postage has been paid. It would be appreciated if all publishers would remember this fact and refrain from leaving handbills, publications, or other printed matter in mail- or letterboxes at any time.

## The Bible Study Servant

1 The privilege and responsibility of the Bible study servant is to oversee and care for the back-call and Bible study activity. He will set a proper example by engaging in the back-call and Bible study work, while his chief concern will be to assist other publishers in making back-calls and starting and conducting home Bible studies.

2 The Bible study servant can ascertain which publishers need assistance by regularly checking the Publisher's Record cards. He will endeavor to tactfully aid his fellow worker by offering to take him along in the back-call work, show him how it is done and aid him in starting home Bible studies. Assistance to the publishers in larger companies can be rendered by the Bible study servant's working through the company book study arrangement, rotating from group to group.

3 In large companies it would be well for the Bible study servant to have an assistant so that he can have more opportunity to work with the publishers. He will also enlist the aid of other publishers and pioneers who are active and efficient in the back-call and Bible study work. The Bible study servant may assign these to assist the various publishers needing aid, and thus make them their personal charge until they have been sufficiently trained.

4 A Study Record file will be kept by the Bible study servant, which

will include a record of all the home Bible studies held by both the publishers and pioneers, as well as each of the company meetings. The Bible study servant will receive the Study Reports from the assistant company servant and will post the information from them on the Study Record slips, making out a new Study Record slip for any new studies started. The Study Record slip need no longer be filed under the territory number, but can be filed alphabetically according to the name of the householder, if this is more advantageous in your company. However, the territory number will continue to be recorded, though filed alphabetically. If a publisher is unable to continue a study and cannot find another to take care of it, then the Bible study servant should be informed of this so he can arrange for another publisher to care for it. He will also work closely with the territory servant, furnishing any information regarding studies and good-will data that may be necessary, when territory is assigned.

5 With the Bible study servant diligently and systematically fulfilling his responsibility of giving loving assistance to his fellow publishers, and all the publishers in turn cooperating with him in the same spirit, Jehovah's organization will continue to be blessed with theocratic increase.
