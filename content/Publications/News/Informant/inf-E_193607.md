## NAME

With God's children there should be nothing in a name to furnish an excuse for the exaltation of men. Some persons appear to gladly seize upon the meaning that may be placed upon a name as an excuse for self-exaltation or for unnecessary criticism of others.

The name "Director", by which this paper has been known for some time, means in the English merely one who transmits information concerning the directing or instructions relative to field service work. In some languages, however, the word when translated appears to have a different meaning. For instance, the name "Director" translated in one language is given the meaning of "Commander, Big boss, or Important one". All such names are displeasing to the Lord and should be avoided. For reasons assigned, this paper hereafter will be designated as INFORMANT. That name means merely one who or that which gives information. The purpose of this paper is to give information to the field workers about the work and the manner in which it is to be done, so that all may work harmoniously. When you receive your copy you may expect to find therein up-to-date information given out by the Society organization for the benefit of those who do the publishing work concerning the Kingdom.

When a man desires to be approved of men, the Devil seizes upon that selfish desire to induce such a one to exalt himself and thus cause friction in the Lord's organization. It seems best, therefore, that the following changes should be made in the names or titles of positions held in the organization, to wit: Instead of using the name or title "company director", hereafter that position will be known as "company servant"; instead of using the name "regional service director", hereafter that position will be known as "regional servant"; instead of "divisional leader", hereafter that position will be known as "divisional servant". Remember the words of the Lord Jesus: 'For one is your Master, even Christ, and whoever shall exalt himself shall be abased.' (Matt. 23: 9-13) All the true followers of Christ Jesus are servants. There is no excuse or justification to exalt one above another. To whatsoever position one in the Lord's organization is assigned, he should serve obediently and joyfully and without murmuring, complaining, or fault-finding.

Where a number of companies are working together, for instance, such as the area of Greater New York or London, the person who takes the lead and transmits the instructions received, instead of being known as "service director", will hereafter be known as "servant of companies", and the servant of the separate companies in this organization will be known as "captain of service".

Hereafter none of the servants in the field will be known as "sharpshooters". That is eliminated, and all such workers are considered as companies or integral parts of companies. If there is just one isolated person in the community, instead of being known as "sharpshooter", he is merely a servant, meaning, of course, servant of the Lord.

Let us see to it that no excuse is furnished for self-exaltation of any creature. We are all brethren in Christ and delight to serve one Master and one God. All at unity in Christ will work exactly in harmony and will be willing and anxious to carry out the information received through the INFORMANT, or other means that proceeds from the proper source.

Heretofore the Society's representative at a branch office has been known as "manager" or "director". Hereafter such representatives will be known as "branch servants", meaning the representative of the Society at a branch office, acting with authority from the main office of the Society and, of course, as servant of the Lord. All branch offices, please be governed accordingly.

## New Phonograph

### Weighs Only 9¾ Pounds

The use of the phonograph is becoming more pronounced every day. Its use in the field is bringing splendid results and presenting clearly to many of the "great multitude" the truths which sweep away the refuge of lies. Do you have one? and are you using it?

The Society has just completed a newly designed phonograph, the size of which is 14 x 13 x 5 inches, covered with brown cloth and having rounded corners. Its weight is 9 pounds 13 ounces, and one can carry three discs. With the three discs it weighs 12 pounds 5 ounces. This machine is made very compact, which could be accomplished only by use of a removable tone arm. It is a very simple matter to attach this in its proper position, and it is ready for use immediately. The volume is just as strong as of the previous model, and its tone is of the best quality. When the machines are sent out from here they are timed properly, at 75 r.p.m. Do not change this.

The phonograph is being placed with the company publishers and auxiliaries with three discs on a contribution of $10.00. If you already have records and prefer this phonograph it can be had for $8.00. Special rates are made for pioneers. Order your phonograph now. Keep them busy in your regular door-to-door witnessing, back calls and loaning to parties who will use them extensively. Have in mind that this new phonograph is 5½ pounds lighter than our last one.

# “Riches” First Week’s Placements

## Surpasses Any Previous Distribution of Books

### Publishers Increased; Hours Dropped

The result of “His Strange Work” testimony period has been waited for with anticipation by the brethren everywhere. Herewith find a comparison between “His Strange Work” testimony period of April this year and the “Anointed’s Thanksgiving” period, of April last year.

The _Riches_ book went splendidly. It has exceeded any other single bound-book distribution we have ever had by over 35,000. In fact, the _INCREASE_ in _Riches_ placements over its closest competitor, _Preparation_, was greater than the _TOTAL_ placements for special periods a few years back. We placed 140,491 _Riches_ during that week, to compare with 104,553 _Preparation_. The figures in this comparison speak for themselves.

Let each one of the anointed and their companions, however, study them very carefully, compare them with their own personal activity and see how the Lord really blessed them by permitting them to have a part in contributing to this splendid increase, and also let each one examine himself and see to what extent he was responsible for the decrease in time spent in the service. Even though there were 611 more publishers

ers in the field during “His Strange Work” period, this year, than there were during “The Anointed’s Thanksgiving” period, of April last year, we are 10,521 hours short of last year. In other words, the average number of hours per publisher dropped from 11.3 hours last year to 10.3 hours this year. That drop, on an average of one hour per publisher, meant just 13,500 _Riches_ books. In other words, if we had maintained last year’s average, each publisher putting in 11.3 hours, we should have 13,500 more _Riches_ in the hands of the people than were placed. There is much food for serious thought here on the part of each and every one of us.
