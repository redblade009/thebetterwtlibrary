## Around the World

with Jehovah's Witnesses

### Young and Old Can Preach

It was predicted that Jehovah's spirit would move many to praise his name in the last days, including young persons and those old in years. (Joel 2:28; Acts 2:16-18, NW) In Poland we find this fulfilled, even under difficult conditions, as shown by the "Yearbook" report, in publishers as old as 100 and as young as 16 years. (249) Are you too old to imitate a 74-year-old pioneer in the Bahamas who devotes 90 to 130 hours to preaching each month? (103) Think of the 75-year-old publisher in North Borneo who conducts a regular home Bible study. (257) Do you sometimes feel that people to whom you witness are too old to get the truth and rejoice in it? Examine the report from Venezuela and ask yourself whether good-will persons with whom you study are older than the woman mentioned. (284)

And what about youth? Well, persons take notice that the organization abounds in young people today, as evidenced in the report from the British Isles. (117) Also, young folks in the truth are more than listeners: they are workers. Consider the young publisher who combines witnessing with her school activities in Guadeloupe. (77) Even a 13-year-old house-to-house publisher is not abashed at meeting persons with strong but misguided feelings against Jehovah's people. Have you ever been confronted with a situation like this young publisher in Finland? (158) Young ministers are ready to give an effective witness no matter what the occasion, as demonstrated by the 16-year-old publisher in Fiji who spoke to 40 people. (96)

All persons, the very young and those very old, can have part in preaching the good news regularly, if they wish to show love for others as Jehovah has shown love for them.

### Incidental Witnessing

While everyone should take part in house-to-house preaching, publishers can often supplement their regular activity by taking advantage of opportunities for incidental witnessing. Often excellent results follow. Consider the "Yearbook" report on the brother in Brazil who took opportunity to witness while delivering coal. He started a study. Soon, because of the attendance, it was made a congregation study; and, finally, it reached a climax with 100 persons in attendance. (110, 111) Incidental witnessing to one person will often open up the way to talk to many other persons, as shown by the report from Ecuador. (148)

Did you ever consider handing a tract to your storekeeper? See what happened in El Salvador when a sister did this, incidental to making a purchase. (156) Brothers who have secular occupations that keep them on the move from place to place can also find opportunities to preach. (133, 184) Sometimes a publisher's occupation brings him in contact with people only too glad to have something to read, as reported from the Netherlands West Indies. (217) Incidental witnessing to employers, coupled with faithful service to them, can produce good results, too, as shown in Southern Rhodesia. (268)

Bear in mind that there will be many opportunities for incidental witnessing while traveling to and from the world assembly in July. Will you be prepared to seize opportunities that present themselves? Be sure to report time spent in incidental preaching.

# YOUR SERVICE MEETING

Publishers should manifest a positive, optimistic attitude relative to attending the world assembly, realizing its importance and encouraging one another and good-will persons to attend, as shown in the first demonstration below. The second demonstration emphasizes carefulness to details in applying for rooms and the proper thoughtful attitude toward the householder with whom you will stay in New York.

**SCENE I (3 min.)** Publisher calling on good-will person encourages attendance at world assembly. He shows 1950 convention report and demonstrates enthusiasm for forthcoming assembly in New York. Good-will person, interested, asks about rooms, transportation. Publisher helps by showing Room Request form, then offers a place in his car for trip to and from New York, stating the cost for traveling.

**SCENE II (3 min.)** At congregation meeting good-will person talks to publisher about possibility of attending the assembly, but this publisher has negative attitude. "What!" says Negative, "travel all the way to New York when a little later on you will get the same things right here at home? I was there last time and once is enough for me. That city is too big and too crowded, and I just simply can't find my way around by traveling in the subways like a mole! No, sir, not for me this time!" Good-will person is taken aback by negative talk. Congregation servant learns of incident and decides to give talk at next service meeting for benefit of the few who lack full appreciation.

**SCENE III (8 min.)** Servant, in talk at service meeting, shows purpose of assemblies and how necessary it is to hear Jehovah's call to assemble. He recalls Israelite nation, with God's commands to attend assemblies, not once but regularly. They made advance preparation and put money aside to attend. (Deut. 14: 22-26) They had farms and cattle to leave in the care of someone, but arrangements were made so they could go to Jerusalem. Children were taken along, even though primitive travel methods meant long, hard journeys. (2 Chron. 30: 5-11) But all of Jehovah's people arranged to attend and marvelous blessings resulted. (2 Chron. 30: 21-27; 31: 1) Think, too, how happy the 3,000 were who were present at the assembly at Pentecost. What if they had missed? —Acts 2: 41, NW.

**SCENE IV (4 min.)** Publisher talks to good-will person as they ride home from meeting. He briefly refutes objections that negative publisher brought up and outlines proper viewpoint toward the world assembly. He points out that one must plan to get greatest spiritual benefit while at convention by attending all sessions. It is illogical to make long trip to New York and then not attend the sessions of the assembly. If he wishes to see other sights, he can do this before or after the convention. Good-will person agrees that vacation time and money will certainly be spent best by attending world assembly.

**SCENE I (10 min.)** Brothers Jones and Brown meet at the Kingdom Hall. Brown has just received his room assignment card and is quite elated. He says, "We are all ready for the assembly now, because we have received our room assignment. I hope this one turns out as well as the one in 1950." He tells of the good time had talking to householder every evening after sessions and how impressed she was on being taken to public meeting. "You know we corresponded

quite a long time after the 1950 assembly. In one letter she told of someone calling on her. A study was started and now she is attending meetings. So you can see why I'm so anxious to meet the people I shall stay with. At my earliest convenience I'm going to write and let them know we received this assignment in their home for the eight days, and that we plan to arrive on Saturday afternoon, July 18, and are looking forward to meeting them. The telephone number is on this card, too, so I plan to call her when I arrive in New York. Say, Joe, what is the matter with you? You look sort of glum about something."

Joe answers, "Well, I got a card too, but not that kind. Mine is a card marked 'INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION'. Apparently I did not take enough time to fill out my Room Request form, because this card shows I failed to give the date I expect to arrive in New York, and I did not check the price I desire to pay for a room." Brother Brown looks at the card and says, "You will have to write to the rooming office right away and give them the necessary information. Did you see this notation too? It says to enclose this card with your letter." Joe, discouraged, says, "I guess it will take two weeks or more now before I get my room assignment. Oh my, and we wanted to be together too, but no doubt this will mean we'll have to be separated."

Brother Brown assures him not to worry about that part, because it probably will be necessary to use the subway anyway to get to and from the stadium. "I remember three years ago the Society suggested we use the subway because of the extremely limited parking space around the stadium. You know, when I got on one of those things I had no idea where I was going. Everyone seemed to be racing somewhere, and no one paid any attention to me. I finally asked a man how to get to the stadium. He stopped and very pleasantly gave me the directions. So, no matter where we stay, we can just arrange to meet at some location in the stadium. You remember that we both volunteered to work in the salad-making department of the cafeteria, so we will see each other there."

They both call their householders and are directed to their respective homes, one to the Bronx and the other to Brooklyn. Publishers meet householders with very pleasant and friendly greetings and briefly discuss arrangements for coming and going each day.

## New Report Slip for Congregation Publishers

With this _Informant_ you will find several specimen copies of a new report slip to be used by congregation publishers in reporting their field service. This slip is designed to show the publisher's activity for an entire week, running from Monday morning to Sunday evening. Entries can be made daily, and then the slip can be totaled by the publisher and turned in Sunday evening. Activity for each month will be kept separate, however. For example: June ends on Tuesday the 30th. A slip will be made out for Monday and Tuesday, June 29 and 30. But a separate slip will be made out for Wednesday to Sunday, July 1 to 5, so that activity for July will not be included in the June report of the congregation.

Publishers are encouraged to report field service once a week as far as possible, for this will make it much more convenient to total the congregation report. Of course, a publisher may turn in a report for one day's service at the end of that day if he wishes to do so, using this new slip. It is suited for daily reporting as well as weekly, but weekly reporting is encouraged. However, it is always better to get one report than none at all; therefore, do not be dogmatic in insisting that publishers use it weekly.

Inasmuch as there are quantities of the old field service report slip (S-4) on hand in the congregations these should, to avoid waste, be used up before the new slip is put to use. The Society will continue to send out supplies of the old slip now on hand until these are gone. Advance notice is given now of the new S-4 slip so that publishers will know how to use it.
