## "Theocracy's Increase" Witnessing Period

Introduce All to the King Through MAGAZINES

### Increased Praise Schedule

"Jerusalem shall be inhabited like unwalled villages, because of the mass of men and beasts within her." (Zech. 2: 4, AT; Watchtower, January 1, 1951) In these last days Jehovah God has built up his organization, Jerusalem, and brought about Theocracy's increase. But people in the 'great city' are not idle! They continue to spread out and preach the message of God's kingdom to the ends of the earth so that all shall know that Jehovah is the Supreme One of the universe and that the vindication of his name and word is near at hand. Being citizens of the new world organization, let us share in praising our God and King by offering subscriptions for the Watchtower and Awake! magazines, with the premium.

Our house-to-house work will continue as a regular feature of our service activity. But there is also the responsibility of calling back on all persons who showed interest by taking a copy of the magazine, booklet or book and those who expressed a desire to subscribe. Caring for these calls and conducting home Bible studies are activities of vital importance in the Lord's work. That this has the Lord's blessing is proved by the tremendous growth of the 'unwalled city'. Results go hand in hand with such efforts in cultivating the field of God.

When obtaining subscriptions or otherwise finding interested persons we should be diligent to accept the responsibility of caring for these sheeplike ones. We should make a complete record of interest found and then water and nourish this interest for continued increase. Regular calls on these persons, designed to increase their knowledge of the Creator and his purposes and to bring them to full-bodied maturity, need to be made.

### Increased Activity

To properly care for our God-given responsibility of 'feeding the sheep' group contacts should be well organized. It has been found that

9:30 a.m. Sunday is the best time for the publishers to assemble for group service. Discussion of the daily text and a consideration of the activity for the day should start promptly at the appointed time. Brief quizzes, discussions, experiences, demonstrations, or reviews, based on previous service meetings, may be prepared and presented for refreshment and stimulation before going afield. The entire meeting need be no longer than 15 minutes, thereby making it possible for all to be in the field by 10:00 a.m. for a full day of service.

Many publishers have found it practical to start the day's activity with a couple of hours of house-to-house activity, eat a lunch while still in the field and then continue into the afternoon by calling back on persons who previously displayed interest and starting and conducting home Bible studies.

So prepare for and take an active part in Theocracy's increase now by uniting yourself with your brothers world-wide in this great witness work.

## Around the World

with Jehovah's Witnesses

### "The Watchtower" Aids Men

of Good Will

Second only to the Bible itself is the place "The Watchtower" occupies in the hearts and minds of the servants of Jehovah. It has been stressed so much by them as to be synonymous with them and their work.

The report on Canada appearing in the 1952 "Yearbook" calls attention to how greatly improved "The Watchtower" is in its new form: "convenient size, the rich variety of subjects covered, the interest-sripping headings and illustrations, and the satisfying answers to the readers' questions." Appreciating these points will help us in our presentation. (106)

The United States report tells of a mother of 17 children who, as the result of reading "The Watchtower" over a period of years, wrote the Society for instructions on how she too could have a part in the witness work. (61) But would it have taken years for that mother to see her privileges of service if someone had studied "The Watchtower" with her? The report from Turkey tells of two women of good will becoming publishers within one month of the time one of Jehovah's witnesses began a study with one of them on the illustration of the sower. (237, 238)

"The Watchtower" also helps men of good will to see what are God's righteous requirements. Certain "church" members in British Guiana took "The Watchtower" on "Marriage" to their clergyman, in defense of a person divorced on Scriptural grounds who wanted to remarry. They won their argument. (94) What a privilege is ours to help men of good will!

### Theocracy's Increase

Jehovah's prophet Zechariah, foretelling Theocracy's increase, said that "Jerusalem shall be inhabited as towns without walls for the multitude of men and cattle therein". (2:4) Why without walls? Because walls would interfere with the expansion of Jehovah's organization. The aptness of the simile and the various ways in which this prophecy is being fulfilled today are forcibly brought to our attention in the "Yearbook".

The advertising of public meetings plays a vital part in Theocracy's increase. Said a Swedish newspaper: "One day in the spring an energetic gentleman appeared in this community and started to invite people to a lecture... It did not \[take] long until the lectures had to be held out of doors for lack of space." (231)

And the part circuit assembles play in Theocracy's increase is noted in the report from El Salvador. As a result of a circuit assembly at San Miguel, "The hall overflowed for every public talk, so more walls had to come out, and to date we have removed three walls, and each time the hall has filled right up to its new capacity as if by miracle." (132)

Missionary activity is another vital factor in Theocracy's increase as seen by the report from Uruguay. "To accommodate the many new attenders some good people have opened up their private homes for company meeting places. One brother tore out a brick wall... and... is going to do away with another wall to make \[the room where the meetings are being held] still larger. Six missionaries in a small city... now find that they have to enlarge their dining room and use it to accommodate the people." (238, 239) Truly, literally as well as symbolically, Zechariah's prophecy is finding fulfillment in our day, and the various kinds of activity are all having a part therein.

# YOUR SERVICE MEETING

Now that two demonstrations are presented here each month, two different points can be stressed. This month, the first demonstration shows how the two new uses of the S-10 form, that is, the procedure in following up new subscriptions and obtaining renewal subscriptions, will be put into operation. The second one demonstrates the need of training for the preaching work.

**SCENE I: Calling back to the new subscribers.**

**Part A (7 minutes)** Company servant, Bible study servant, accounts servant and at least one company book study conductor are engaged in a lively discussion of the new procedure. Company servant, explaining system, emphasizes that accounts servant will make out S-10 in triplicate, stressing need for double-checking for accuracy and legibility. He will note on 11 names of publishers who placed subscriptions and give one copy to Bible study servant. About six weeks after receiving S-10, Bible study servant will make out lists of subscriptions received along with names of publishers who placed them. He will give lists to company book study conductors. Study conductors will check with publishers who placed subscriptions to see that back-calls are being made.

**Part B (3 minutes)** A study conductor, after company study, is checking with publishers and briefly explaining why this is done and importance of calling back on subscribers. Publishers express appreciation for reminder even though they had been calling back with each current issue of magazine. Study conductor mentions that he will have to check with one publisher on back-call day since he was not able to be here.

**Part C (2 minutes)** Publisher continues to follow through by calling on subscriber with current issue again even though six weeks have passed since subscription was placed. This should be very brief, and only the introduction need be given.

**SCENE II: Calling back to get renewal subscriptions.**

**Part A (5 minutes)** Above-mentioned servants continue discussion started in first scene, taking up use of S-10 in obtaining renewals. One or two months before subscriptions expire, Bible study servant makes out subscription blanks and gives them to study conductors. For this year, since in some cases the names of publishers who placed subscriptions are not available, Bible study servant can see if expiring subscriptions belong to those with whom Bible studies are conducted. If so, conductor's name is noted so that he will be approached concerning the call. If publishers have back-calls on some whose names are listed, they should call for the renewal.

**Part B (3 minutes)** Company study is just closing. Study conductor is giving out subscription slips handed to him by Bible study servant. One slip is given to a publisher who conducts a study with the subscriber. Another is given to a publisher who obtained subscription almost a year ago. Some slips are left over because a publisher moved and others because ones who placed subscriptions could not be determined. These slips are given to various publishers. A publisher recognizes the name on an unassigned slip and it is given to him. Some unassigned slips still remain to be used at contact points.

**Part C (2 minutes)** The final part of demonstration shows a publisher just leaving a home after obtaining a renewal. He is assuring householder that when the renewal notice appears in his magazine he may ignore it and that he will call again as arranged.

# COMPANY ORGANIZATION

## Pioneer Publisher

The pioneer is a publisher who devotes at least 100 hours a month, or 1,200 hours a year, to Kingdom preaching work. Privileges of pioneer work are open to company publishers who have regularly published for at least six months after their baptism. If necessary, a pioneer may engage in part-time work to provide things essential for his livelihood. (Acts 18:2,3; 20:33,34; 1 Cor. 4:12, NW) However, such work must always be viewed as secondary to his pioneer service.

The primary responsibility of a pioneer is to devote his time to the preaching work, and this by regularly engaging in house-to-house, back-call, Bible study and magazine activity. He should use the current offer when witnessing, obtaining his literature through the company when at all possible. Each pioneer will direct interested persons to company meetings and endeavor to aid these and other publishers in the field service. This he can do by working in co-operation with the company's weekly service arrangements at the Kingdom Hall and company book studies.

Due to his consistent field activity and practical field experience he can be of real help to the company in its service arrangements and programs. By taking personal interest in company activity and putting forth a real effort to improve his ministry, as well as that of his fellow servants, he will be exercising understanding and maturity. Servants in the company will show appreciation for this assistance by not imposing various duties on the pioneer that may interfere with his field service work. In exercising love and showing enthusiasm for his work, the pioneer will be an example in service and godly conduct and prove to be a stimulating inspiration to the entire congregation.

The pioneer endeavors to exercise patience and tact at all times and will work for the upbuilding and unity of the local organization. He wisely appreciates that regular attendance and oral participation at company meetings and enrollment (if a brother) in the theocratic ministry school are a real encouragement to the company publishers, and it will benefit him personally and prepare him for his daily ministry. Many opportunities and privileges are open to him to expand his ministry by accepting assignments to companies in need of pioneer assistance.

By doing this a pioneer is able to contribute much toward the increase and progress of a company.
