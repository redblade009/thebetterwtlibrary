# Satanic Influence Near.

St. Peter, so far from believing that Satan is for self, stoking fires, described him as a roaring, angry lion, going about amongst men, seeking whom he might devour—deceive, involve in sin. St. Paul forewarns us that Satan and his agents are crafty and not to be expected to present themselves as angels of faith—leaders in advanced truth. We are assured that this dreadful, injurious agency has been deceiving the whole world for centuries. The Divine promise is that Messiah's Kingdom shall bind or restrain Satan for a thousand years, that he must deceive the people no more until Messiah shall deliver up the Kingdom to the Father.—Chrichthans 15:24.

The Scriptures specially forewarn us of great activity on the part of the evil spirits in the end of this Age, and indicate that they will have since come with the great Time of (world-wide) Trouble which will precede the inauguration of Messiah's Kingdom. It is time for us to be on the lookout for those wonderful manifestations of disincline which for a time will seem to make more of our poor race madly insane, brutal, inhuman, disloyal. This is the real danger from the demons—in the present life, and not, in the future.

They will deceive and torture humanity to the limit of their permisses, when they will be restrained by the King of Glory and the bright shining of the sins of lightlessness, which will savor in the New Day of earth's blessing and restitution.—Malachi 4:2; Acts 2:13-21.

The words "weeping and gnaubing of teeth," represent, in a new condition amongst men, but those which may be witnessed every day—sorrow, heartache, grievous disappointment; and this is the use of this expression in the Bible. We should dismiss all thought that the experiences mentioned will be after death and the result of tortures inflicted by demons.

All Bible students will recall several other passages of very similar language to that of our text. A greater cure in the study of the Bible might have protested, as from the thought that any of these references apply to the dead, because the Scriptures not only tell that they are sleeping and waiting for the morning, but they also particularly inform us that "the dead know not anything;" "their sons come to honor and they know it not, to dishonor and they perceive it not of them;" and that "there is neither work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom in the grave \[Bless]"—whether all go—Ecclesiastes 9:5, 10; Job 14:21.

The difference between them is in respect to their wisdom or unwiedom. The wise saints of God in the end of this Age, guided by the Divine Word, will walk very circumspectly, very faithfully, searching the Scriptures in respect to all they believe, and serving to conform themselves to the Divine will in all that they do and say.

Because of their watchfulness, alertness, devoutness and loyalty they are

# DEPART AND BE WITH CHRIS.

Ah, says one, I have great faith in St. Paul, and I remember his words: "I am in a strait between two things: saving a desire to depart and to be with Christ, which is far from the right. If St. Paul expected to depart and be with Christ, why is it not reasonable to suppose that he did not and that all others, at least of the saintly, at death so depart and pass at once into the presence and fellowship of Jesus?

Such a misunderstanding of St. Paul's words and thoughts are excusable in view of the general trend of Christian thought on the part of the Christian, and in view of the error made in this case by the translators. We are not feasting the translators, because they had the erroneous thought firmly embedded in their minds and presumably were trying to make the Apostle love say what they conscientiously thought to ought to say.

But what we are interested in knowing in, What did he say on the subject? Let us read the Apostle's words critically: He was in a strait between two things—whether he would prefer to live and suffer further for the Truth's sake, and assist him in the life; whether he would prefer to die and rest from his labors. Between these two positions he had no choice. But there was a third thing—and if this had been a possibility he would have had no difficulty in deciding—he had a real positive desire respecting it; neither of the things which were possible to him would have stood in comparison at all, this third thing would have been as desirable.

Now what was that third thing? It was not to live and suffer and help the brethren, nor was it to die and be at rest from his labors. The third thing, according to a literal translation, is expressed thus: "I have a desire for the returning, and being with Christ, which so far better"—the better than either living under the present trying conditions or dying, sleeping, resting and waiting for the Kingdom.
