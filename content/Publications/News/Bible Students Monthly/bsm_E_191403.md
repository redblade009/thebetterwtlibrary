"Judged by human standards man is far better prepared to write a Bible now than when our Bible was written. ### HEART AND STOMACH WORK IN GLASS JARS.

A heart, stomach and kidneys, transferred from the casement of flesh to glass jars in his laboratory by Dr. Amos Chertel, of the Rockefeller Institute, are now performing their normal functions. This startling announcement was made by Dr. Chertel himself to a group of scientists who listened breathlessly to his account of his latest achievement.

Dr. Chertel has kept a heart taken from its body, alive for 104 days. His success with this experiment strengthened his belief, he said, that the stomach and other vital organs of animals could be transferred from one body to another without cessation of their normal functions. He experimented with a heart, stomach and kidneys and told the scientists.

"The heart is beating, the stomach is digesting and the kidneys performing their functions as well as ever, while the body that contained them is long since dead.'—New York American.

Our forefathers cried, evidently, in their declaration of total disparity. We are not, however, to conclude that God made us sinners; for the Bible contradicts this thought, and assures us that "all His work is perfect." According to the Bible, we received our lives from our parents; and they there from their parents; and so on back to Father Adam. The Bible tells us that our first parents sinned, and came under the Divine residence of death; and that this meant menial and moral, as well as physical, degeneracy. Undoubtedly as human sympathy extends through a knowledge of the facts, both parents and children will be blessed by that increase of knowledge. Parents need to learn that their own mental needs and sympathies with right or wrong serve to stamp, to birth-mark their children, either favorably or unfavorably. It requires a great deal of training to work out of the child's disposition that which was inbred at its birth. **The Riches of God's Grace.**
Our Great Creator is rich in grace, the Apostle of God. He not only purposed to recover mankind from the disadvantages of the fruit, but additionally took advantage of the circumstances connected with the permission of sin to give an especial opportunity to any of the sinners to manifest, if they would, special loyalty to Himself. God might have placed a different penalty against sin. He might have excluded our first parents from Eden, for a year and then have returned them, or He might have simply banished the race from Eden, and have

allowed it in lies without sentence of death against sin. Had He done so, Jesus would not have needed to do for man's redemption. Because the sentence of death had been imposed, however, it must be canceled before the race could be restored in perfection and to God's favor.

It is evident, then, that God wished to have the death penalty upon our race so as to make necessary the death of His Son. This, in turn, meant that the Father invited the Son to become man's Redeemer, and that the Son accepted the offer, and came into this world for that very purpose. This implied that the Father would reward Him with a high resolution, in recognition of His loyalty and obedience unto death, even the death of the cross. Thus Jesus suffered for our sins, and entered into His glory.

**Story Ground Hearers.**

There are well-meaning people, 'good people,' who are man's idealist—well-intentioned, but they have not deep, strong characters. When God's Truth reaches these they can appreciate it and they receive it with joy and start in the good way with rest, but they are not the kind that the Lord wants in this special election. He will want them and all during the Millennial Age, but now He is seeking a special class for which these are not fit. Their unitness is shown by the fact that they are not producing the fruits of the Spirit. Preservation with them. They are not strong characters. They will be passed by an anteword; but, thank God, they will not be tortured, but reserved for the Messianic Reign, when the influences of the Kingdom will be quite sufficient to deal with their case and to give them strength of character and to take away the unfavorable, deep condem which now hinders fruitage.

**Good Seed in Thorny Ground.**

It requires good soil to produce thorns, but when the good seed was sown upon that soil and both sprung up together, the thorns abided the wheat. It agood the nourishment of the soil to itself and by wheat was choked. Perhaps all of us have heard these thorns compared to the attractions of the thestery, the glands table, sports, novels, etc.—that these as thorns best the Christian way and tend to choke the development of the fruits of the Spirit. But is this the proper interpretation? Is it true that the danger of the Christian lies along these lines? It seems to us that here are nominal Christians who might be best by such an error, but that the higher thought is that the true Christian, together of the Holy Spirit, is out of sympathy with such things and that they have no place in his heart.

Thus the Apostle declares of such: 'Old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new,' and 'The things that I once hated I now love,' and the things that I once loved I now hate.'

A brother in the Lord, who had recently made a full consecration of himself, remarked to us recently: 'I'll seem as strange, brother Russell, the wonderful change that has come over my life. A year ago I was wildly excited over baseball. It occupied much of my thoughts and I always attended the games when possible. This year it has no attraction for me whatever.' Instead, God's wonder, 'Old Plan of Salvation captivates my thoughts, and every spare hour that I can command I wish to do anything it or if it is dominating with those of like precious faith. They will be old things have passed away; all things have become new.'

Hearkening to our Lord's explanation we notice that it is in agreement with this thought, that the thorns are not wicked practices, but 'cures of this life and the decolfations of riches.' Ah, yes! the true Christian whose heart has been prepared by the gracious promises of God's Word and the hopes of a share in the Kingdom with his Lord is in danger of being swamped by the ordinary duties on life, proper enough in themselves to be limited degree. The Lord's Word commands him to be not suicidal in business

and to make provision for those of his own household, but he is in constant danger of allowing those things to crowd his spiritual interests as a 'glamour' instead of curtailing earthly desires, ambitions, business and family responsibilities, minimizing those that he may have the more time or money, or both, to expend in the Divine service, he is apt to follow the matter to go the other way, to give the great food to those things and to provide more than 'things needful and decent.'

Frid, ambition for self and for family, love of money and the pursuit of it, and the award which the pursuit brings and the hindrances to spiritual growth and to fruitage in the Lord's service—whose are the 'thorns.' Also, many of God's true saints are thus injured! These will not be fit for the Kingdom. They will miss the great Prize. They will suffer a great loss, whatever may be the blessings and whatever they may attain to be God's providence by coming up through great tribulations.

The thorny ground, of course, cannot cast out its own thorns, and herein the parable is defective. The Lord's explanation shows this, and all Christians are reburied of the Lord to keep themselves in the love of God, to cast out thorny influences and to bring forth much fruit. If, then, we find that our hearts are infested with the cares of this life and with the pursuit of earthly riches let us at once cast out these thorns that our hearts may bring forth the fruit of the harmsery with the infirmities of our Master to bring forth the good fruit.

**Thirty, Sixty and a Hundred Fold.**

Some good-ground hearers will be found. God forwards the and hence greatest of His Gospel Age and all of the sowing for the sake of these who bring forth good results. Let us cast out the cares of this life and the decolfations of riches, and let us overcome the spirit of the world, the spirit of indifference, and let us be so enthroned with the Gospel of the Kingdom that we shall bring forth much fruit. 'Herein is your Father glorified.'
