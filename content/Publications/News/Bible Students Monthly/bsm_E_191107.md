## Religious and Scientific Gleanings

### CHINAS CRY FOR PITY.

The intoxicating curse of China is opium; itself a century ago, Chinese who used to control the situation due to exclude all foreign opium. But wealthy Englishmen, were concerned, because they had large interests in India, where the poppy, from which opium is made, grows profusely.

The result was a war, in which the beatless Chinese, not having up-to-date Christian (7) cannons and ships, had heavily. Then the British fast-ened themselves on the Chinese, took control of a liberal piece of territory, and made a treaty, which the conquered Chinese were forced to accept. That treaty stipulates the admission of opium into China. The Chinese cannot stop the traffic without precipitating war with the most powerful Kingdom of Christ (7), on earth, so far as naval strength goes.

Now, after years of suffering, the Chinese are becoming civilized, or Christianized (7), to the extent of adopting cannon, rapid-fire guns, a regular army, etc. They are about to establish a Parliamentary form of Government also, and are arraigned to school the rising generation in the English language. It is said that war orders for these new school-books have been placed in the United States.

### A Monster Petition.

Now, China realizes that opium is her great curse; she has passed laws against the growth of the poppy and the manufacture of opium. But she finds that the imports of opium amount to 2,000,000 pounds per year. She cries out again, as in the past, that this Great Kingdom of Christ (7) (Greek Britain), may save nearly upon her good cease to insist on this curse being introduced to blight China morally and physically.

The young men of heathen China have started a monster petition for the cessation of opium importation. The signatures are of the 200,000, representing China's 400,000,000 people. It is to be addressed to his Majesty King George, of Great Britain and Ireland, and Emperor of India and the Isles of the Sea and Head of the Church of England—the nightmare Kingdom of Christ (7) on earth.

The leaf of this appeal has already appeared in the New York Herald, February 17. It recounts the a previous appeal, of similar character, was made without avail to the King's Royal grandmother in 1818, when she was the representative of this branch of Christ's Kingdom (7).

### A Terrible Indictment.

Is not all this very incongruous, very strange? What hypocrisy we have indulged in? How we all should be ashamed of it? Think of one of the chief nations of earth sending liblies and missionaries to the heathen in our hand and rain and opium in the other. Is it any wonder that the heathen expert now-faced overtures as best they are able? Is it any wonder that the "common people" of Great Britain are unable to take their religion seriously against falling away from all church associations—into infidelity?

It is no wonder! Let us get back to honesty and truth. Let us admit that Great Britain is one of the Kingdoms of this world. Let us not charge the limits of our selfishness to God and to Christ's Kingdom. Let us stand for the best possible in worthy government and continue to pray for and to hope and wait for the Kingdom which is to come and to bring about the doing of God's will on earth as far as is done in heaven, according to the prayer of our new Redeemer, our Lord and Savior.

### "THE DAY HAS NOT COME!"

Says Archbishop Ireland.

The night of keeping is to give place to the morning of joy, says God's Word.—Pub. 31:1.

Messiah is to establish His Kingdom of Peace and compel the abolition of war, on the Bible declares, and on both Catholics and Protestants agree.

Yet both Catholics and Protestants agree that Messiah's Kingdom was set up in power and grant since more than eighteen centuries ago. Why, then, has it failed of its mission? Can either Catholics or Protestants explain?

Hear Archbishop Ireland at Paooria, on February 23, as follows:—

"Has the day come of such eminent prophecy of the principle of arbitration that a great nation, such as the United States of America, may safely turn all its swords into plow-shore, and all its powers into sickles? No one will make the affirmation.

"No, the day of assured and lasting international peace has not arrived, if ever ambitious and pride of nations permit it to arrive.

"Today the nation that dismantles its ships of war and demands its soldiers puts itself in danger of gross humiliation, if not fatal disaster. The day America is respected by its sister nations—it is respected because, also, it is feared.

"Peace America invokes, but to be the more sure of peace America must be ready at a moment's notice to summon to its defense an army and a navy to whom defeat is impossible."

### The Explained.

God's Kingdom, Messiah's Kingdom, was not set up eighteen centuries or more ago. That is the mistake! It is not yet set up! The Church was not commissioned to conquer the world and to reign as and for Christ during this time. Her commission was, as "make herself ready." (Rev. 19:7.) She is to be her Lord's mouthpiece in calling and instructing the elect, who, at the First Resurrection, are to be "changed" to the "divine nature," as St. Peter declares, and then will inherit with their Redeemer His Messianic Kingdom, which will rule the world with a "red of iron" for its command, in light out of sin and sinfulness—2 Peter 7:1.

Christendom (Catholic and Protestant) has labored under the huge mistake of supposing itself saturated to rule the world. The attempt to live by this erroneous opinion deluged the world with the blood of religious "holy wars" and cruel persecutions of each other and of the Jews.

It is time to awake to the fact that the Lord's call now to His people is to show their loyalty by faith and obedience to the Divine Law of Love, even unto self-sacrifice, even unto death, to the Apostle wrote, "The suffer with Christ, we shall also reign with him."—Rom. 8:18, 17; 1 Cor. 6:2, 3.

### Let Us No Longer Be Deceived.

The First Resurrection, like the Second Coming of Christ, will be invisible, except as the glory and power of the Messianic Kingdom will quickly follow; there will be: (1) A time of world-wide trouble; (2) A reign of righteousness world-wide and the execution of wars, etc., as promised in the Prophets.

Let us no longer deceive ourselves by speaking and thinking of civilized nations as Kingdoms of God in any sense. Let us recognize them, Scripturally, as "kingdoms of this world," identity kingdoms, permitted to hold away until the time of Messiah's Kingdom—and no longer. (Dan. 2:44) Let us not expect of these kingdoms the blessings promised only under Messiah's Kingdom. Let us, on the contrary, watch and pray for the Kingdom of Heaven and prepare ourselves, and all who have the bearing ear, for our promised resurrection "change"—and let us have all else to God. He is able and willing to work all things according to the counsel of His own will.

## Fear, the Bane of Humanity

- Fear not, for I have redeemed thee; I have called thee by thy name; thou art Miss."—Dan. 42:1

FEAR is a great calamity. It is the closely related to all the sorrows of life, closely linked to all the torpedoes of the world. One of the blessings of true Christianity is the release it gives from fear. But alas! true Christianity, true Christian faith, and the present rewards of these are not very common even in Christendom. Few show the meaning of the Master's words, "Come unto Me, will ye that God and are heavy taught using will give you rest." Few appreciate the assurance, "If the Son shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed."—Matt. 13:28; John 3:26.

Fear has its foundation in sin. You see sure that the angels in heaven have so fear. After Adam had developed the Divine command in Eden, he feared his Maker and fell from Him. Similarly, fear affects all of Adam's posterity, because of the general realization that "all are sinners." God and His righteousness are, figuratively, light, and the sinner and his sins are, figuratively, darkness. There is no compromise—the darkness hairds (opposite) the light, the light scattered the darkness. Love and reverence—is, hand in hand, but love and fear are hypocrites; we cannot love that when we fear, we do not fear that which we love.

### Fear, Worry, Sickness.

Hope, pride and ambition encourage the business man and the laborer, the living and the peasant, the housewife and the maid, the readers of the social set, and the patient mother. Nevertheless, all of these have a drug-sweetened fear, which as the answer of life produces worry; worry produces indignation, indigestion produces insomnia and nervousness, which lead us to worry. Pride of disease is the unimpeachable one. To the extent, therefore, that fear is removed, health and happiness are encouraged and aided.

All physicians recognize the fact that fear is closely related to disease. Hence the wise physician always make this cheerful surroundings for the sick, cheerful conversation, diversion of the mind, the avoidance of thought being a perisome lines—the rest cure, etc.

It may be asked, Why is it that the Master's cure is little considered today? Why are we few pointed to Christ, the Great Physician, for the rest and peace and comfort and the health it is promised to His followers eighteen centuries ago? Have His words proven untrue? Are His professed followers amongst the most anxious and troubled and overcharged and sick and sorrowing and fearful of our poor groaning creation? If so, what is the explanation? We must concede the inference. We must admit that the fear hundred millions of professed Christians are much more unhappy, much more fearful, much more nervous, prevails and sickly than the twelve hundred millions outside of Christendom.

### The Name Christendom a Minsomer.

The explanation of this peculiar situation is that Christendom is not Christian, that the name Christendom is a misnomer! Christ's Kingdom does not prevail because the civilized one-fourth of humanity. For centuries now Christendom has deceived itself, and has seriously misrepresented Christ, His teachings and His promised Kingdom of Eighteousness, for which still we are praying, "Thy Kingdom come, Thy will be done on earth." Our standing armies, prepared to destroy each other, and our Dreadnought service, prepared to blow each other to shame; and our submarines and anything machines, our great cannons and torpedoes—all prove the existence of calling human civilization Christ's Kingdom.

Similarly, the worry, fear, strife, nervousness, etc., of Christendom and prove that the Great Teacher's antidote for fear and worry has not been taken by the masses of Christendom. We hold that the Good Physician is ready for sin, corroding care, worry, fear, in just as effective today as it was eighteen and a half centuries ago.

we hold that the real difficulty is that His prescription has not been faithfully followed. We urge that those who now have "an ear to hear" His message should accept it faithfully, and that they should not accept at the hands of anybody another prescription, said to be "just as good." We should make sure that we get the remedy which bears the endorsement of the Lord's Word, and which has the seal of "the precious blood."

### The Master's Teachings Perverted.

During the Dark Ages superstitious fear was encouraged, as all will concede. The attempt was made to drive humanity to God. Atrocious doctrine were promulgated in the name of God, Christ and His Apostles. St. Paul denominates the "Baptisms of Eighteousness" (1 Tim. 4:2). These, primarily, taught a Purgatory, to be mitigated by prayers and masses.

Under the impulse of fear, millions accepted the great Redeemer and professed Archbishop, in total ignorance of His real teachings, and as more His followers than when they were heathen. Later on the adversary partially sidelined the Reformation Movement by leading zealous people to renounce Purgatory as unscrupulous and to accept in its stead for the world to large, eternal turmoil—a still more unscriptural proposition—a still more God-dishonoring doctrine of demons.

Fortunately for ourselves we Protestants do not take our own doctrine seriously. However much all Protestant Creeds assert that such baptized saints will reach heaven and, contantwise, that all the unseemly will suffer eternal torture, nevertheless, not one in a hundred believes that.

The most saintly, the most conscientious of them are troubled by their fears, tormented by their sympathies, perplexed to understand God's dealings, hindered from having the peace and joy which the Master promised His followers. The less conscientious of them little conscience to their own injury, gradually losing faith in the Word of God because of this error. They thus escape some of the tenments of fear, but they do not attain the love, joy, contentment and peace which the Master promised His faithful ones.

### Irreliable Subterfuges Harmful.

There is only the one Divine remedy for this harmful fear. That remedy is associated with the Gospel of Christ, but there are numerous subterfuges, some drown then rock, disappoints, excels and sorrows with alcohol or various narcotics. Some seek to drown them with sentuous pleasures, some with business activity, some with church activity, some with "develmayable" unreason. The result of this self-deception indeed relieves from a considerable measure of fear. To this extent it proves a solace and satisfaction; but it is a subterfuge and not the true remedy. As a proposition its influence upon the mind, upon all the reassenting faculties, is injurious. The twisting of reason necessary to this philosophy, which calls black white, and pale pleasures, and sin righteousness, as destructive of sound reassuring ability. The habit of perversion necessary to the calling of bad good and wrong right not only injures the mind, but, in attempting to sustain itself from the Scriptures, perverts the entire Word of God. With reason perverted and the Scriptures perverted, the true marriage of the Scriptures cannot be enjoyed. Unless fear be consummable eliminated, the proper faith, hope, joy and spirit of a sound mind are impossible.

For such self-sacrificing love how necessary is the spirit of humility and gentleness and patience and faith! How forceful are the Master's words, Except ye be converted from the spirit of the world to the Spirit of Christ; and become as little children (in meekness and teachableness), ye shall not enter into the Kingdom of heaven.—Matt. 18: 1-8.

We should not only have this love, but we should have it with foreway, warmth, power; not only do a semi-continence, but with a real interest in each other's welfare—the rich as well as the poor; the educated as well as the ignorant. Our love should be cut to those as we see any lack in them that we could singly—rendering assistance of any kind; using always, of course, discretion, for love learns to be wise, and to take into consideration our motives while we endeavor to do them good.

The Apostle suggests that we are to say that this is our own experience, not merely that it is a principle which we recognize, but that we should give good to ourselves that this should be accomplished in us, in our own lives. We would not be natural to have the benevolence of mind which would practise forgiveness of those who trespass against us. But when we think of the fact that the whole race is fallen and degraded through heredity, it should make us sympathetic if some are more depraved, we should have the more sympathy for them. As we think sympathetically among these men, our sympathetic love will be crease; as we practise sympathetic love the New Creature develops.
