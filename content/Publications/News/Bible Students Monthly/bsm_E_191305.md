#### TRANSFORMED BY SURGERY.

Marquette, Mich., March 21.—A surgical operation on the brain has changed from a dangerous criminal to a kind and gentle man, Edmund Holsbay, the tumult known as "Illush Bart," who terrorized the West twenty years ago, and a year from east November he will be freed from the Mass penitentiary. Holsbay received a life sentence in 1889 for holding up a stage coach and, incidentally, shooting and killing A. E. Rosenbein, an Illinois banker, near Lake Gagebo.

Twenty-two years old when captured, Holsbay declared at his trial that his mind had been deranged and perverted by reading so-called dime novels. The court decided that he was a victim of delusional obscurity, and accordingly he was sentenced to prison for life instead of death.

In the March following his incarceration he smuggled a table knife to his cell. One day he refused to leave his cell, and Warden Tompkins found "Illush Bart" holding a guard by the throat and measuring him with the skimpsend knife. The warden drew his revolver.

SEVEN is a very prominent number in the Bible—in everything relating to the Divine Progress. In the first chapter of Genesis the Sabbath Day is referred to in a figuring view as speaking of the Seventh Epoch of God's creations on our earth—bringing order out of chaos. Not until Mt. This does not signify that the setting apart of a certain time for rest would be of advantage only to the Jew, nor that a special Seventh Day devoted to God would be disadvantageous to all people. It merely means that God entered into Covenant relationship with the generation only, and hence to them only He told His Will, His Law—obedience to which His made the foundation of the blessing He promised to that people. There is no room to question the import of the Fourth Commandment of the Jewish Law. It distinctly commanded that the Seventh Day of the week should be to the Jews a rest day, in which no work of any kind should be done, either by parent or child, employer or servant, male or female, ox or ox, or any creature owned by a Jew. It was a rest day pure and simple. ## ACCIDENTS AND FATIGUE.

It was shown by an exhaustive inquiry of the subject in France that the number of accidents increases progressively from four during the first half day; that after the rest at midday the number of accidents is notably less than in the last few days. In addition to the course of the second half day accidents again become from four to four progressively more numerous, and that the maximum number of accidents toward the end of the second half day is notably higher than the corresponding maximum in the morning.

The influence of the workingmen's fatigue on the production of accidents stands out clearly from these observations, and it is easy to understand how this comes about when it is remembered that with fatigue the attention readily of statistics and disappears. The conclusion, therefore, is that in order to produce a diminution in the number of accidents it would be sufficient to intercalate in the first half of the year. Now we do not want a period of repose, naturally not so long as that at midday, but the length of which remains to be determined. In addition, the fact that Jesus arose from the dead on the first day of the week, and that He met with them on that day, led them to meet again and again on the first day, in hope that He would again appear; thus gradually it became a sin, and the law of the Lord that day for Christian fellowship. In this way, so far as we know, both the first day and the seventh day of the week were observed by Christians for quite a time, but neither was understood to be obligatory—as bondage. Both days were privileged.
