# BOTTLED LIGHT AND HEAT.

Bottled light at a heat for use anywhere, in any climate, at a minimum cost, is now possible through a discovery of a method of liquefying natural gas by Dr. Walter A. Steiling, a government chemist. The discovery paves the way for the commercial use of millions of dollars in data from the past two years. The waste in the petroleum fields of the country, Dr. Steiling liquidated several hundred feet of the gas, stored it in a thick glass bottle, and lighted his office at Pittsburgh with it for several months. The cost was less that that of ordinary illuminating gas.

The gas is forced into a long cell of steel piping and then to a compressor. In the compressor, it is subjected to 750 or 800 pounds to the square inch. While the gas is completely liquefied, in the course of several hours, it is drawn off in metal or thick glass containers, capable of sustaining high internal pressure. In this condition, it can be shipped to any climate. All that is necessary to obtain the gas is to tap the plug of the container. According to the government experts, the commercial possibilities of the discovery are almost unlimited.—Emerson (Neb.) Enterprise.

## CHURCH FEDERATION.

Church Union is Aim of Leading Denominations.

"Apart from the ever-present duty of evangelizing the world, which presses invariably upon every denomination, the two great problems confronting the churches today can: First, correlation of effort and enterprise; and second, unity, long despaired of the been brought within practical realization, not by crest-elimination, but by a federation of all the Protestant creeds.

"The Federal Council of the Churches of Christ in America grew out of the inter-church conference in New York in November, 1916, whereat a definite plan was proposed looking to the organization of a federal council of the Churches of Christ in America. From 1916 to 1918 the plan was presented to and approved by the gathering bodies of no less than thirty-two great denominations, such as the Presbyterian general assembly, the Lutheran synode, the Deciples of Christ, the general conference of the Methodist Episcopal church, and the general conference of the Methodist Episcopal church South.

"As a result of this, there was held in Philadelphia in December, 1916, the first meeting of the Federal Council. The organization was completed, national officers were elected, state and national headquarters appointed, and a working plan agreed upon. The personnel of the council is composed of four hundred officially appointed or elected delegates from the thirty-two evangelical denominations alluded to, representing over a hundred thousand miniature and eighteen million members. The Committee of the Federal Council holds the general meeting quadrants. During the interim the activities are controlled by an executive committee of sixty, whose meetings are held annually, and by special sessions whenever necessary. A business committee is formed by special sessions.

"While the foregoing brief outline of united action indicate only a primitive form of federal union, nothing more could perhaps be expected in such a short duration of time. Yet the federation is real, and the federal council demonstrates the fact not only that the church union is feasible, but that it has been actually achieved.
