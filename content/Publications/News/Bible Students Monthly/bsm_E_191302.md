## One More Before the Time.

Many have remarked at the peculiarity of Saint Paul's statement that he saw the Lord as one prematurely born. But, if at first the statement was filed and unstilling, now it is luminous and enlightening. His thought is that the time for giving circular demonstrations of the Lord's resurrection had gone by; the next manifestation of Him is to be to His saints, and after that to the world. Thus we read: "We shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is"—made like Him by our resurrection change from the human condition to the spirit conditions.

Strictly speaking, then, the experiences of Saul of Tarsus were out of the catholic. No one else than he alone, and even the saintly, were to see the Lord before their resurrection change, in the end of this Age. When, therefore, he saw Jesus, he saw Him before the time—more than eighteen centuries before the time. Moreover, he saw Him as one born before the time—as one resurrected before the time. We are to have in mind the Scriptural use of this word born—that the Church must all be forgotten of the Holy Spirit in order to experience the resurrection birth.

Thus, concerning our Redeemer's resurrection, we read: "He was the First-born from the dead"; and again, "He was the First-born of many brothers." So the hope of all of God's people is that the beginning of the Holy Spirit in the present time will be followed to the resurrection birth, which Saint Paul describes in this same chapter, saying, "It is sown in weakness, it is raised in power; it is sown an animal body, it is raised a spirit body."

With these things before our mental eye we see the meaning of the Apostle in the words, "He was seen of me, also, as of one born before the time." He of all this man is to see the Lord's Most Apostles. They saw Him merely as He appeared—sometimes in one form of body and

with a pamphlete clothing, at other times in another form and with diverse clothing. He appeared and disappeared, but they did not see Him, the New Creature; they saw merely the various forms in which He appeared. But Saint Paul saw the Lord more nearly as all the saints shall see Him when they shall be born from the dead by the glorious resurrection change.

## Church's Glory to Be Greater Than That of the Angels.

One great difference between the effect of what Saul saw and what the entire glorious Church will see in the Resurrection, is that God's eyesight was injured by the sight; besides, he saw nothing very distinctly—merely a blinding light, which the voice explained to be the appearance or manifestation of Jesus. For more precious will be the experience of the Church. Before beholding Him who is declared to be "the express image of the Father's person," "whom no man hath seen, nor can see, dwelling in light which no man can approach unto"—before this, we shall have been changed, glorified. This will enable us to see Him as He, in the glory of the Church will be like to the glory of the Lord, and superior to the glory of the angels.

While heavenly glory does not consist exclusively of brightness, nevertheless the Scriptures everywhere seem to associate bright shining with the heavenly ones, and would imply that the higher the skin can and what the brighter will be the sheen, the glory. Thus the glory of the Heavenly Father is represented as being so great that few could endure it, and angels and scruplin are represented as veiling their faces before the Divine glory, which so greatly transcends their own.

It should not, therefore, surprise us that the Scriptures everywhere represent that our Lord Jesus and the Church, "made partakers of the Divine nature" (1 Peter 1:4), will have a great honor and brightness, far above that of angels and all others except that of the Heavenly Father. As the Lord Jesus was able to veil the glories of His preters, and to appear as a man, after His resurrection, so undoubtedly He could do as respects the world, during the thousand years of His Messianic Reign.

And, similarly, it would be possible for the Church to appear as men with the glory veiled. It might appear as first as though this was what was signified through the Mosaic type when Moses, representing Messiah in glory, came down from the mountain, his face radiant, but veiled for the sake of the people. Our thought, however, as that Jesus and the Church will never thus appear in the flesh as angels have done in the past, but that, on the contrary, the Ancient Worship, perfected on the human place, will be their agents and representatives in all communication with mankind. Thus, "the Law shall go forth from Mount Zion (the Spiritual Kingdom) and the Word of the Lord from Jerusalem" (the earthly Kingdom), to every nation, people, kindred and tongue, that all may be blest if they will, and attain everlasting life.
