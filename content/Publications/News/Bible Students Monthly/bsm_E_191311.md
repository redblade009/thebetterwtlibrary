### ORGANS OF DEAD SAVE SICK.

#### Remarkable Story of Surgery on the Battlefield.

George F. Vint, of Norfolk, Va., has made public a startling letter he received from Mr. Cremodas, a young Greek who left Norfolk a year ago with a party of his countrymen to fight against the Turks. Cremodas, in his letter, says that Dr. William Damien, a physician from Paris, who served in a semi-official capacity with the Greek forces at Janina during the military operations about the city performed some remarkable surgical experiments.

According to Cremodas, Dr. Damien brought with him from Paris ninety wealthy patients suffering with diseased kidneys and other organs, and established an invalid camp at Philates, a small town fifteen miles from Janina.

Dr. Damien made several unsuccessful attempts to obtain an audience with the king, but was finally successful when a sum of money was contributed by one of the invalids to equip a battery for the army.

Commanders of the divisions at the front were given orders to send Dr. Damien all recently killed soldiers, that he needed. The experiments then commenced. Upon the receipt of a dead body the doctor and his assistance proceeded to transfer the healthy and still living organs desired from the corpse to the body of one of his patients. Old men on the verge of the grave from Brigh's disease had the secret of their trouble removed, and the healthy organs of some strong man put in their place. These transfers are said to have included no one than three of the vital bodily organs and numerous smaller operations, in which forty were a complete success, while there was a loss of only ten out of the ninety.—New York Herald.

# THE PEACE OF GOD

"And the peace of God, which parcels all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Jesus Christ."—Psalm 4:11.

PEACE is defined to be a state of action or tranquility, freedom from disturbance or agitation—calmness, repose. Such a state of mind can test affirms of God. His is a mind tranquil, calm, undisturbed, never agitated, nor even wearied, nor perplexed by any of the cares of His vast dominion. Yet this perfect peace of God, the Scriptures show, is due neither to the fact that there are no disorders in His vast domain, nor yet to any stoical indifference to pain or pleasure, but rather to that perfect poise of His glorious attributes which makes His Master of His situation as Sovereign on the whole Universe.

Have we admired the coolness and calm self-possession of a great general, such as Grant or Napoleon, in the midst of the confusion and smoke of battle? or in the great statesman, such as Gladstone or the other, and the most of the national people—from the national people—from the political times and places? These are only faint illustrations of the peace of self-possession and self-confidence which rules in the mind of God. He is never confused, bewildered, perplexed, anxious or unleaven, nor in the least fearful that His plans will miss the hope of His return, but, because all power and wisdom in them in Him.

The scope of His mighty intellect reaches to the utmost bounds of possibility, comprehends all causes and discredit with precision all efforts; consequently, He knows the end from the beginning, and that, not only from philosophical principles, but also by isolation. As the Creator of all things and the originality of all law, He is thoroughly acquainted with all the idealest ministries of physical, moral and intellectual law, so that no problem could arise the results of which are not manifest in His mind. "God is light, and in Him is no darkness at all."—1 John 1:8.

God, the Creator of all things, is also the competent Sutrather of all things. In silent predator, from Age to Age, He whose physical Universe fulfils His will without a suspicion of disorder or misuse; and the same Power is pledged to its sustenance throughout the eternal future.

These from His own vast, inherent resources of Power and Wisdom springs the peace of God. But not from this source alone in the Divine peace; for peace is the common concomitant of the perfect goodness. God is the impersonation of the virtue and every grace; and consequently He has the blessed satisfaction and peace of conscious moral perfection as well as inherent Wisdom and Power.

## God's Emotional Nature.

Yet we find this peace of God co-existeth with much of disorder and trouble. As a Father He shows us that He bears a father's love to all His intelligent creatures—"the whole family \[of God] in Heaven and in earth"—and that for His "pleasure they are and were created." (Ephesians 3:15; Revelation 6:11.) He created that in His own likeness—with the same mental and moral attributes, so that He might have consummion and fellion, with them as sons, and they with Him as a Father, that then, in mutual fellowship and communion, the Creator and the creature might find pleasure, happiness and delight.

This likeness of God includes in all not only the same mental faculties, but also the free exercise of the spiritual foundation of character and the creation of character. The creation of character would not be in God's likeness. And for the purpose of developing character, the alternative of good and evil must be placed before him. The right and the wrong principles of action must be discerned and the individual left free to his own choice in the matter, that the pleasure of God may be realized in the virtuous character re-election from the free choice of righteousness.

Since the love of God for His newly created and innocent creatures is akin to, but much stronger than, the love of an earthly parent for an innocent infant; and since that turning interest and solicitude does not grow cold as the creature advances in years, the earnestly watcher of the devotional life of the principal and fruits of righteousness, it is manifest that, like an earthly parent, God experiences the sense of either pleasure or pain, according as His free, intelligent creations, choose the right course or the wrong. Of this we are fully assured, not only to thus reasoning from the fact of His Fatherhood, but also by all of these Scriptures which speak of some things as absorbable, displeasing, hateful and despicable to Him and as giving Him no pleasure, which say that His anger burns against them, and that His indignation and wrath was hot, even to their destruction. Other Scriptures speak of His pleasure, love, joy and delight in pleasing things—in the principles of righteousness and those who obey them. The appreciation of pleasurable emotions necessarily implies ability to appreciate emotions of an opposite character, for pain and pleasure

sure may properly be considered the elix and flow of the same emotion.

The exhibition of the mind of God indicate clearly an emotional nature in the Divine Being, of which fact we might also judge from the realization of our own emotional nature, since man was created in God's image. No, our great God is not a God of stoical indifference, ostensibly to the emotions of pleasure and of pain; but the perfect poise of His attributes preserves the equilibrium of peace under all circumstances, whether or not in pleasure.
