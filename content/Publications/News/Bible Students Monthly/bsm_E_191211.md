# THE FACE OF GOD

"The peace of God which passeth all understanding shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."—PHILIPPHY 4:7.

GOD'S LAW represents God's will. All
who are right minded, rightly dis-
missed, will rejoice in having God's will
well done. Originally, God's will was
written to man's mind, and the people
of the Church of Christ, God wrote it upon
those of stone for Israel. It is the right-
nesses of this Law that Christians obey
(the Divine regulations, the Divine re-
government, whatever they may be) so
far as possible. We delight to do God's
will.

To have God's Law, then, would be to
appreciate the fact that God has a great
purpose: to take delight in finding out
what God's will is; and to have full con-
distance in His Justice, Wisdom, Love and
Power. Great peace have all those who
hold. They do not understand every
dealing of Divine Justice, but their faith
is not only the fact that God is too weak
to cry. Thus they have peace in confiding
their interests to Him.

In this text the Apostle differentiates
between the mind and the heart. The
heart represents the affections. The
Apostle urges not only that we should
have good feelings in the matter, but that
our minds should be at rest. If, after we
have made a connection with the people,
the Lord, we should do something to ve-
ulate our conscience in some respect, we
would feel estranged from Him. Then
our hearts should know that we might
draw more to the Lord again; and we
should endeavor by prayer to get back
into harmony with God and thus to effect
a reconciliation. Our Lord has made pos-
vices on our behalf that we should have
an Advocate with the Father. (2 John
2:1.) He who appeared in the presence
of God for us at first is the same One
who ever lived to make intercession for
us. So we come to the Lord through the
arrangement which He has made; and we
repoke that we must have the forgiveness
and grace to help in time of need.

The text does not refer to our own
peace, but to the peace of God, the peace
which comes to us through a realization
of God's power, of His goodness and wil-
lingness to hold us by His right hand as
His children. This peace stands guard
continually, as a sentinel, to challenge
with battle or worrying thought or fear.
If so know the Christian's mind that he
at heart has peace with the Lord, follow-
ship, communion; and it guards His mind
him, his reasoning faculties, instructing
him and assuring him respecting the liv-
ing power, wisdom and love.
The sound made when increasingly
for grace and wisdom and the truths of
the Spirit, for opportunities for serving
the Lord and the brethren, and for grow-

ing more and more into the likeness of
God's dear Son. Under these conditions
the promised 'peace which passeth all
understanding' will guard our hearts and
minds. We are the sinless and ambitious
would find little room in hearts so filled.
Even when in 'deep water' Divine peace
can dwell in our hearts and keep them.

The Apostle's thought seems to be that
those whom he addresses have come into
harmony with God through acceptance
of His terms. Turning from all appen-
tion, they have become the children of
God through faith, obedience, self-sacrif-
ice and consecration to death. The Apo-
sel urges that God's peace should be in
them and should continue. They should
be guarded by that peace. The expres-
sion, 'through Christ Jesus,' suggests
that, as we entered into this peace
with God's great spirit, so we can
continue in this peace only by His con-
taining to be our Advocate, otherwise,
through imperfections of the face, we
would get out of harmony continually.

'Let us, therefore, come boldly note
the throes of grace, that we may dream
merry and find grace to help in time of
need.' (Heb. 4:14.) Thus as we come
to God say, 'We will be the trespasser
as we forgive those who trespass against
us,' we abide in the peace; for we have
this great Advocate. Therefore, this peace
addresses continuing supply of grace
through the great Advocate.
