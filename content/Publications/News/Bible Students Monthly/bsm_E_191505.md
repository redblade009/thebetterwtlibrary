"The objections which Mr. Ross raises to your letter are but another evidence to my mind, that no person can understand Christian Science properly until his reasoning faculties are forwarded from the distorting influences of its self-decating theory.

"After five years of such application of such principle of Christian Science, as a Practitioner and Reader and current worker, I gladly found myself in this predicament: 'Well, what am I been for?' I have rented this office, have arranged for telephone service, etc., and have set myself apart for—those prepared. For my purpose other than to enable others to tell me they are suffering and that they wish me to return them. But whence come the patients? Is there any sickness in spirit? Is there any pain in perfection? Am I in spirit? Christian Science says that I am. But do I believe that God is all in all? I do. Then am I a consistent practitioner when I encourage myself to believe that there is any sufferer, any one needing my services? Not. Then the time has come for me to be honest with myself, and acknowledge that my so-called patients are themselves the objects of my own false sense, according to the entire theory upon which I have built. Should I ask rather practice upon myself all the basis of Mrs. Eddy's own statement, 'The substance, life, intelligence, truth and love, which constitute Deity, are reflected by His creation; and so (not any patients) shall see this true firmness and relentless everywhere when we understand the false testimony of the unspaced senses to the facts in principle.' (Science and Health, p. 519, edition of 1903.)

"With all due respect for Mrs. Eddy, I indeed must recognize the inconsistency of her attempt, through Science and Health, to hear conciliation to the sorrowing, and healing to the sick, instead of at once proceeding upon the basis of Christian Science herself, to rehabilitate the false testimony of the unspaced senses which deceived her into believing that 'God \[who suffers not a woman to teach in the Church—(Timothy 2:12) called her to proclaim His Gospel to this Age,' whereas God Himself (Principle), according to Christian Science, had no sense of any need of a Christian Science Gospel, or any other.

I thank God for the works of Pastor Russell, the careful precast of which has opened to me the great plan of God, has made me acquainted with my father, and has given me the joy and themsdness of a consistent Christian, an experience which Christian Science can neither give nor take away.
