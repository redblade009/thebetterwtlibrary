RED CROSS SAYS CHURCH GIFTS "PITFULLY SMALL."

# POCKET WIRELESS TELEGRAPHY

Father Alfani, director of the Florence Observatory, thinks the invention of the priest Domenico Argentieri of a system to receive wireless messages without the use of poles or batteries is scientifically sound.

After war was declared the Italian Government forced all private wireless stations, including that of the Florence Observatory, to discontinue. The next day Father Alfani got from Argentieri one of official wireless messages which had been intercepted without a wireless installation.

"There are many ways to do this," Father Alfani explained. "One of the simplest utilizes in very elemental expedient of sticking two steel needles into a potato."—The York World.

# THE CHILDREN'S TEETH ON EDGE

The Buffalo Enquirer asks: "What is the matter with the rising generation? A few weeks ago, following the Rosenthal murder, Assistant District Attorney Frank Masi created a sensation by declaring that New York's most dangerous criminals are 'of the high school full type.' A little later Bird S. Coler expressed the same idea in his book deploring the lack of moral training of the crime.

"Now comes District Attorney Crocker of Kings County, with this declaration: 'Over 23 per cent. of the criminals of Kings County who are convicted on the more serious charges are boys under 20 years of age. A few years ago the percentage reached only 20."

"Recently, in Buffalo, two astonishing cases appeared. In one case three young boys of well-to-do parents were found to have been long escaped in systematic burglary. In the other case three boys of well-to-do parents were nailed as they were beginning a similar course of crime. None of these boys was in need. All of them had good homes. All had been reared in better than ordinary environment.

"Something, plainly, is the matter with the rising generation. What is the explanation? Is there any better answer than that, with 'liberality' merging into laxity, fundamental morality is not ground into youthful minds as in more rigid days?"

# Why God Permits Sin and Death.

Only as we look at the Divine Plan as a whole can we understand God's relationship with this bloody war—and with all war. To the majority it is confusing to think that God would bring about any war. There is surely something wrong or there would not be such perplexity, for the Lord tells His people concerning this time of trouble that they "need not be in darkness that that day should overtake them unawares." God's people should have general information regarding His plans and arrangements.

Many have seen only small portions of the Divine Plan, and so have failed to grasp the great margin, benefits, heights and depths of God's great purpose. But whoever has an understanding of the great Plan of God will ever after be able rightly to appreciate and to locate not only the events of today, but also everything that is coming; for the Bible outlines the history of man.

This perfect man was placed in a perfect home, in every way adopted to his needs. This Eden was especially prepared for him and he was to enjoy it. His mankind remained in harmony with God, the whole world would have become as Eden.

**YET SOME CLAIM THEY WERE ALMOST SMOKEED.**

That the old Egyptians did not spend all their spare time building pyramids and wrapping up mountains is borne only the excavations on the site of the ancient city of Antinope made by Paul Thomas A. Whittmore of Tufts College. A portion of his collection, just arrived, contains a weeping forest, a rug on the baby's neck and over a hundred pairs of shoes, socks, boots, sandals and other forms of footwear which show an almost incredible resemblance to present day styles.

In the group is the first and oldest boot with eprints ever discovered in Egypt. A party slipper is made of a fine grade of leather —meated with gold, red and black thread. It has a very elaborated carved piece of leather on the instop. The archbishop broach which was discovered had a wooden base and legules on both sides.

Several dentist instruments have been described. Some are similar in shape to modern instruments.

Some druggists' labours are of equal interest. Lamps were found with the white still in them, and near them were implements used in keeping them in condition.

# Fear Is a Cause of Great Nervous Tension

"The Lord is My Helper, and I will not fear what man shall do with me."—Hebrews 12: 6.

Fear is one of the greatest evils in the world, causing much nervous tension and distress. It is a result of man's being out of harmony with the Creator. If any be sure that the angels have no fear, God maintains them in their presence, condition because they are in harmony with Him. We may be sure, also, that this was the case with our first parents. When they were created they had no fear. Divine protection was over them. But when sin came in the curse followed. Being for sin thousand years under the reign of Rin and Death, man has become very fearful. He realizes that there are forces outside of himself with which he cannot cope. And the knowledge robs him of man's of the pleasure of life.

There are a few people who have lost this fear, but these are the exceptions. Our text tells us of some who do not fear, and shows us why this is a reasonable condition. They are longer fear what man can do unto them, because the Lord is their Helper. The fear of man is a greater fear than the fear of boards. As the poet wrote,

"Men's ichunessity to man
Makes countless thousands mourn."
On account of sin man's have become murderers. Though they are restrained by the law from committing the overt act, they have the murder spirit, the selfish spirit. This lends to all kinds of wrong-doing—margelessness. Those who will not actually take life, may commit murder by the use of slanderous words. Thus they inspire fear in the minds of others.

The Source of Relief.
The Psalmist speaks of some who had gotten rid of this fear of man—of such were Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and the Prophets. And what was true of them should be true of the consecrated Body of Christ. The reason for this peace of mind is that these have come into special

relationship with God. By the Covenant of sacrifice, made possible to them by the Savior, they have become sons of God; God if children—then have theirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ," their Lord. God thus being their Father has a deep interest in them; and so they learn to trust Him.

With this confidence in God, then, these have no fear. He has assured them that He is able to make all things with together for their good. If at first they found that as followers of Jesus who had more trials, more difficulties from the world, the flesh and the Adversary than any other people in the world will realizes that they have filled them with alarm and intimidated them. But as they have gone to the Father in the spirit of prayer and he has been the message which He has sent through the Prophets and Apostles, they have learned that He is dealing with them as New Creatures. Grainingly they learn that "no good thing will He withhold from them that walk uprightly."

The Father will not withhold anything necessary for the proper development of His children. He also promises that we shall not be trapped above that we are able to endure, but some injury come to us. Thus we see that He is supplying all our needs, enabling us to lay down our earthly lives. And as we learn the proper lessons from our experiences, we find that perfect love casts out all servile fear of our Heavenly Father; for we love and trust Him. We have no fear of the Adversary, because we know that the Lord will not permit the Adversary to injure us, if we keep close to Him. And if God permits us to have experiences of trial from the Adversary and our fellowmen, He does so to the intent that these may work will good to us.

# Significance of Ordination

Of Christian Ministers

# PASTOR RUSSELL'S REPLY TO CRITICS

BULIEVISH the following letter from the President of a friend in British West Indies would be of interest to our readers, who may have come to contact with some of the alanders offered by opponents, we have asked permission to reproduce it in these columns. This letter was published in the newspapers of Trinidad, as follows:
Mr. E. J. CORMAN.

"Port-of-Spain, Trinidad, B. W. I."
"Dear Brother in Christ: You're of October 10, is before me. Thanks for its clippings from the Gazette and the Evangelical Christian."

"I am quite familiar with the alandersons served issued by Rev. J. J. Ross. In Canada they have just two laws governing libel. Under the one the falsifier may be punished by the assessment of damages and money. Under the other, criminal libel, be it subject to imprisonment. I entered suit against Rev. Ross under the criminal suit, at the advice of my attorneys, because, as he has no property, he must not damage would be the absolute him nor stop him. The lower Court found him guilty of libel. But when the case went to the second Judge he called up an English precedent, in which it was held that criminal libel would operate in a case only where the jury did not state that there was danger of rioting or violence. As there was no danger that either I or my friends would resort to rioting, the case was thrown out. I could not bring my action for financial damages, but it would be costly to me and impotent as respects Rev. Ross. He, however, is having troubles of his own. The Bible authorizes a defense of the Truth only. We are not to unite the brethren. Whoever undertakes an attack upon any Christian minister's reputation violates the Master's commands, and puts himself on the side of the Advocacy. A lying spirit is sure to be a boomerang. It is a respects my education to Greek and Hebrew. Not only do I not claim very special knowledge of other language, but I claim that not one minister in a command in either a Hebrew and Greek school. To be able to spell out a few Greek words is of no earthly value. Nor do it necessary longer to study these languages, in order to have knowledge of the Bible. Our Presbyterian friends have gotten out at great cost Young's Analytical Hebrew, Chisholm, Greek and English Lexicon Concordance, which anyone may prefer. And our Bishop's friends have issued a similar work—Strong's Analytical Concordance and Lexicon. And there is a still older one entitled Englishman's Hebrew, Chisholm, Greek and English Lexicon and Concordance. Additionally, Liddell and Scott's Greek Lexicon is a standard authority. The prices of those are not beyond the reach of the Average man. By these works scholarly information respecting the original text of the Bible is obtainable. I have all four of these works and have used them faithfully. Very few college professors, even, would risk to give a critical translation of any text of Scripture without consenting these very works of reference, which are standard. To merely learn to read the Greek and Hebrew without a six years' course in their grammars is more likely to hinder than to help in Bible study; for better take the acknowledged scholarship to which I have referred.

"Additionally I remind you of the many translations of the Bible now excuses—all of them very good. I have all of these and find them useful in comparison in the study of any text—one sometimes giving a thought which another may not. The other day, for curiosity's sake, I counted Bibles in different translations, etc., in my study and found that I have thirty-two."
"As respects my business dealings, I need not remind you that Neaphan Courts are very strict, and that if anybody feels that I have wronged him out of a dollar, he would have no difficulty in halting me into Court. You have my assurance, dear Brother, that I do not owe any man on earth a penny, and that I have never taken a penny from any one unjustly. On the contrary, as you know, I have spent several small fortunes in seeking to do good to my fellowmen—in helping them to a better understanding of God and the Bible. Having ever met the Bibles in it, and having less frequently found that I had confused the teachings of the Bible with the teachings of the creeds, and that the Bible's teaching is a dishonor, grand doctrine, it has since been my business and pleasure to do all in my power to help fellow-mortals out of darkness into the true light."

"I need not tell you how absurdly untrue Rev. Ross' statements are in respect to my ordination; but really it seems strange how little people use their thinking faculties in such matters—how few who would read the Rev. Ross' state-

ments would use their absurdity. For instance, for a Baptist and was authorized or ordained by the Baptists—not by Methodists. Presbyterians, Lutherans, Catholics or Episcopalians. Would an Episcopalian recognize Rev. Ross' ordination? Surely not! Would a Roman Catholic recognize his ordination? Of course not. Ordination merely means authentication. The Catholics will authorize, or ordain, those only who belong to their faith. The Baptists will ordain, or authorize, those only who are Baptists. How foolish, then, to talk about ordination from their standpoint!"
But ordination from my standpoint, the Bible standpoint, the standpoint of an increasing number of Bible students all the world over, is different. It is a Divine ordination. But our Baptist friends and our Methodist friends would say that they, also, recognize Bible ordination, that they are not merely dependent upon each other. But we challenge them to prove that they ever had a divine ordination. But not only do they know of it. They merely think of a sectarian ordination, or authorization, such from his own sect or party.

True, Catholics and Episcopalians are different and do recognize a Divine ordination. They claim that Jesus ordained His Twelve Apostles and that these have successors in the Bishops, and are styled "apostolic bishops," and under the theory of "apostolic succession" have the same power to a the original Twelve Apostles to certain and to teach. Bible students believe that they cry in this claim and that the doctrine of "apostolic succession" is unscriptural. The Bible recognizes only Twelve Apostles. More than that, the Bible denounces all apostolic bishops as being in error. Referring to them, Jesus said that they claimed to be apostles, and are not, but do lie. (Revelation 2:2.) In other words, contrary to the superstitions of Catholics and Episcopalians, their bishops have no authority whatever to ordain anything. What, then, is the proper ordination of a minister of Christ, and how can it be obtained, according to the Bible?

We answer that God's ordination, or authorization, of any man to preach is by the impartation of the Holy Spirit to him. Whoever has received the Holy Spirit has received the power and authority to teach and to preach in the name of God. Whoever has not received the Holy Spirit has no Divine authority or sanction to his preaching. In other words, he is ascertained in the highest truest sense of that word.

What is the secret of the opposition and slander that is being raised up against me and against all who, like me, are Bible students? It is malice, hatred, envy, strife, on the part of those who are still hugging the nonsense of the Dark Ages and neglecting true Bible study. They see that their influence is waning. But they have not yet awakened to the true situation. They think that I am responsible for their smaller congregations and small collections. But not so. The real difficulty with them is that the people are becoming more intelligent and can no longer be driven with the crack of a merely man-devised whip of fear. The children of the world have been teaching that the Bible is a foolish old book, until few preachers and few of the educated of the world believe it to be of Divine inspiration. Losing faith in the Bible, in the preachers and in the creeds, the people are drifting toward atheists. That is the real difficulty.

While my work does not, indeed, help to build up any of the sorts of Christendom, it is helping to establish Christian people in a true faith in God and in the Bible. It is giving them a firm foundation and an intelligent understanding such as they had prayed for and hoped for before, but never had to do. This is not because of great ability on my part, nor on the part of my associates, but because God's time has come for blessing Bible study in the light of prevent-day opportunities. It is as Jesus promised—the Wise Virgin class of Christian people, who 'trips their lamps'—study the Bible—find it to shine our brightly and to point them to the new Age of blessing under Messiah's Kingdom.

"If you choose, you may make such extreme of this as are likely to reach intelligent people through all of the world papers. But really I love little for what men may say or think about me. Of course, such things are painful; but they are only what the Bible tells us will be more or less the experience of all who would be loyal to God and His Word. So persecuted they the saints and prophets of the past—even the Master Himself. And as for how I got my education—it seems to me of little consequence. I have enough to serve my own purposes, and, apparently, too much to please Rev. Ross and others of his type,

who, not knowing how to meet my own logical teachings, do not attempt to do so at all, but merely charge me with ignorance. As I read his wit, slanders I thought of what the New Testament says about St. Peter and St. John. They were so woefully ignorant that at the people perceived that they were 'ignorant and

unlearned men.' If they were living today, I suppose that the Rev. Ross and Co. would be after them to show them up as not having been ordained by the Baptists and not knowing anything anyway.

"Very truly your servant in the Lord,
"G. T. RUSSELL."

# PRAYERS FOR KINGS

AND GOVERNMENTS

"I rebut, therefore, that, first of all, supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men; for kings, and for all that are in authority; that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all Godliness and honesty."—2 Tim. 5:1, 2.

THE Scriptures can be clearly that the kingdom of this world are not the kingdoms of our Lord. (Luke 19:11, 12.) They inform us in an indirect way that the world cannot realize that the affairs of the present order of things are all under the supervision and care of Satan (Matthew 4:8, 9; John 14:39) that the Lord will not set up His Kingdom of righteousness and His appointed time. When that time shall come, all kings and priests and peoples shall serve and obey Him. (Daniel 7:27.) His reign will be the one that will be the theatre of all nations.

But in the meantime, the Bible gives us to understand, how steward kingdoms are given the opportunity of seeing what they can do under the conditions. (Daniel 2:37-44.) When the typical kingdom of Israel was destroyed and the kingdom was given to Nebuchadnezzar it was for the opportunity of seeing what his kingdom could do. It might be right soon or unrighteous.

And so it has been from the kingdom of Nebuchadnezzar down. They are all Gentile kingdoms, and use representatives of God. All these various kingdoms are demonstrating various principles of government. Mankind seems those kingdoms are learning lessons of experience which will be valuable to them in the future. Under these kingdoms we have seen men battling for rights. Sometimes they have been defeated, and sometimes viciousness—as the ones may have been. In the various battlings we have seen righteousness and unrighteousness striving together; but with all, godly raise.

Under all the kingdoms mankind has demonstrated that no government by impetition may can rectify the difficulties of the confines humanity, and the abolish sin and sorrow, crying and dying. Had only one king or one nation been permitted to experiment with the race, we would not have known whether or not other nations might not have been successful, but they had the opportunity. Each nation in turn seeks to be the universal power, and each citizen to be the better government; as, for instance, the American Government wants to give better government to the people of India, and Great Britain wants to give better government to the people of India and South Africa. What do civilized nations and congress do in less civilized lands? What can they do for the betterment of conditions? In all these countries they show that selfishness dominates.

# Sympathy for Those in Authority.

We who belong to the new Kingdom are as citizens in a foreign country. We see that we are more or less influenced by the condition of the things of this world. We have sympathy with mankind. We are glad that a New Dispensation is coming in. We see that those who are striving to make things better have a Herculous task. If they were to give it up to us it would take all of our time; and under present, the nations we could do so better than they are doing so. We are great sympathy for kings and princes. They do well to accomplish so much, with sin be every direction.

For sympathy would lead us to consider them kindly in our minds. And we may pray for them such wisdom as God sees best. It would not be for us to request of God that one of them should be limited, if we were sick. If we had some means of helping, we should use that means and us for the results, we should remember that these lie in the hands of God. We should help in any way we can. We are not to specify, but to pray blessings upon these kingdoms.

We are interested in these kingdoms because we are interested in mankind in general. We wish to live a peaceful and godly life, that we may have that much more opportunity for reading and studying. (1 Timothy 2:1, 2.) We would be glad if there were peace in the earth now, and we do not intend to quarrel. We intend to pray for those robers; for we do not believe that they are at heart vicious or evil-intentioned. Perhaps they are trying to do to the best of their knowledge what would be the best for all. We believe that most of the monarchs of Europe did not really wish to pull the people into war.

# Presents Day Blessings.

As to the people who are keeping such a wonderful government in our own land, we see how they are having persons to watch every building that is being erected; how they give special attention to the fire department and the water department, that they may be a proper supply and purity of water; and how they are the general health of the city, providing for quarantine, etc., etc. Those who have charge of the school systems for the young, and of the hospital systems, are doing a great work.
