It has about seventy missionaries, home and foreign. They traveled over the past and past year. They visited more than 3,000 cities. They held more than 1,000 meetings. # Pastor Russell's Reply to Cardinal Gibbons' Sermon

Cardinal Gibbons' sermon, widely publicized, attracted a great deal of attention amongst Protestants as well as Catholics. What will Protestantism say to this? was the query. We have heard but one Protestant response, and that, properly enough, from the pen of the best and most widely known Protestant, the first answer in the world—Pastor Russell, of Brooklyn Tabernacle, New York. We place the reply immediately following Cardinal Gibbons' presentation, assured that it will, so interesting to our Protestant as well as our Catholic readers.

## Pastor Russell Congratulates the Cardinal

It fails to say lot to respond to Cardinal Gibbons' sermon on "A Free Of United Christendom," in which he urges very forcefully that there is but one Church in Europe, therefore, all Protestants should abandon their sectarian attitude and join the Roman Catholic Church. The reply fails to my lot, because, although I stand free and independent of all Protestant sects and parties, my brethren of the muttings in various denominations of Protestantism would find it rather awkward and difficult to acknowledge that there is but one true Church, and, in the same breath, to acknowledge that their denominations is no more that one Church than is any other sect.

I am very glad with Cardinal Gibbons' kindly moderation in the handling of the subject. It contrasts very favorably with the terrible times of the past, when Roman Catholics on the one hand and various Protestant sects on the other, waged an indirect and inoffensive strife to the death in the name of God and of our Redeemer and of righteousness. Surely what all intelligent people need is to abandon foolish prejudices, hypocrites and superstitions of the past and to come together as true followers of the Lord.

## Agrees With Cardinal Gibbons

I am pleased to say that I agree most heartily with Cardinal Gibbons' presentation in almost every particular. Unquestionably, the sectarianism is wholly one of accord with the teachings of the Scriptures. Assembly St. Paul rebuked the Cornishian brethren because they presented a divided front, saying, 'I am of Paul; I am of Apolice; I am of Peter, etc. Mr. Paul's reply to this was that it was proof of normality, of feastly minds and proof of an inexpiritual state. All Christians are coming to realize this—Catholic and Protestant—although it is but a few years since some claimed that sectarianism was a positive advantage; that it led to a very real and energy in the Divine service than if all were agreed.

All who are conversant with history understand what I mean when I say that God has been pleased to permit an illustration of—that, a united Christendom, and, secondly, a divided Christendom. For long centuries there was practically but the one Church in Europe, the Roman Catholic. The results surely were not all that could have been desired. That unity of the Church brought to us an secular nor religious education, nor did it bring to the world the Catholicism promised as the great desideratum of humanity. Rather in that long period we see this ignorance and superstition held the reins. During the last few centuries we have had the opportunity of God to the division of Christendom into various forms and periods. "While the results are not satisfactory, while the condition is not what God's Word prompts us to desire and expect, this divided condition has certainly tended toward greater freedom of thought, greater liberty from ignorance and superstition."

## The Perplexing Question

Where shall we find; how shall we obtain; by what process shall we accomplish this unity?

We agree with the Cardinal that our Lord speaks of himself in the Scriptures as the one Shephard of the one Rock of this July and the Supervisor of the one fold. We agree that there is but the one Church, for whom the Shephard prefers during his sermons—"that they all may be one in me." We agree also that the Apostle speaks of the one Church, likening it to a human body, over which there is the one Head, and of which all are members. We agree that there are not many routes, but they, not many heads, not One. We agree, also, that there is but the one true Vine of the Father's right-hand planting and that this refers to Christ, the parent stock, and to his true members, the branches. We agree, also, that as there is only one Lord, so that, we agree, also faith, also, and only, that there is but one baptism.

Agreeably with all these Scriptural premises laid down by Cardinal Gibbons, we must, nevertheless, dispute the conclusion, that this one Church which Christ declared he would hold upon the rock of Truth and which would grow to a glorious temple composed of living alone, of which St. Peter was one, is the Roman Church, but as we disagree with our Baptist friends when they tell us that the Baptist Church is the one Church. The unusual spectacle of 4,000 He-
brews enthusiastically applauding a
Gentile preacher, after having listened
to a sermon he addressed to them con-
cerning their own religion, was pre-
sented at the Hippodrome yesterday
afternoon, where Pastor Russell, the
famous head of the Brooklyn Taber-
nacle, conducted a most unusual service.

In his time the venerable pastor has
done many unconventional things. His
religion is bounded by no particular de-
nomination, and encompasses, as he
says, all mankind. His ways of teach-
ing it are his own. But he never did
a more unconventional thing than this
—nor a more successful one.

### Received at First in Silence

In the crowd watch filled the big
showhouse were scores of rabbis and
teachers who had come to speak out
in case the Christian attacked their
religion or sought to win them from
it. They had questions and criticisms
really for him. He was received at
first in a dead silence.

But the Pastor did not speak to con-
vert the Jews. To their unbounded de-
light, he pointed out the good things
of their religion, agreed with them in
their most important beliefs as to their
salvation, and finally, after a warm ad-
vocacy of the plan of the Jews senti-
blebing a nation of their own, brought
about a tumult of applause by leading
a choir in the Zionist anthem: "Hailin's
Our Hope."

A more interesting audience the Hip-
podrome never held, perhaps. From all
parts of the city came serious-minded
Hebrews to hear what it was an alien, a
clumsy, highest, as to say to them at a
service held during their week of feast-
ing, High Toeshore. They were quiet,
well-dressed, thinking men and women.

Among them were many prominent
figures of the Hebrew literary world.
Some of these escorted Pastor Russell
to the Hippodrome in a motor of his
then took places in the auditorium.
The literary men recognized the Pastor
as a writer and investigator of inter-
national fame on the subject of Juda-
ism and Zionism. Some of these pre-
sent were Dr. Jacobs, editor of the
American history, W. J. Burdson, of
the Hebrew Standard; J. Brody, associate
editor of the same; Louis Lipsky,
editor of the Macacabani; A. B. Landau,
of the Wachtel; Leo Wolfehn, presi-
dent of the Federation of Roumanian
Societies; J. Pfeffer, of the Jewish
Weekly; R. Diamond, editor of the Jew-
ish Spirit; S. Goldberg, editor of the
American History; J. Maronpotts, of the
Jewish Big Birds, and Goldman, editor
of H'Yom, the only Jewish daily.

### No Religious Symbols There

No symbol of any religion at all
greeted them when they gazed at the
Hippodrome stage. It was entirely
empty save for a small lectern and
three peace flags hauling from silken
cords above. One was the familiar
white silk banner with the Stars and
Stripes in its centre, together with the
words "Those Among Nations" in let-
ters of gold. Another bore a rainbow
and the word "Pax." The third was a
silken strip bearing miniature repre-
sentations of all the nations flags.

With a powerful, yet charming voice,
that lines the great playhouse, the un-
conventional clergyman made his every
word audible to every bearer. His
tones pleased their ears, his graceful
gestures soon captivated their eyes,
and in a few moments his apparently
thorough knowledge of his subject ap-
pealed to their minds. Though still
about, the 4,000 were "warming up" to
him.

### Reserve and Doubt Vanish

It was not long before all reserve,
and all possible doubt of Pastor Rus-
sell's entire sincerity and friendliness
were worn away. Then the mention of
the name of a great Jewish leader—
who, the speaker declared, had been
raised by God for the cause—brought
a burst of applause.

From that moment on the audience
was his. The score became as enthusi-
astic over him as though he had been a
great rabbi or famous orator of their
own religion. He hailed them as one of
the bravest races of the earth—having
kept their faiths; Joseph the persec-
tions and cruelties of all other people
for thousands of years. And he pre-
dicted that before very long they would
be the greatest of the earth—not more-

ly a people any longer, but a nation. By
a system of deductions based upon the
prophecies of old, the Pastor declared
that the return of the kingdom of the
Jews might occur at so near a period
as the year 1915. Persecution would be
over and peace and universal happi-
ness would triumph.

As he brought his address to a con-
clusion, the Pastor raised his head
again to his choir. This time they
raised the quaint, foreign-sounding
striae of the Zion hymn, "Our Hope,"
one of the masterpieces of the eccentric
East Side Post Imber.

The unprecedented incident of Chris-
lin voices singing the Jewish anthem
same as a tremendous surprise. For a
moment the Hebrew auditors could
scarcely believe their ears. Then, mak-
ing sure it was their own hymn, they
first cheered and clasped with such ar-
ter that the music was drowned out,
and then, with the second verse, joined
in by hundreds.

At the height of the enthusiasm over
the dramatic surprise he prepared,
Pastor Russell walked off the stage and
the meeting ended with the end of the
hymn. He was congratulated by scores
of men and women who had come to
indifferent. If not hostile, frames of
mind, and he made a friend, they all
declared, of everyone who had heard
him.

### What Pastor Russell Said

Pastor Russell, in opening his ser-
mon, read many quotations from the
prophecies of the Bible relating to
Zionism, the first one of which was
Psalm 101:13-18: "Thou shalt arise and
have mercy upon Zion; for the time to
love her, yea, the set time, is come."
David, Solomon and others represented
Jahorsh in the kingdom of Israel and
that upon the throne of the kingdom
of the Lord. Later the kingdom was
taken from Zedekiah, the last of the
line of David to sit upon the throne of
God's typical kingdom. When the do-
mission was taken from him the Gentile
governments were recognized, but not
in the same manner as was Israel.

So long as God acknowledged the na-
tion of Israel as his Kingdom their
kings were his representatives: but
when Zedekiah was rejected it was not
inconsistent on the Lord's part to rec-
splice the Gentile governments as
above suggested. Of King Zedekiah we
read: "O thou profane and wicked
Prince, whose time has come that in-
quity should have an end. Remove the
bladem. Take off the crown. This
shall not be the same. I will overturn,
overturn, overturn it until he come
whose right it is (Mesela), and I will
give it unto him" (Ezekiel 21:26, 27).
It was at this very time that God gave
the lease of earthly power to Neho-
chadneazar and his successors, as is re-
lated in Daniel's prophecy. Nehochad-
neazar dreamed, but disremembered his
vision. Daniel the Prophet, made pris-
inter at an earlier date, was, by Divine
providence, introduced to the king as
the one person in all the world able to
rehearse the King's dream and to give
its interpretation, and his power so to
be declared to have been of the Lord.

### When Messiah Shall Stand Up

The vision was of a stupendous ins-
age. Its head of gold represented
Nehochadneazar's Empire—Babylon. Its
breast and arms of silver represented
the Medo-Persian Empire. Its belly

and thighs of brass represented the
Jerusalem Empire. Its strong legs of iron
represented the Roman Empire, East
and West. Its feet of iron and clay
represented Papal Rome. The iron con-
tinues to represent civil governments,
and the miry clay, making them appear
like stone, represents ecclesiastic-
ties as it is now mixed up with the politics
of the ten kingdoms of Europe. The
young period of time in which these va-
rious Gentile governments would domin-
ate the world would last until Mes-
iah's promised Kingdom. And this
period is symbolically stated to have
been "seven times", that is, seven
years—evidently not literal years, but
symbolical.

At their end the lease of earthly
power of Gentile governments will ter-
minate in the great time of treatise
forcified by Daniel (12:1). Then Mes-
ia shall stand up in the sense of as-
gerning control of earth's affairs and
Gentile governments will cease, for all
stations shall serve and obey Messiah.
And then God's Chosen People, Israel,
will come to the front in the world's af-
fairs, because they will become the rep-
resentatives and instruments amongst
men of Messiah's Kingdom, which will
be spiritual and invisible, as is that of
the Prince of this world, the Prince of
Darkness, whom Messiah will hold or
restrain during the thousand years of
his reign of righteousness and destroy
at the conclusion, when he shall deliver
over the kingdom of earth to the
Father. Mankind will then be perfect,
because all willful sinners will be de-
ployed in the Second Death. Meantime,
Messiah's reign will bless and up-
fit not only Israel, but through Israel
the blessing will extend to every pre-
sion, people, kindred and tongue in full
accord with the Divine promise and
oath in Abraham more than
their centuries ago.

The speaker would not pretend to
say the day or month or year in which
these things would be accomplished—
in which the Gentile lease of earth's
dominion would expire and Messiah's
Kingdom assume control.

He did, however, offer a suggestion:
So far as he could discern, the time for
these stupendous events is very much
closer than many of us had supposed.
The seven times, or years of Gentile
domination, reckoned on the basis suc-
gested in the Scriptures themselves,
though he interpreted a day for a year,
longer time. Seven years in inner time
would represent 2,550 days, and these,
symbolically interpreted, would mean
2,520 years—from the time Nehochad-
neazar, the head of the image, was rec-
spitied, down to the time of the ex-
piration of the lease of Gentile power,
when the stone shall unite the image
in the feet. So far as Pastor Russell
has been able to determine, the year of
Zedekiah's dethronement was old
B. C. Thus calculated the 2,520 years
of Gentile lease of power will expire in
October, 1916.

### Indications That Gentile Times Are Expiring

"About twenty years ago," said the
speaker, "Providence raised up for your
people a great leader, Dr. Heral, whose
name is now a household word with
your race. Dr. Heral's endeavor was
to have all loyal sons of Israel rise
from the dust and aspire to be a na-
tion amongst nations and to provide a

home for the persecuted of their race
in Russia and Eastern Europe. Dr.
Heral struck the popular chord in the
hearts of the people. At first it was
purely political, and the name of Zion
meant little of anything religious; but
probably Dr. Heral and all the leaders
of the counsels of your people began to
see that the religious element of the
movement was the strongest, the most
powerful.

Dr. Heral has been succeeded by a
Nicolau, also evidently a man of great
talent and great patriotism; but Zion-
ism inagitated, but it will not fail, as
many fear. According to my under-
standing of the Hebrew prophets, the
time of "Jacob's trouble" is not yet
ended. Further response of Russia may
be expected and further atrocities in
Roumania and elsewhere. It is sad in-
deed to be obliged to admit that these
tribulations will probably come to you
from professing Christians. How
ashamed I feel of those who thus dis-
honor the name and the teachings of
my Master, I cannot find words to ex-
press!

"They are deluded. They have mis-
understood the Teacher whom they
profess to follow. Their thought is
that God will torment eternally all who
be not people the name of Christ. Con-
trolled by delusion they are serving the
great adversary and dishonoring Jesus.
But as the trials and difficulties of the
patriarch Joseph were God's provi-
dences to lead him on to influence and
power and honor, so will all these ex-
periences and persecutions work bless-
ings for your race and tend to drive
them out of their present satisfaction
and make them long for home—for
Palestine.

"It is my thought that some of your
most earnest and saintly people will go
to Palestine quickly, that the repren-
tion there will be astonishing to the
world. Further, it is my thought that
Jews in every part of the world, in
proportion as they come under the
confidence of God's promises through
the prophets, will go to Palestine sym-
poistimously—by encouraging those who
can strive go upon themselves and by
financial assistance and the establish-
ment there of great enterprises.

"Permit me to suggest that in the
time of trouble, incidental to the trans-
fer of Gentile rule to the power of Mes-
ia, all financial interests will be
isopardized. Many of your race, grow-
ing wealthy will surely take pleasure
in forwarding the work of Zionism, as
soon as they shall realize that it is
God, foretold through the prophets.

"And those of your people of insuffi-
cient faith to use their means in for-
warding the Lord's work on this im-
portant juncture will, before very long,
find themselves in the condition pro-
posed by the Prophet Ezekiel, who
declares (7:19) that in this great day
of trouble They shall cast their silver
in the streets and their gold shall be
removed; their silver and their gold
shall not be able to deliver them to the
day of the wrath of the Lord. The
great Messenger of the Covenant whom
ye delight in (Ezekiel 2:14) will test
and prove you as a people. Those who
worship them of gold and silver, stocks
and bonds will receive severe chastise-
ment at his hand that they may learn
a great lesson before he will grant
them a share of the coming Heavings."
