## “Examining the Scriptures Daily”

### _Foreword_

“Man must live, not on bread alone, but on every utterance coming forth through Jehovah’s mouth.” (Matt. 4:4) How true! Even as we need physical food every day, we daily need to take in spiritual food from God’s Word. Not without good reason was the king of Israel commanded to read God’s law “all the days of his life.”—Deut. 17:19.

Setting a fine example for us in this regard were the Beroeans to whom the apostle Paul preached. Concerning them, we read at Acts 17:11: “Now the latter were more noble-minded than those in Thessalonica, for they received the word with the greatest eagerness of mind, carefully examining the Scriptures _daily_ as to whether these things were so.”

Because they were so noble-minded, they were not unduly critical, antagonistic, or disposed to be skeptical about what they heard. Noting Paul’s earnestness and sincerity, they no doubt respectfully listened with “the greatest eagerness.”

At the same time, those Beroeans were not gullible or credulous, ready to believe anything or everything they heard. Rather, they were ‘carefully examining the Scriptures as to whether these things were so or not.’ They had good reason for doing that, for this was the very first time they had heard the good news about the Messiah. Was it really so or was it too good to be true?

To help us and encourage us to follow the example of the noble-minded Beroeans in considering the Scriptures daily, the Society has, ever since 1907, produced a publication containing a Bible text and comments to be used each day of the year, first by means of the _Daily Manna_ book and since 1927 by means of an annual

---

<sup>**Page 4**</sup>

[Open Page 4 of PDF](https://pdfs-noindex.themindscapeishere.workers.dev/1986_es_E.pdf#page=4)

_Yearbook_. (However, it should be noted that in the _Daily Manna_ book the same texts and comments were used year after year.)

Now we are happy to introduce the booklet _Examining the Scriptures Daily_. It publishes the daily texts and comments separately from the annual _Yearbook_. It is hoped that the texts and comments will thus be available early enough so that everybody will be able to start using them from January 1 of the new year. The booklet format will also prove to be a convenience.

While some families may wish to have the discussion of these texts in the evening when the entire family is together, there is an advantage to starting the day with consideration of a Bible text. We have received letters from parents telling how these morning discussions have prepared the minds of their children to deal with the threats to their spirituality they meet up with in school during the day.

In fact, not long ago a schoolteacher in Mexico asked one of her pupils why she, in a good way, was so different from all the rest of the children. The young girl replied that she was one of Jehovah's Witnesses and that each morning they had a discussion of a Bible text published in the Witnesses' _Yearbook_. From then on, the teacher asked her each day to read to the class the text and comment from the _Yearbook_.

When having these discussions, the father or the mother can draw out their children by simple questions that will cause the children to think and to express themselves. These morning discussions can also serve for teaching, as when a child gives a wrong comment or answers in a superficial or glib manner, betraying a lack of understanding of spiritual things. Yes, the morning discussions can furnish good opportunities for building up our children with fire-resistant materials, to the mutual benefit of all involved.—1 Cor. 3:12-15.

## 1986 Yeartext

### “Go . . . , declare abroad the kingdom of God.”—Luke 9:60.

Jesus had said to one of his listeners: “Be my follower.” But the man had an excuse: “Permit me first to leave and bury my father.” What was Jesus’ reply? “Let the dead bury their dead, but you go away and declare abroad the kingdom of God.” (Luke 9:59, 60) Yes, not only are those lying in their graves spoken of as being dead but so are those living in sin. Thus Paul wrote to the Christians at Colossae: “Furthermore, though you were dead in your trespasses and in the uncircumcised state of your flesh, God made you alive together with him.”—Col. 2:13.

If declaring the good news was important back there, it is even more so now that the Kingdom has been established in the heavens!

What a privilege it is for those of the remnant and of the “great crowd” of “other sheep” to represent Jehovah’s Kingdom! What good news we have to tell the people about the Paradisaic blessings so near at hand! Yes, God’s Kingdom is a real government, not just something in the hearts of believers; nor is it based on mere wishful thinking.

No other religious group on earth has such good news to bring to the people. No wonder that people are becoming ever less interested in religion! This explains why Christendom’s missionaries have such little lasting success. Regarding the situation in Japan, a Baptist missionary said: “A Japanese Christian has to give his allegiance to a ‘foreign’ god and say that he is not first and foremost a Japanese—an impossible task.” (_Time_, August 1, 1983) But the missionaries of Jehovah’s Witnesses have not found it impossible to help formerly nationalistic Japanese people embrace the worship of the

Those who preach “this good news of the kingdom” find that it is possible to motivate Japanese truth seekers to put on “the new personality” and become advocates of Jehovah’s Kingdom by Christ Jesus.—Matt. 24:14; Eph. 4:24.

Tuesday, December 30

Brothers, even though a man takes some false step before he is aware of it, you who have spiritual qualifications try to readjust such a man in a spirit of mildness.—Gal. 6:1.

To readjust a child's erroneous thinking requires that a parent intensify his training efforts. The parent may need to make adjustments in his lifestyle to give the necessary attention. A child trained in godliness can become overwhelmed with feelings of worthlessness and guilt when his sin comes to light, so a parent may need to confirm his love for the child. Help the child to see that it is not himself but his conduct that is disliked, and that this conduct can be corrected. (Jude 23) True, at times a son or a daughter may become totally rebellious, even 'despising obedience.' (Prov. 30:17) How encouraging to know that, in the vast majority of situations, when the parents patiently try to reach their child, the results are good! w 8/15/85 15, 16, 18b

Wednesday, December 31

There exists the one speaking thoughtlessly as with the stabs of a sword, but the tongue of the wise ones is a healing.

—Prov. 12:18.

Use your Bible-trained ears to "test out words," and do not be influenced by those that do not ring true. (Job 12:11) Remember that Paul said: "If anyone contends even in the games, he is not crowned unless he has contended according to the rules." (2 Tim. 2:5) Yes, God's "rules"—not unscriptural opinions—must guide your thinking. (Compare 1 Corinthians 4: 3, 4.) True, at times a fellow Christian may 'stab' you with some thoughtless word. Do not let this make you quit the race for life! Remember Joseph. His own brothers considered murdering him, and though restrained from doing so, they finally sold him into cruel slavery. Joseph, however, did not allow this to embitter him or 'enrage him against Jehovah.' —Prov. 19:3. w 7/15/85 18, 19a
