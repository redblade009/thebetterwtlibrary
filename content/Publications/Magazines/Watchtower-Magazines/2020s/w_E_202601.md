# Continue to Satisfy

Your "Spiritual Need"

OUR YEARTEXT FOR 2026:

"Happy are those conscious of their spiritual need."

—MATT. 5:3.

JEHOVAH has created humans with certain basic needs. For example, to sustain our life, we need food, clothing, and shelter. If we lack any one of these necessities, even for a brief time, life becomes a trial. In addition to creating us with a need for those material things, Jehovah has also created us with a spiritual need. (Read Matthew 5:3.) If we want to be truly happy, we have to be aware of that need and then continue to satisfy it.

2 What does it mean to be "conscious" of our "spiritual need"? The original-language expression conveys the idea of being a beggar for the spirit. That phrase creates a powerful mental picture. Imagine a scraggly figure clothed in rags, crouching on the corner of a city street. He is weak from a lack of food, shunned because of his appearance, and exposed to the searing heat of day and to the bitter cold of night. The beggar is keenly aware that he will need help if he is to improve his situation in life. Similarly, someone who is conscious of his spiritual need—a beggar for the spirit—will need help to improve his situation. So he is eager to take advantage of the abundant spiritual provisions that Jehovah supplies to those who love Him.

_“Miserable man that I am!”_—ROM. 7:24.

$^{2}$ **Read Romans 7:21-24.** In his letter to the Romans, Paul expressed feelings that many of us can relate to. In addition to this inner conflict, Paul at times struggled with negative feelings about his past and about a persistent problem he was facing.

## Why We Need the Ransom

_“Who will rescue me from the body undergoing this death?”_

—ROM. 7:24.

IMAGINE the scene. A building collapses, trapping a man in the rubble. He is alive, but he cannot remove himself from the wreckage. All he can do is cry out for help and hope that he can be rescued.

2 Each one of us is in a comparable situation. In what way? When Adam rebelled against his Creator, he became a sinner. Later, he passed on his sinful condition to his children. As a result, the entire human race became trapped in the rubble of inherited sin, and we cannot remove ourselves from its effects. The apostle Paul well described our sinful condition in his letter to the Romans. (**Read Romans 7:22-24.**) He begged to be rescued “from the body undergoing this death.” Paul was trapped by inherited sin, which unavoidably leads to death. STUDY TIP

Visualize Bible Accounts

The Bible contains many historical narratives that teach important lessons. By creating mental images of these accounts, we can better understand what we read and benefit more from it. Consider some tips that can help you visualize Bible accounts.

- Read aloud. Reading the Bible aloud can help you imagine the scenes more clearly. When reading the Bible as a family, you can make the account come alive by assigning each person a different character to read.
- Picture the scenes. Try to capture the thoughts and the feelings of the characters involved. For instance, ask yourself: ‘Why did the person speak or act as he did? What would I have been thinking or feeling if I had been in that situation?’
- Draw or sketch. Sketching Bible scenes can help you visualize and remember the narratives better, even if your finished product is not a work of art.
