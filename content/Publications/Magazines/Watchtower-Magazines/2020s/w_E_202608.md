## What activities took place at city gates in Bible times?

CITY gates were more than just entry points. With so many people passing through them, they became busy centers of daily life. The Bible mentions city gates over 50 times, and it describes the activities that took place there.

**People gathered at city gates.** The Hebrew word for “gate” refers to more than just the front door of a city. It conveys the idea of a built-up area at the entrance of a city or a town. In some places, there were broad public squares inside the gates where large groups of people could meet.—Neh. 8:1.

**Merchants set up markets near city gates.** Some gates in Jerusalem, such as the Fish Gate and the Sheep Gate, may have taken their names from the goods that were sold nearby.—Zeph. 1:10; John 5:2.

**People cared for both business and personal matters at city gates.** Abraham and Boaz made official transactions in front of witnesses at city gates. (Gen. 23:10-15, 17, 18; Ruth 4:1, 10) Some travelers would spend the night in the public square near the city gates if no one offered them hospitality. (Judg. 19:15-21) People also went to the city gates to hear the latest news.—2 Sam. 18:24, 25.

**Judges held court near city gates.** They decided on cases before onlookers who could hear both the testimony and the outcome. (Deut. 21:18-20) This public setting encouraged the judges to be just and fair in their decisions. After the judges made a decision, officials carried out the sentence right away. (Deut. 25:2) At the city gates,

**Faithful men encouraged the people of Israel who were at the city gates to trust in Jehovah.** Prophets and others spoke at the gates because they knew that people gathered there. (1 Ki. 22:10; Jer. 17:19, 20) For instance, Jehu declared before all the people that Jehovah had fulfilled His word against the house of Ahab. BIBLE FACT

The Tree That Wakes Up Early

The almond tree is one of some 30 types
of trees mentioned in the Bible. It is among
the earliest trees to blossom following winter.
Its lovely pink or white flowers can be seen
as early as late January. Fittingly, the Hebrew
name for the almond tree literally means
“awakening one.” Is there a similarity be-
tween that tree and its Creator?

The footnote to Jeremiah 7:25 shows that
Jehovah describes himself as rising early.
The March 15, 2011, issue of The Watchtower
states: “Just as the almond tree ‘awakened’
early, so Jehovah was figuratively ‘getting up
early’ . . . to warn his people about the conse-
quences of disobedience. . . . And he would
not rest—he would ‘keep awake’—until his
prophetic word was accomplished.”—Jer. 1:
11, 12.

The white blossoms of the almond tree are
also used in the Bible to illustrate the white-
headedness of old age. (Eccl. 12:5) And likely
because of their delicate beauty, the tree’s
flowers served as a pattern for the cups on
the branches of the tabernacle lampstand.
—Ex. 37:17-20.
