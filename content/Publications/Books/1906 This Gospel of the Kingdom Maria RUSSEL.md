# This Gospel of the Kingdom.

'And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world _for a witness unto all nations_, and _then_ shall the end come.' Matt. 24:14.

THESE are the words of our Lord Jesus, and should be read, not with drowsy listlessness, but with interest alert, for indeed they are freighted with weightiest import to the people of _to-day_. They point to an ideal kingdom over the whole earth which is yet future, but no longer in the distant future; for the testimony of this gospel has now been borne to every nation. These words were spoken in reply to the inquiry of his disciples, 'Tell us what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?' The statement just noted, and now an accomplished fact, is the _sign_ that Jesus gave.

### A WITNESS TO ALL NATIONS.

The church of Christ was to go into all the world and preach this gospel of the kingdom in all the world _for a witness unto all nations_, and _then the end of this order of things would come_, the promised kingdom of God would be established, and the kingdoms of this world become the kingdoms of our Lord and of his Christ. Rev. 11:15.

But, it is asked, of what value is this witness to all nations, if it does not accomplish their conversion to God?

This long epoch of human history is divisible into seven periods of one thousand years each and the Lord counts each one of these thousand-year periods as one day in the outworking of his plan of salvation, as Peter, in this connection, says, “Beloved, be not ignorant of _this one thing_, that _one day_ is with the Lord as a _thousand years_, and a _thousand years as one day_.” 2 Pet. 3:8. Thus these seven periods constitute a great week, each of whose seven days is one thousand years. The last one, corresponding to the Sabbath day, is the period of Christ’s reign upon earth.

During the first six days (six thousand years) the race has been increasing, multiplying, to fill the earth, but as it multiplied, generation after generation has been laid in the grave. Because of sin, it has indeed been a mournful procession from the cradle to the tomb, and so it must continue until Christ’s kingdom is established in the earth in the dawn of this millennial Sabbath day.

## THE BINDING AND LOOSING OF SATAN.

In the beginning of this millennial day, we are further informed, Satan is to be bound for a thousand years, that the world, being thus relieved from his deceptions and snares, may the more rapidly progress toward perfection of character: 'And he laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent which is the devil and Satan, and bound him a thousand years, . . . that he should deceive the nations no more till the thousand years should be fulfilled. And _after that_, he must be loosed a little season.' Rev. 20: 1–3.

As soon as Satan is thus loosed we see that the entire work of the millennial day is put to the test. Under the favorable conditions of Christ's reign of righteousness—its wholesome measures of discipline, correction, instruction and training, unhampered by any of the wiles of Satan—humanity will make rapid strides of progress; and at the end of that day God will require of every man an established righteous character. ## THE END OF THE SEVENTH DAY.

Mankind being thus established in everlasting life and in full possession of the restored earthly inheritance at the end of this millennial day, “then cometh the end \[the end of Christ’s special reign on earth, the end of the great plan of salvation, issuing in the glorious victory of truth and righteousness, and in the eternal salvation of all the righteous], when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God, even the Father; when he shall have put down all rule and all authority and power. Now let us observe further that the six preceding days (six thousand years) have all been days of preparation for this last day’s work, and that no less time could have accomplished that necessary preparation.

## THE SIX DAYS' WORK.

During these six days the race has been increasing toward the filling of the earth, and men have been getting acquainted with the value of this inheritance. They have spread themselves abroad over its surface; they have noted the grandeur of its mountain heights, the beauty of its smiling valleys, the breadth of its seas, the course of its rivers; they have sought out its treasures of mine and of ocean; they have harnessed its forces of earth and of air; they have basked in its sunlight and tasted its fruits; and they have said, How glorious this inheritance, if only the curse of sin were removed and we could possess it forever!

While the race has thus been increasing and getting acquainted with the inheritance, which, though forfeited, has been redeemed, and shall be restored to them when they also are prepared to receive it, what has God been doing?

During these six days (six thousand years) God's overruling providence has been shaping human experiences for the accomplishment of his own wise ends. Though men have very generally chosen to forget God and to ignore his Word, and have pursued their own course regardless of his will, God, like a wise father, has been letting them do it to its bitter end, and allowing them to try

the beloved John to this good news of the glorious kingdom _at hand_—to the blessed assurance of Jesus, “Surely I come _quickly_”—“Amen. Even so, come, Lord, Jesus.”

“When ye hear I am come, then can ye arise,\
The joy of your heart springing up in your eyes?\
Can ye come out to meet me, whate'er the cost be,\
Though ye come on the waves of a storm-crested sea?”
