# Why is Evil Permitted?

THE earth is full of sorrow; and every one has more or less human ailments, pains and weaknesses. Crime is prevalent in every land; hatred, ill will and malice are manifested without limit; and the question frequently comes to the mind of the one who is asked to consider the divine arrangement: Why should a just and wise and loving God permit evil to be in the world and afflict the people so long?

Evil is that which produces unhappiness; and anything which either directly or indirectly causes suffering may be classed as evil. The question is asked: Why did God permit Satan to tempt our first parents and cause them to violate his law? Could he not have prevented all this trouble, the fall of man, and the thousands of years of suffering that have been upon humankind? The statement that Jehovah is all-powerful at once leads to the conclusion that he could have prevented it had his wisdom dictated that he should do so. The fact that he did not do so is sufficient proof that he permitted it for some wise and good purpose. When we see and understand the outworking of his great plan, his wisdom is manifest in all that he has permitted, and his love is ever manifest in the method provided for the restoration and blessing of mankind.

## End of Evil after Earth is Populated

The permission of the reign of sin and death these many years gave sufficient time for enough people to be born into the earth ultimately to fill the earth when they are restored to perfect human condition and afforded ample time and opportunity for the growing up ones to acquire an experience with the baneful effects of sin. All those who have died in infancy will get their experience during the thousand-year reign of Christ, during which they will be uplifted. They will acquire knowledge by learning what happened to others and by seeing the effect upon others in addition to their own experience. The death of Jesus was for the benefit of all. We read: "We see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honor; that he by the grace of God should taste death for every man." (Hebrews 2:9) Thus we observe it was the purpose of God that the death of Jesus and his resurrection should provide a price sufficient to redeem the entire human family—Adam and all of his offspring. This is in harmony with St. Paul's statement: "This is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Savior; who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth. For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time." (1 Timothy 2:3-6) The order here named is that first mankind shall be saved from the Adamic curse by the death of Jesus and the presentation of the merit of his sacrifice to Divine Justice, and that subsequently all mankind must be brought to a knowledge of the truth and given one fair and impartial trial for life.

# Who Made the Devil?

_'Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honor and power; for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created.'_—Revelation 4:11.

SINCE we read in the Bible that all things were created by the Lord and that all of his works are perfect (Deuteronomy 32:4); and since we know that the devil is the evil one, we feel sure that God would not create such a wicked one. Therefore we ask: Who created the devil?

All intelligent creatures must be free moral agents, with privileges of choosing good or evil; otherwise the creature would be merely a machine, compelled to do that for which he was created and nothing else. Since free moral agency leaves it to the creature to choose the right or the wrongful course, it is easy to be seen how a creature may be perfect at the beginning and by choosing an evil course become a very wicked one.
